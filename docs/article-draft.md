# Three days, 86 million tokens, and a negative result

## The question

I set out to write an algebra of drives. The claim to be tested was that a person has a small set of stable dispositions, such as curiosity, fear and desire, that can be measured beforehand or recovered from behaviour, and that a group's outcome is a composition of its members' drives, with a composition rule that depends on group size. The outcome I chose first was activity: how much a group does, and whether it runs away. Holds and splits were to come out of the model as a second layer.

The plan was to adopt the standard dynamics at each scale and add laws of my own: coupled affect equations for dyads, structural balance for triads, consensus for teams, thresholds for crowds. I would test them first on a modelled population built independently of the equations, then on public data. "Drives as primitives" was declared a working hypothesis, to be tested and not assumed.

It ran for three days, from 29 October to 1 November 2026 (docs/write-up.md). The hypothesis failed. This post records how, and what I would do differently.

## Where the tokens went

The programme used about 86 million tokens. About 80 million went on the survey and its validation, and about 1 million on the three experiments that decided the question (docs/write-up.md). The write-up rounds that to one percent of the survey.

The survey was thorough. It produced 118 files, each written by one agent and checked by a second that resolved every DOI at Crossref. The ledger holds 5,817 claims, and the whole-corpus audit resolved 2,125 DOIs (docs/write-up.md). Its framing finding came on the first day and was never overturned: drive dynamics exist for single agents, act algebras exist for groups, and nobody has joined them. That finding is a map of the literature. It does not say whether the join works.

I ran the survey first and the tests last. The brief had asked for the modelled population first.

## The discipline

Every empirical test was pre-registered, and the mechanics were simple. The rule was written into the results file before any number existed. The reporting command refuses to run unless that block is already in the file. The held-out set was scored once. Every deviation is disclosed in the file that carries it.

The synthetic test shows what a disclosure looks like. Its exit rule could not fire: the realised utility of a person is a maximum that includes the always-available idle action, so ten successive steps below the rule's threshold have probability of at most 1.6e-12 (sim/results/synthetic_population_identifiability.md). I worked this through before the run. I repaired it by choosing the threshold once, at +3.5, on a third seed that no later number used (sim/results/synthetic_population_identifiability.md).

Two flaws remain in the record and I name them here. In the Szekely test, the K1 margin was fixed after I had seen the outcome frequencies: 57.5% of group-rounds reached the threshold, and the frequency fell from 0.82 in round 1 to 0.44 in round 28 (sim/results/szekely_composition.md). I had not computed any relation between a drive and an outcome by then. The margin was still set with the aggregate in view. In the Bitcoin test, the raw mean response by received rating, and some responder-fixed-effect contrasts, were looked at before the model forms were coded, so the test was not blind (sim/results/gate_reciprocity_bitcoin.md). Its results are findings on seen data.

## The oracle test

The brief asked for one test above the rest: a modelled population built independently of the equations. I ran it last. The world has three known drives per person, groups of 3, 8 and 25, and behaviour generated by a softmax choice mechanism unlike the equations. Persons choose among acting, idling, approaching and avoiding, and leave the group when their realised utility stays low for ten steps. The inference sees only whether each person acted, the signed tie events, and the exit step. It never sees the drives.

The first question is whether the drives can be recovered. They can, through ties. The correlation of recovered with true drives runs from 0.608 to 0.931 at 60 steps, across all three drives and all three sizes, so criterion C1 was met at every size (sim/results/synthetic_population_identifiability.md). With the tie events withheld and only activity observed, the affiliation and aversion drives fall to correlations between 0.03 and 0.50, and only the activity set point survives, at 0.741, 0.544 and 0.559 at the three sizes (sim/results/synthetic_population_identifiability.md). The channel carries the drives, not the volume of activity.

The second question is whether recovered drives predict held-out group outcomes beyond what each person's own rates predict. They do not. Criterion C2 was not met at any size. At n = 3 the drive model's AUC on exits was 0.025 below the person-rates model's, against a required margin of 0.02 above (sim/results/synthetic_population_identifiability.md). At n = 8 the drive model beat person rates on group-activity RMSE by 0.055 of the person-rates figure, which clears the 0.05 margin, and trailed on exit AUC by 0.009 (sim/results/synthetic_population_identifiability.md). At n = 25 its RMSE on group activity was worse than person rates, with a relative gap of -1.051 against a required +0.05 (sim/results/synthetic_population_identifiability.md).

The oracle, which is given the true drives, did not settle the matter in the drives' favour either. Its RMSE on group activity, relative to predicting the training mean, was 0.995, 0.741 and 0.667 at n = 3, 8 and 25, against 1.057, 0.840 and 0.389 for person rates (sim/results/synthetic_population_identifiability.md). On exit AUC the oracle scored 0.497, 0.751 and 0.728 against person rates at 0.596, 0.653 and 0.457 (sim/results/synthetic_population_identifiability.md). So the oracle beat person rates on both outcomes at one size only, n = 8. At n = 25 the person-rates model predicted group activity far better than the true drives did. The file records why: group features were the mean and minimum of per-person values fed to a linear regression, while group activity is a sum of softmax probabilities, so person rates measure the target directly and the drives have to reach it through a poor map. Part of the C2 failure belongs to the prescribed predictor form. The held-out sets were 30 groups per size and the file computes no intervals (sim/results/synthetic_population_identifiability.md). A check at the close-out asked whether the generator's own memory, a decaying relationship state and a ten-step exit streak, had handed the person-rates model its advantage. With both removed, same seeds and criteria, C2 passed at n = 3 on three exit events and failed at n = 8 and 25 as before, and the oracle still lost to person rates on group activity at n = 25, 0.669 against 0.357 relative to the baseline (sim/results/synthetic_population_identifiability.md). The advantage comes from the composition rule, not from memory.

The real-data counterpart is the Szekely experiment: 286 people with social value orientation measured before play, groups of six reshuffled every round, a threshold public-goods game, and 345 held-out group-rounds (sim/results/szekely_composition.md). The group mean of the measured drive scored an AUC of 0.581 in phase 1 and 0.543 in phase 2 (sim/results/szekely_composition.md). Claim 1's count-above-a-cut form scored 0.556 and 0.548 (sim/results/szekely_composition.md). Each member's past contribution rate scored 0.811 and 0.853, and rates plus the drive scored 0.810 and 0.852 (sim/results/szekely_composition.md). K1 was met in phase 1 and pooled. K2 and K3 were not met in either phase or pooled. The drive was measured by the standard instrument, before play, and it added nothing to last behaviour.

## What reduced to what

Everything that survived was somebody else's, and every term that was mine was refuted or collapsed into a person effect.

At the dyad scale the adopted law was Gottman and Murray's coupled difference equations. I designed the dyad test and never ran it. At the triad scale the law I added was a first-tie sign rule: the sign of a closing tie depends on the sign of the first. On held-out log-loss at the pre-specified split, across Bitcoin OTC, Bitcoin Alpha and wiki-RfA, weak balance scored 0.264, 0.306 and 0.500, and the first-tie rule was never ahead of it (sim/results/triad_rule_comparison.md). Weak balance is Davis's, not mine. A per-person propensity scored 0.244 on Alpha and 0.471 on wiki-RfA, beating every triad rule on both (sim/results/triad_rule_comparison.md). A post hoc reading in the same file puts the Newcomb pattern that had looked like a triadic law down to a receiver effect (sim/results/triad_rule_comparison.md).

My gated reciprocity law with a dead zone failed on the Bitcoin trust networks. The response to a received rating is graded, not flat: +0.85 going from +1 to +2 received, and the graded form beat the step form by 6.5 standard errors on OTC (sim/results/gate_reciprocity_bitcoin.md). A sign gate is real, at -8.36 at the sign boundary (sim/results/gate_reciprocity_bitcoin.md), but that asymmetry belongs to Altafini and Leskovec.

The sign registry fared worse. The gate term added at most +0.010 AUC to person terms on every dataset, while person terms beat the triad types by +0.056 to +0.152 (sim/results/sign_registry_battery.md). The registry's strongest coefficient, FFmm at -0.48 fitted alone on wiki-RfA, fell to +0.01 once person terms entered the model (sim/results/sign_registry_battery.md).

At team scale the adopted law was consensus dynamics with the spectral-radius stability condition, and my activity certificate is a restatement of it. On the Apache Incubator, team size predicted graduation at an AUC of 0.76 (sim/results/apache_activity_survival.md). A member's own history predicted her next-month activity at an AUC of 0.90, and the others' activity share added +0.0007 (sim/results/apache_activity_survival.md). In the pre-registered decider, first-year growth added +0.014 AUC to size, with an interval from -0.033 to +0.063, and missed the criterion of a gain above 0.02 (sim/results/apache_fitted_case.md).

At crowd scale the adopted law was Granovetter's thresholds. Co-movement with the recent majority is real on wiki-RfA, with a logit coefficient of 1.44 against a shuffle null of -0.01 (sim/results/crowd_gate_wikirfa.md). The dependence on the next ten votes is 1.63 (sim/results/crowd_gate_wikirfa.md), so the data cannot tell influence from a shared shock. Claim 1, the one claim I wrote down as mine, is a threshold distribution with a merge sandwich. It is internally consistent, and on measured data it scored no better than the plain mean, as the Szekely numbers above show.

The winning model across every test was the same: a per-person latent state with inertia, plus coupling, plus size. That is a state algebra and not a trait algebra. The only way to keep the word "drive" is to redefine it as a latent state estimated from behaviour, which dissolves the hypothesis into existing state-space models.

## What I would do differently

I would run the oracle test first.

It cost a fraction of the survey. It needs no literature: a generator, a recovery method, a rival, and criteria written down before the run. The three decisive experiments together cost about 1 million tokens against about 80 million for the survey and its validation (docs/write-up.md). The oracle test was one of the three, and the first thing the brief asked for.

Run on the first day, it would have answered the composition question before the survey began. It gives the composition rule the true drives, in a world where the answer is known, and it measures whether the rule beats person rates. The answer in my world was no for the recovered drives at every size (sim/results/synthetic_population_identifiability.md). The same run would have shown that drives are recoverable only through ties, which says what a real dataset has to record before it can test the algebra.

The measured-drive test needed a dataset, and the search for one was a single file of the survey, the last of the 118 (docs/prior-work/118-measured-drive-datasets.md). It found Szekely and nothing with a measured drive and more than one group size.
