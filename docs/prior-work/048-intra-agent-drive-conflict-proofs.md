# Prior work 048: formal models of conflict between drives inside one agent, with proved equilibrium or switching results

Direction: D085

Date: 2026-10-29. Survey for the social-algebra strand. Question: which formal models of conflict between drives inside one agent come with *proved* equilibrium, bifurcation or switching results, as opposed to simulation? Context: Aldred's aim of "an algebra which expresses basic human drives and group drives, which we can compose into equations, which we can then use to start making theorems".

**Method and honesty note.** Read at source this session (PDF downloaded and text-extracted, so equations are transcribed, not paraphrased from a summary): Reverdy & Koditschek, arXiv:1703.01662v2 (model, Definition 1, Theorems 2 and 3, proof of Theorem 2 outlined); Pais et al. 2013 (White Rose copy; model description, bifurcation statements, not the supplementary proofs); Bogacz et al. 2006 (Eqs 14, 18, 21, 22 and the balance statement). Read at abstract level only: Hughes et al. 2018 (arXiv abstract), Rabinovich et al. 2001, the Arizona 2019 seminar abstract, Thompson & Reverdy arXiv:2109.13408 (search summary; the PDF fetch returned compressed bytes). Gul-Pesendorfer and Fudenberg-Levine are tagged [standard account]; their deep-reads are D094 and D095, not here. DOIs checked against Crossref where given. (Validation pass 2026-10-29 also read the Pais et al. main text, the Bogacz et al. abstract and the Thompson & Reverdy arXiv PDF; see the log at the end.)

**Headline: the two seeds do not fit the question.** Hughes et al. 2018 and Helbing, Farkas & Vicsek 2000 are not models of conflict between drives inside one agent with proved results. Hughes is a multi-agent reinforcement-learning paper whose inequity-averse reward is a Fehr-Schmidt-type shaping term, with empirical results on gridworld benchmarks and no theorem; Helbing 2000 is a simulation paper whose single "panic" parameter mixes two forces (file 23 section 3, "none provable"). Neither is the right anchor. The real answer to the question is a small cluster: **the Reverdy-Koditschek motivation dynamics (the only drive-labelled model here with stated theorems), the Seeley/Pais honeybee-derived decision dynamics it borrows, and the Usher-McClelland/Bogacz competing-accumulator reduction**. Everything else with a proof is in economics (axiomatic representation theorems, files 36 and D094-D096), where the proof is about preference representation, not state dynamics.

## 1. Reverdy & Koditschek: a dynamical system for prioritizing and coordinating motivations

**Citation.** Reverdy, P. & Koditschek, D. E., "A dynamical system for prioritizing and coordinating motivations", arXiv:1703.01662v2 [math.DS], 8 Mar 2018. https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.01662 [read]. Journal successor: Reverdy, P., Vasilopoulos, V. & Koditschek, D. E., "Motivation dynamics for autonomous composition of navigation tasks", *IEEE Trans. Robotics* 37(4), 1239-1251, Aug 2021, DOI 10.1109/TRO.2020.3043720 [Crossref confirmed at validation: authors, volume, issue, pages; not read]. The arXiv paper cites the journal paper as "in preparation".

**Their vocabulary.** Task, motivation state, value (urgency), drive (psychology sense: "the animal can be said to have drives which motivate it to perform actions that reduce those drives"), deadlock, undecided motivation, stop signal, limit cycle, Hopf bifurcation, relaxation oscillation.

**Drives/operands [read].** Agent position x in D (a domain in R^d); motivation m in the N-simplex Delta_N = {m in R^(N+1): m_i >= 0, sum m_i = 1}, with m_i the motivation to perform task i and m_U the "undecided" motivation (no task); value v_i in R+ per task, "its current urgency". Each task i has a goal x*_i and a distance function phi_i(x) = (1/2)||x - x*_i||^2 (Eq. 1). The paper explicitly relates v-dynamics to "drive reduction theory", so v_i is the drive level and m the competition state among drives.

**Operators / equations [read].** State (x, m, v), with x' = f_x(x,m), m' = f_m(m,v), v' = f_v(v,x) (Eqs 3-5).
- Navigation (Eq. 6): x' = -Phi(x) m, with Phi(x) = [F_1(x) ... F_N(x) 0], F_i = -grad phi_i. So the action is the m-weighted convex combination of the task vector fields.
- Motivation (Eq. 7). Reverdy-Koditschek say they take it "from Pais et al.'s work [29]" in Section 2.2.2 and "from Seeley et al. [35]" in the introduction; the model originates with Seeley et al. 2012 (a deterministic model built from swarm data) and Pais et al. 2013 analyse a stochastic extension of it, so credit the equation to Seeley et al. 2012 and its analysis to Pais et al. 2013: m_i' = v~_i m_U - m_i ( 1/v~_i - v~_i m_U + sigma_i (1 - m_i - m_U) ), with v~_i = v*_i v_i, v*_i > 0 a gain, sigma_i > 0 the stop-signal (cross-inhibition) strength. Term reading given in the paper: v~_i m_U spontaneous commitment, -m_i / v~_i abandonment, v~_i m_i m_U recruitment, -sigma_i m_i (1 - m_i - m_U) the stop signal from options other than i.
- Value (Eq. 8): v_i' = lambda_i ( phi_i(x) - v_i ), so the urgency of a task rises with distance from its goal and relaxes toward it with timescale 1/lambda.
- Two-task symmetric instance (Eq. 9, N=2, goals (1,0) and (-1,0), sigma_1=sigma_2=sigma, v*_1=v*_2=v*, lambda_1=lambda_2=lambda): x' = -m_1(x - x*_1) - m_2(x - x*_2); m_1' = (v*v_1) m_U - m_1( 1/(v*v_1) - (v*v_1) m_U + sigma m_2 ); m_2' symmetric with m_1; v_i' = lambda(phi_i - v_i). Mean-difference coordinates Delta m = m_1 - m_2, mbar = (m_1+m_2)/2, Delta v = v_1 - v_2, vbar (Eq. 10), with epsilon_v = 1/v*, epsilon_lambda = 1/lambda (Eqs 11-17). The system has S_2 symmetry (task interchange) when the two values are equal, "no inherent bias for either underlying task".
- **Definition 1 (symmetric deadlock equilibrium)**: an equilibrium of Eq. 9 of the form (x_1d, x_2d, m_d, m_d, v_d, v_d); exists if there is a point x_d with phi_1(x_d) = phi_2(x_d).

**Theorems [read, statements; Theorem 2 proof outline read].**
- **Theorem 2.** In the limit epsilon_lambda = 1/lambda -> 0, i.e. lambda -> infinity (values slaved to distances, v = phi(x)), the reduced system (18) has a deadlock equilibrium; **for sigma > 6 the dynamics undergo a Hopf bifurcation resulting in stable periodic solutions** at (deadlock equilibrium, epsilon_v,0), where epsilon_v,0 is the unique solution in [0, 1/2] of 4(2 - sigma) eps^2 - 4(2 - sigma) eps + (6 - sigma) = 0. For sigma = 8, epsilon_v,0 = (3 - sqrt 6)/6, about 0.09175. (Corrected at validation: an earlier version had "lambda -> 0" and "(2 - sigma)^2 eps^2", both artefacts of PDF extraction dropping the epsilon subscript and misplacing the superscript. Check: at sigma = 8 the corrected equation is -24 eps^2 + 24 eps - 2 = 0, whose root in [0, 1/2] is (3 - sqrt 6)/6; the earlier form gives about 0.061, not 0.09175. The roots sum to 1 and their product (6 - sigma)/(4(2 - sigma)) is positive only for sigma > 6, which is where the sigma > 6 condition comes from.) The bifurcation occurs as epsilon_v is lowered through epsilon_v,0, i.e. as the gain v* is raised. Proof uses the standard Hopf theorem (Lemma 6: Jacobian has a simple pure-imaginary pair; Lemma 7: d Re(lambda)/d eps < 0; Lemma 8: first Lyapunov coefficient negative, so the bifurcation is supercritical).
- **Theorem 3.** "Accepting Conjecture 20, below, for sigma > 6, there exists a stable limit cycle of (11) for sufficiently small, but finite, values of epsilon_lambda and epsilon_v. Equivalently, fixing lambda, there exists a stable limit cycle ... for sufficiently large, but finite, values of v*." Proof by singular perturbation and Poincare-Bendixson on a reduced planar system, then geometric singular perturbation theory.
- Supporting lemmas 13-17: Lyapunov argument that x_2 -> 0 and x_1 enters [-1, 1].
- The Fig. 1 caption labels a region "Theorem 1"; no Theorem 1 statement was found in the extracted text, so treat that label as an artifact of the preprint.

**What is conditional or only numerical.** Theorem 3 rests on **Conjecture 20**, the hyperbolicity (asymptotic stability, Floquet multipliers 1 and rho_2 < 1) of the periodic orbit of the reduced planar problem, which the authors call "numerically corroborated", not proved. Conjecture 20 is stated **only for sigma = 8**; the authors say "numerical investigation suggests that this result holds for generic sigma > 6", so Theorem 3's "for sigma > 6" is conditional on the conjecture at sigma = 8 and on numerics elsewhere. The curve epsilon_v*(epsilon_lambda) for epsilon_lambda > 0 (Fig. 1) is numerical. The "basin of attraction is quite large" is numerical. The asymmetric case (sigma_1 != sigma_2, lambda_1 != lambda_2) is "numerical experimentation suggests" persistence, unproved. The link between the pitchfork of the motivation subsystem and the Hopf of the closed loop is stated as future work.

**Empirical standing (blunt).** None as psychology. This is robot control. The word "drive" is borrowed from drive-reduction theory (Hull; file 16 sections 1 and 7), and the paper's cited psychology is Woodworth 1918 (*Dynamic Psychology*), Hull 1943 (*Principles of Behavior*) and a textbook (Petri & Govern 2012) [refs 37, 17, 30, read in the reference list]; it calls drive reduction a concept "in social psychology". No human or animal data constrain sigma, v*, lambda. The value-follows-distance law (Eq. 8) is Hull-style drive reduction, whose psychological record is poor (file 16 section 1). The honeybee mechanism it builds on is empirical in swarms (Seeley et al. 2012), but that is a group of bees, not one mind.

**Operand/operator translation for the algebra.** Operands: agent state (position, motivation simplex point, urgency vector). Operator: the vector-field map "m-weighted convex combination of per-drive policy fields" is a clean composition rule, and the value/motivation subsystem is a closed template for **drive competition with a built-in undecided state**. The proved content is a *regime statement*: two drives of equal urgency at a point equidistant from both goals can deadlock; with strong enough cross-inhibition (sigma > 6) and fast enough feedback the agent instead alternates (a stable cycle between serving drive 1 and drive 2). That is the nearest thing in the literature to a theorem of the form "two drives in conflict produce periodic switching, not a fixed compromise".

## 2. Pais et al. 2013 and Seeley et al. 2012: stop-signal cross-inhibition, deadlock and value-sensitivity

**Citations.** Pais, D., Hogan, P. M., Schlegel, T., Franks, N. R., Leonard, N. E. & Marshall, J. A. R. (2013). A mechanism for value-sensitive decision-making. *PLoS ONE* 8(9): e73216. DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0073216 [Crossref confirmed]; PMC3759446. Seeley, T. D., Visscher, P. K., Schlegel, T., Hogan, P. M., Franks, N. R. & Marshall, J. A. R. (2012). Stop signals provide cross inhibition in collective decision-making by honeybee swarms. *Science* 335, 108-111. DOI 10.1126/science.1210361 [Crossref confirmed; not read].

**Their vocabulary.** Commitment, abandonment, recruitment, cross-inhibition (stop signal), deadlock, value-sensitive, pitchfork, saddle-node, cusp catastrophe, hysteresis.

**Operands.** Fractions of a population committed to alternative A, to B, and undecided (y_A, y_B, y_U); alternative values v_A, v_B; Delta v = v_A - v_B; mean value vbar. Reverdy-Koditschek reinterpret the same equations as one decision-maker's motivation simplex.

**Operators / equations [read, PLOS PDF main text; Greek letters reconstructed from a garbled extraction].** Pais Eq. 1: dy_A = ( y_U gamma_A - y_A (alpha_A - y_U rho_A + y_B sigma_B) ) dt + noise term, and symmetrically for y_B, with y_U = 1 - y_A - y_B; gamma discovery, alpha abandonment, rho recruitment, sigma stop-signal (cross-inhibition) rate. Reverdy-Koditschek's Eq. 7 is this deterministic part with gamma_i = v~_i, alpha_i = 1/v~_i, rho_i = v~_i (same structure).

**Derived results [read, at the level of the main-text statements; supplementary proofs (Text S1) not read].** (i) For equal alternatives (Delta v = 0) the dynamics exhibit a **pitchfork bifurcation as a function of cross-inhibition rate sigma**: below it, a single stable deadlock attractor with the two alternatives equal; above it, two stable attractors (one per alternative) and a saddle. **Credit: Seeley et al. 2012** — Pais et al. write "our previous analysis showed ... a pitchfork bifurcation" and that the critical threshold "was previously [13] calculated", [13] being Seeley et al. 2012; Pais et al. 2013 restate it. (ii) For unequal alternatives and varying sigma, a **saddle-node bifurcation**; with both Delta v and sigma varied the bifurcation set is "qualitatively identical to the cusp catastrophe" (shown with bifurcation plots and simulations, not a stated theorem). (iii) **Value-sensitivity**: for two equally poor alternatives deadlock persists, for equally good ones a random choice breaks it. Caution: the paper states the direction two ways — the introduction says "a stronger cross-inhibition increases the minimum mean value of the alternatives above which a decision deadlock is broken", while the Fig. 2 caption says "the level of cross-inhibition required to break deadlock decreases with increasing value v", which implies the minimum mean value *decreases* as sigma rises. The Fig. 2 version matches the plotted threshold; do not rely on the introduction's wording without the threshold formula (Eq. 3, not legible in the extraction). (iv) **Hysteresis** in Delta v. (v) A Weber-law-like coefficient K controlled by sigma, with lower sigma giving lower K. The one result called proved in the main text is the separation of timescales (fast convergence to a one-dimensional slow manifold, Eq. 2) by singular perturbation in Text S1.

**Empirical standing.** Strong for honeybee swarms qualitatively (Seeley et al. 2012 showed that stop signals avoid deadlock in nest-site choice); the mapping to a *single* mind is an analogy asserted by Reverdy-Koditschek, not tested.

**Translation.** Operand: a probability/proportion vector over "active drive i" plus "none". Operator: pairwise cross-inhibition with strength sigma. The analysed structure (pitchfork, Seeley et al. 2012; saddle-node and cusp, Pais et al. 2013) is the exact algebra of "when does conflict between two equal drives resolve into one winning versus stay stuck", with a bifurcation parameter that can be named (inhibition strength) and a value-sensitivity law (the threshold depends on absolute urgency, not just difference).

## 3. Competing accumulators: Usher & McClelland 2001; Bogacz et al. 2006

**Citations.** Usher, M. & McClelland, J. L. (2001). The time course of perceptual choice: the leaky, competing accumulator model. *Psychological Review* 108(3), 550-592. DOI 10.1037/0033-295X.108.3.550 [Crossref confirmed; not read]. Bogacz, R., Brown, E., Moehlis, J., Holmes, P. & Cohen, J. D. (2006). The physics of optimal decision making: a formal analysis of models of performance in two-alternative forced-choice tasks. *Psychological Review* 113(4), 700-765. DOI 10.1037/0033-295X.113.4.700 [Crossref confirmed]; PMID 17014301; PDF at sites.engineering.ucsb.edu/~moehlis/moehlis_papers/psych.pdf [read, relevant sections].

**Their vocabulary.** Decay (leak) k, mutual inhibition w, input I_i, decision units, balanced, decision line, drift-diffusion model.

**Operands.** Activities y_1, y_2 of two decision units (competing "option strengths"), inputs I_1, I_2.

**Operators / equations [read].** Linearised mutual-inhibition model (Eq. 14): dy_1 = (-k y_1 - w y_2 + I_1) dt + c dW_1; dy_2 = (-k y_2 - w y_1 + I_2) dt + c dW_2; y_1(0) = y_2(0) = 0. Rotated coordinates (Eq. 18): x_1 = (y_1 - y_2)/sqrt 2, x_2 = (y_1 + y_2)/sqrt 2. Decoupled Ornstein-Uhlenbeck processes (Eqs 21-22): dx_1 = ( (w - k) x_1 + (I_1 - I_2)/sqrt 2 ) dt + c dW_1'; dx_2 = ( (-k - w) x_2 + (I_1 + I_2)/sqrt 2 ) dt + c dW_2'.

**Theorem-like results [read].** The x_2 process is always a stable O-U process (rate k + w, since -k - w < 0). The x_1 process is stable if k > w and **unstable (self-reinforcing) if w > k**, i.e. when inhibition exceeds decay the winner-takes-all runaway appears along the difference coordinate. When **decay equals inhibition (k = w, "balanced")** the term (w - k) x_1 vanishes and x_1 is a pure drift-diffusion process (the DDM); if k = w is also large relative to noise, the 2-D model approximates the DDM (only approximates, because of threshold effects in 2-D, the authors state). Bogacz et al.'s abstract [read] states that of 6 models analysed "all but one can be reduced to the drift diffusion model" (the race model is the exception), which implements the statistically optimal test; this is an analytic equivalence under stated parameter conditions. They state the 2-D/DDM resemblance neglects threshold effects ("these models are not identical").

**Empirical standing.** The DDM fits human and animal choice-time distributions widely (Ratcliff; D089, D090 are the places to take that further), but fit does not distinguish these models from one another: Bogacz et al. note, with Ratcliff & Smith 2004's model-mimicry study, that the mutual inhibition model "can mimic the DDM, O-U, and race models for different parameter values", and that the DDM and O-U with lambda near 0 mimic each other. (Corrected at validation: an earlier version said the paper calls the six models "indistinguishable"; that wording is not in the paper.) The mapping to *drives* is the translation, not the authors' claim: the accumulators are perceptual evidence, not need states.

**Translation.** Operand: two drive activations y_1, y_2. The balanced condition k = w is a one-line law saying **a drive pair is a pure integrator of its urgency difference exactly when each drive's self-decay equals the other's inhibition of it**; w > k gives winner-take-all (a proved instability), k > w a compromise attractor. That is a two-parameter classification of drive-conflict regimes that is already proved, and it connects with the Miller conflict types in file 16 section 3 (approach-approach unstable, avoidance-avoidance stable).

## 4. Other candidates considered

- **Rabinovich et al. 2001, winnerless competition.** Rabinovich, M. I., Volkovskii, A., Lecanda, P., Huerta, R., Abarbanel, H. D. I. & Laurent, G. (2001). Dynamical encoding by networks of competing neuron groups: winnerless competition. *Phys. Rev. Lett.* 87, 068102. DOI 10.1103/PhysRevLett.87.068102 [Crossref confirmed]. From the abstract (fetched, [from abstract]): trajectories pass near heteroclinic connections between saddles, so information is carried by paths rather than stable states, with capacity of order e(N-1)! for N neurons (abstract re-checked at the APS page at validation). This is a candidate form for *sequential* switching between competing drives (the system never settles on one). The proof content, the heteroclinic-cycle conditions for generalised Lotka-Volterra systems, is standard in the dynamical-systems literature but was **not read here**; to be treated as a follow-up. [standard account / abstract]
- **Thompson, C. & Reverdy, P., "Drive-based motivation for coordination of limit cycle behaviors"**, 2019 IEEE 58th Conference on Decision and Control (CDC), DOI 10.1109/CDC40024.2019.9028915 [Crossref confirmed; seminar abstract read, paper not read]. Extends the framework from point-attractor tasks to limit-cycle tasks: an attracting set of orbits flowing repeatedly between two Hopf-oscillator limit cycles, with "general results for arbitrary disjoint limit cycles" announced [from abstract].
- **Thompson, C. & Reverdy, P., "Multi-Agent Recurrent Rendezvous Using Drive-Based Motivation"**, arXiv:2109.13408v1 [math.OC], 28 Sep 2021 [read at validation, theorem statements]. N agents, each with a binary motivation (own task vs rendezvous), coupled. Proved: Theorem 3.7, in the singular limit lambda -> infinity a critical sigma* = 1/(4 y*(1 - y*)) at which the deadlock point loses stability; Theorem 3.8, for finite lambda a sufficient bound sigma-hat = (N lambda + N + y* - 1)/(2 N y*(1 - y*)) above which deadlock is unstable (a trace argument). Lemma 3.5 gives a deadlock equilibrium for N >= 3, with its stability (Claim 3.6) only numerically supported; the recurrent-rendezvous attractor (Theorem 3.10) is conditional on Claim 3.9, and the "strange attractor" is numerical. This is the one proved result found that couples drive-conflict systems *across* agents.
- **Reverdy, P., "Two paths to finding the pitchfork bifurcation in motivation dynamics"**, 2019 IEEE CDC, pp. 8030-8035, DOI 10.1109/CDC40024.2019.9029711 [Crossref confirmed at validation; not read]. By title, it addresses the pitchfork of the motivation subsystem that Reverdy-Koditschek 2018 left as future work.
- **Gul & Pesendorfer 2001, "Temptation and self-control", Econometrica 69, 1403-1435** (DOI 10.1111/1468-0262.00252, Crossref confirmed, title only). A representation theorem for preferences over menus with temptation: utility of a menu = max over its items of u(x) + v(x) minus max over the menu of v(y). Proved, but it is an axiomatic preference representation, not state dynamics; no equilibrium of a dynamical system and no switching dynamics. [standard account] Deep-read is D094/D095.
- **Fudenberg & Levine 2006, "A dual-self model of impulse control", AER 96(5), 1449-1476** (DOI 10.1257/aer.96.5.1449, Crossref confirmed; not read). The long-run self pays a cost to restrain a myopic short-run self, giving a game within one agent with an equilibrium; it is the main case of a proved *equilibrium* between two internal selves. [standard account] Deep-read is D094.
- **Miller 1944 / Dollard & Miller 1950 approach-avoidance gradients and Atkinson-Birch dynamics of action**: already treated in file 16 (sections 3 and Atkinson/Revelle) with stated corollaries (equilibrium at d* = (a - c)/(b - e); Atkinson's peak at P = 0.5). Not repeated.
- **Hughes et al. 2018** (the seed). Hughes, E., Leibo, J. Z., Phillips, M. G., Tuyls, K., Duenez-Guzman, E. A., Garcia Castaneda, A., Dunning, I., Zhu, T., McKee, K. R., Koster, R., Roff, H. & Graepel, T. (2018). Inequity aversion improves cooperation in intertemporal social dilemmas. NeurIPS 2018, arXiv:1803.08884 [abstract read]. Inequity aversion is a reward-shaping term inside each agent (Fehr-Schmidt form per file 23), the "conflict" being between own payoff and others' payoffs; the results are empirical across sequential-social-dilemma gridworlds. No theorem.
- **Helbing, Farkas & Vicsek 2000.** Nature 407, 487-490, DOI 10.1038/35035023 [Crossref confirmed]. Read at source in file 23: simulation; no proof. Not a conflict-between-drives model with a proof.

## 5. What exists versus what does not

**Exists (with a proof, or an exact analytic reduction):**
- Two competing "motivation" states with an explicit **undecided** state, a proved Hopf bifurcation (Theorem 2, sigma > 6; epsilon_v,0 = (3 - sqrt 6)/6 at sigma = 8, supercritical) and conditional existence of a stable switching cycle (Theorem 3, conditional on Conjecture 20). Single-agent, two drives, symmetric.
- A cross-inhibition model with an analytic pitchfork threshold (Seeley et al. 2012) and a saddle-node and cusp structure shown by bifurcation analysis, describing **when two equal drives stay deadlocked versus break symmetry**, and value-sensitivity (Pais et al. 2013).
- Proved deadlock-breaking thresholds for N coupled agents each running a two-drive motivation system (Thompson & Reverdy 2021, Theorems 3.7-3.8).
- An exact decoupling (O-U in difference and sum coordinates) giving **k = w as the integrator condition and w > k as winner-take-all runaway** (Bogacz et al.).
- Preference-representation theorems for temptation (Gul-Pesendorfer) and an equilibrium for the dual-self model (Fudenberg-Levine), with their economic-level validation not checked here.

**Does not exist (as far as found):**
- A proved result for conflict among **three or more** drives with unequal parameters; the Reverdy-Koditschek proofs need N = 2, symmetry, planar workspace and a specific distance function, and the asymmetric case is numerical.
- Any of these models with **parameters fitted to human data on named drives** (curiosity, fear, desire). sigma, v*, lambda, k, w have no empirical priors for human drives.
- A proved link from conflict inside one agent to **conflict between group-level drives**. Qualified at validation: Thompson & Reverdy 2021 prove deadlock-instability thresholds for N coupled agents each with an internal two-drive conflict, so "every proof is for one agent or one swarm" was too strong; what is still missing is a proved composition law from individual drives to a named *group* drive.
- A drive-labelled model with **external stimuli** and proofs: Reverdy-Koditschek say responding to stimuli while keeping the cycle is future work.
- Theorems for the seeds: neither Hughes nor Helbing has any.

**Cross-references.** Miller/Atkinson and Keramati-Gutkin in 16; Fudenberg-Levine and Gul-Pesendorfer in 36 and D094/D095; accumulators and first passage in D089; the general question of which compositional-dynamics assumptions drive models violate (Lipschitz, passivity) in 40 and D030. The Reverdy-Koditschek vector field is Lipschitz and has a Lyapunov argument for x; that is a specific assumption check D030 can reuse.
