## 2. Measurement evidence, list by list

### 2.1 Fundamental social motives (FSMI)
- Citation: Neel, R., Kenrick, D. T., White, A. E., & Neuberg, S. L. (2016). Individual differences in fundamental social motives. *J. Personality and Social Psychology* 110(6), 887-907. DOI 10.1037/pspp0000068 (resolved at Crossref [read, metadata only]).
- Pick, C. M., Ko, A., Kenrick, D. T., et al. (2022). Fundamental social motives measured across forty-two cultures in two waves. *Scientific Data* 9, 499. DOI 10.1038/s41597-022-01579-w (resolved at Crossref; full text read via Europe PMC PMC9380674 in the validation pass [read]).
- [read, dataset page abstract] Seven motives: self-protection, disease avoidance, affiliation, status, mate acquisition, mate retention, kin care. 42 countries, N = 15,915, two cross-sectional waves (wave 1 mid-2016 to late 2019, 32 countries, N = 8,998; wave 2 April-November 2020, 29 countries, N = 6,917; 19 countries in both, N = 10,907). The page reports **no** factor-analytic or invariance result. It is a data descriptor; the structure claim is not tested there. [read, full text, validation pass] Confirmed: the Technical Validation section only says the FSMI is a "published scale with established reliability and validity indicators" (citing Neel et al. 2016); Table 1 describes 11 six-item subscales; no CFA, invariance test or alphas are reported.
- Billet, M. I., McCall, H. C., & Schaller, M. (2023). What motives do people most want to know about when meeting another person? An investigation into prioritization of information about seven fundamental motives. *Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin* 49(4), 495-509 (online 2022). DOI 10.1177/01461672211069468 (PMC9989231 [read]). They use the 11-subscale, 65-item FSMI with alphas .77 to .94 [read]. They do not test the factor structure. Their reported correlations between prioritising a motive and one's own score on it average .25 and .26 (two measures), and for mate retention are .15 and .05 [read, Table 5]. So the instrument has reliability but the sources I could reach give **no independent confirmatory factor analysis**.
- Verdict: the best-designed list for **social** drives, with a published instrument and a large cross-cultural dataset, but its structure was proposed and measured by one research programme (Kenrick, Neuberg, Schaller and students). Independent structural replication: **not found**. The 2016 priming-era caution in file 15 still applies to the experimental evidence, not to the questionnaire.
- Note on count: the instrument has 11 subscales for 7 motives (Billet et al. [read]); the mate-seeking and mate-retention and the self-protection splits are not just seven numbers. Treat "7" as a theoretical count.

### 2.2 Reiss 16 desires
- Reiss 2004, DOI 10.1037/1089-2680.8.3.179 [file 15, Crossref confirmed].
- [search] Development: 25 candidate motives, four exploratory factor analyses and one confirmatory analysis, yielding 16 (unverified secondary description). The supporting studies are Reiss, S., & Havercamp, S. M. (1998). Toward a comprehensive assessment of fundamental motivation: factor structure of the Reiss Profiles. *Psychological Assessment* 10(2), 97-106, DOI 10.1037/1040-3590.10.2.97, and Havercamp, S. M., & Reiss, S. (2003). A comprehensive assessment of human strivings: test-retest reliability and validity of the Reiss Profile. *J. Personality Assessment* 81(2), 123-132, DOI 10.1207/s15327752jpa8102_04 (both Crossref-resolved, validation pass). [abstract, validation pass] The 1998 abstract reports EFA and CFA across 7 studies (N = 2,548) and **a 15-factor solution**, not 16. A popular source claims a norm sample over 135,000; non-peer-reviewed.
- A search for an independent confirmatory factor analysis returned nothing. Direction D118 owns this and should be treated as open; this file does not resolve it.
- Verdict: **no independent measurement support found**. Also internally awkward for an algebra: the author's own claim is that the 16 are largely unrelated (file 15), so the list offers axes but no structure. Treat as a **candidate vocabulary**, not as evidence that 16 distinct drives exist.

### 2.3 Self-determination theory needs
- Chen, B., Vansteenkiste, M., Beyers, W., Boone, L., Deci, E. L., Van der Kaap-Deeder, J., Duriez, B., Lens, W., Matos, L., Mouratidis, A., Ryan, R. M., Sheldon, K. M., Soenens, B., Van Petegem, S., & Verstuyf, J. (2015). Basic psychological need satisfaction, need frustration, and need strength across four cultures. *Motivation and Emotion* 39(2), 216-236 (online 2014). DOI 10.1007/s11031-014-9450-1 (resolved at Crossref; abstract read in the author-posted PDF at ULB DI-fusion [read, abstract]).
- [read, abstract] Study 1: Belgium and China, N = 685. Study 2: Belgium, China, USA, Peru, N = 1,051. Adapted scale showed measurement equivalence across groups; each need's satisfaction predicted well-being and each need's frustration predicted ill-being, equivalently across the four countries.
- Verdict: **good support for three-way separability and universality of effect, but not independent of the proposers: Deci and Ryan are co-authors of Chen et al.**, in four countries, but the test is about **outcomes** (needs predict well-being), the criterion by which SDT defines a need. That is a circularity risk: a need is whatever, when thwarted, harms well-being. SDT's authors are heavily represented in the supporting literature. It does not show that the three are the only needs. Autonomy, competence and relatedness are also the most "vocabulary-broad" in file 15's table, and relatedness collapses into affiliation.

### 2.4 Panksepp systems, as measured by the ANPS
- Pedersen, G., Selsbakk, J. M., Wilberg, T., & Karterud, S. (2014). Testing different versions of the Affective Neuroscience Personality Scales in a clinical sample. *PLoS ONE* 9(10), e109394. DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0109394 (resolved at Crossref; Crossref's author records have given and family names swapped, which is likely the source of the earlier wrong "W. S." initials; the second author is Merete Selsbakk Johansen, recorded by Crossref as "Johansen Merete Selsbakk" [read]).
- [read] N = 546 personality-disorder patients. Subscale internal consistency 0.74 to 0.87. Six-factor CFA of the full ANPS: chi-square 7828.18, df 3387, RMSEA 0.049 (0.048 to 0.050), TLI 0.752, CFI 0.759, WRMR 2.112: "poor to mediocre fit". Pearson inter-scale correlations (Table 3): PLAY-SEEK .50, PLAY-CARE .39, PLAY-FEAR -.22, SEEK-CARE .24, SEEK-FEAR -.28, SEEK-ANGER .14, CARE-SADNESS .36, FEAR-ANGER .22, ANGER-SADNESS .25, **FEAR-SADNESS .59**; PLAY-ANGER .01, PLAY-SADNESS -.06, SEEK-SADNESS -.07, CARE-ANGER -.03, CARE-FEAR .16. (All values re-checked against Table 3 and Table 8 of the article XML in the validation pass [read].) The abstract states PLAY with SEEK and SADNESS with FEAR are "too highly correlated".
- Caveat: a clinical sample (77% female, mean age 32), one study; the shorter ANPS-S and BANPS fit better (Table 8: BANPS CFI .933, TLI .924, RMSEA .053; ANPS-S CFI .863, TLI .851, RMSEA .063) [read].
- Marengo, D., Davis, K. L., Gradwohl, G. Ö., & Montag, C. (2021). A meta-analysis on individual differences in primary emotional systems and Big Five personality traits. *Scientific Reports* 11, 7453. DOI 10.1038/s41598-021-84366-8 (resolved at Crossref; full text via Europe PMC PMC8018956 [read]). 21 samples, 612 effect sizes; SEEKING with Openness, PLAY with Extraversion, CARE and low ANGER with Agreeableness, and FEAR, SADNESS and ANGER with Neuroticism; Conscientiousness in the lower effect-size range for all six. Pooled r [read]: SEEKING-Openness .47, SEEKING-Extraversion .34; PLAY-Extraversion .55; CARE-Agreeableness .40; ANGER-Agreeableness -.41, ANGER-Neuroticism .46; FEAR-Neuroticism .69; SADNESS-Neuroticism .62.
- An Italian ANPS 2.4 study (N = 843) reported a similar factor structure to the original [search summary]; I did not locate its citation.
- Verdict: **the animal circuit evidence is a separate line** (file 15/22) and stays unreplicated here. In humans, the self-report measure recovers SEEKING, CARE, PLAY, FEAR-ish, ANGER-ish systems **as trait dimensions that load on the Big Five**; FEAR and SADNESS are not separable in the measured data (r = .59, one sample), and PLAY and SEEKING partly merge (r = .50). This is the strongest direct measurement evidence in this file that two of Panksepp's systems fail to separate when measured by questionnaire. It does not refute them as brain systems; it shows the questionnaire route to "independent drives" fails for those pairs.

### 2.5 Schwartz values
- Schwartz 1992 (DOI 10.1016/S0065-2601(08)60281-6) and Schwartz et al. 2012 (DOI 10.1037/a0029393) [file 15, Crossref confirmed]. Schwartz, S. H. & Cieciuch, J. (2022). Measuring the refined theory of individual values in 49 cultural groups: psychometrics of the Revised Portrait Value Questionnaire. *Assessment* 29(5), 1005-1019 (online 2021). DOI 10.1177/1073191121998760 (resolved at Crossref; full text via Europe PMC PMC9131418 [read]).
- [read] PVQ-RR across 49 cultural groups, N = 53,472, 32 language versions. 15 of 19 values reliably measured in the vast majority of groups, two more in most. Almost all values measured invariantly at the configural and metric level; the authors state they "did not expect to establish scalar invariance", which is what mean comparison needs. MDS on the **pooled** correlation matrix of the 49 groups reproduced the theorised circular order with no reversals; each group's MDS projection was Procrustes-rotated toward that theorised target, so per-group order is assessed against an imposed target, not found free.
- Verdict: **the strongest psychometric support in the whole concordance** for a structure, and the only one with an opposition geometry. Caveats: Schwartz's group authored the instrument and most validations (independence limited); the circle is imposed as an ordering rather than found; and values are not drives (file 15: they weight drives). Four of 19 values are not reliably measured in the vast majority of groups (two of those four are reliable in most groups).

### 2.6 Moral foundations
- Atari, M., Haidt, J., Graham, J., Koleva, S., Stevens, S. T., & Dehghani, M. (2023). Morality beyond the WEIRD: how the nomological network of morality varies across cultures. *J. Personality and Social Psychology* 125(5), 1157-1188. DOI 10.1037/pspp0000470 (resolved at Crossref; a 2022 preprint at DOI 10.31234/osf.io/q6c9r, also resolved). [abstract, validation pass] MFQ-2 developed on data from 25 populations: Study 1 N = 3,360 (5 populations), Study 2 N = 3,902 (19 populations; factor analysis, ESEM, network psychometrics, alignment equivalence), Study 3 N = 1,410 (3 populations); six foundations (care, equality, proportionality, loyalty, authority, purity). Fit statistics not quoted; the abstract claims the MFQ-2 "fares well" across contexts. This is the proposers' own group.
- Independent critiques: Iurino, K., & Saucier, G. (2020). Testing measurement invariance of the Moral Foundations Questionnaire across 27 countries. *Assessment* 27(2), 365-372, DOI 10.1177/1073191118817916 [abstract, validation pass]: "it is difficult to specify Moral Foundations Questionnaire items in a quantitative five-factor model that will converge nonproblematically across a wide variety of populations." Doğruyol, B., Alper, S., & Yilmaz, O. (2019). The five-factor model of the moral foundations theory is stable across WEIRD and non-WEIRD cultures. *Personality and Individual Differences* 151, 109547, DOI 10.1016/j.paid.2019.109547 (existence and title confirmed; result as summarised by search, abstract not read). Atari, M., Graham, J., & Dehghani, M. (2020). Foundations of morality in Iran. *Evolution and Human Behavior* 41(5), 367-384, DOI 10.1016/j.evolhumbehav.2020.07.014 [abstract, validation pass]: six studies, N = 1,945, Persian MFQ structural validity, US-Iran comparison, and an additional Iran-specific foundation ("Qeirat"); the "scalar non-invariance" detail is from a search summary and not in the abstract (not author-independent: Atari and Graham are MFT authors). De Buck & Pauwels (2023, *Psychologica Belgica* 63(1), 92-104, DOI 10.5334/pb.1188, Crossref-resolved) [read, full text PMC10376904]: in an online student sample of N = 1,496 (authors at Ghent University; Dutch translation) the five-factor MFQ20 **failed to converge** (Fairness and Purity too highly correlated), three-factor retained at CFI/TLI .720/.680, RMSEA .081; the Sacredness Scale's five-factor model fit better (CFI/TLI .940/.930, RMSEA .06). One convenience sample of students; translation-specific.
- Verdict: **mixed to weak**. Five or six foundations do not reliably emerge as separate factors outside their home samples. Weak candidate as primitives; better treated as a moral-evaluation layer on top of the drives (consistent with file 41).

### 2.7 Maslow and Kenrick
- Tay, L., & Diener, E. (2011). Needs and subjective well-being around the world. *J. Personality and Social Psychology* 101(2), 354-365. DOI 10.1037/a0023779 [Crossref-resolved; abstract read via Europe PMC/OpenAlex in the validation pass]. [read, abstract] 123 countries; need fulfilment associated with subjective well-being across world regions; associations of well-being with fulfilment of specific needs were largely independent of whether other needs were fulfilled. I could not confirm from the abstract which survey was used.
- Verdict: **against strict prepotency (D119's question)**, and consistent with needs being **separable and additive** in their well-being effects. It supports separability of the needs it measured, not the list as complete. Treat as support for a parallel-drive algebra rather than a hierarchy.

### 2.8 Lexical lists and Big Five (files 32, 34)
- [file 32] The psycholexical method gives a completeness test, not a list, and its structure for values disagrees with Schwartz's circle (open dispute). [file 34] The Big Five/HEXACO are traits from descriptor analysis; links to drives are post hoc. They serve as **measurement coordinates** for parameter vectors, and the Marengo et al. meta-analysis (2.4) gives the bridge between Panksepp scales and those coordinates.

Two patterns:

1. Nothing scores M on the **drive** level in the strict sense (independent lab, independent method, structure recovered). The M ratings sit on SDT needs and Schwartz values, which are the least "drive-like" of the lists (outcome-defined and value-defined).
2. The lists agree at the level of **pairs and families**, not individuals: [belonging + care + kin], [status + power + achievement], [threat: fear + self-protection + disease], [seeking + curiosity + autonomy], [mating]. Within each family, measurement does not separate the members cleanly (FEAR-SADNESS, PLAY-SEEKING, relatedness-affiliation).

## 4. Minimal surviving set

Proposed as a **working basis for the algebra**, to be held as editable data, not as a claim that these are the drives:

1. **Affiliation** (belonging, relatedness; absorbs attachment as the dyadic case)
2. **Status** (rank, power, achievement as a social product)
3. **Threat** (fear and self-protection; disease avoidance as a subtype; sadness/grief kept as an **outcome state** of threat or loss, not an independent drive, on the FEAR-SADNESS evidence)
4. **Seeking** (curiosity, exploration, autonomy/competence; play carried as its social expression, on the PLAY-SEEK evidence)
5. **Care** (kin and nurture; split from affiliation only if a task needs it, since CARE and affiliation are separately named in FSMI and SDT but not separated by a measurement I could reach)
6. **Mating** (acquisition and retention as two parameters of one drive until a model needs two)
7. **Anger** (reactive: a response operator keyed to blocked drives or violated status, not a standing drive; the lists disagree, file 15)

Rationale and honesty: seven in, eleven or so out. The set is smaller than any individual list except SDT because merged families are treated as one operand with sub-parameters. This is a *modelling* judgement, labelled as such. The measurement shows that the pair-level merges are supported (they fail to separate empirically); it does not show that the merged families are the true primitives.

Things deliberately kept out, with the reason:
- Values (Schwartz) and moral foundations: slow-changing weights on drives, and the best measured. Use Schwartz's circle as the **compatibility/opposition matrix between drive-weights** (file 15), not as operands.
- Reiss's extra axes: no independent support; keep as extension vocabulary.
- Big Five/HEXACO: parameter-vector coordinates (file 34), bridged to Panksepp via Marengo et al. 2021.

Consequence for later work: any simulation that relies on seven separate drives needs either a stated coupling or a stated correlation among them. Pedersen's Table 3 gives measured inter-scale correlations in one clinical sample (not general), and could seed a prior correlation structure for the five Panksepp-derived ones; nothing equivalent was found for the FSMI.

## 5. What exists vs what does not

**Exists**
- Large cross-cultural measurement for values (49 groups, N = 53,472) and a 42-country dataset for the seven social motives (N = 15,915) plus a four-country test of SDT needs.
- A published inter-scale correlation matrix for the ANPS, showing which Panksepp systems fail to separate in self-report.
- A meta-analytic bridge between Panksepp-style scales and the Big Five (21 samples).

**Does not exist (or was not found)**
- An independent confirmatory factor analysis of the FSMI seven-factor structure.
- An independent replication of Reiss's 16-factor structure (D118).
- One study that administers the lists to the same people and fits them jointly. The concordance in file 15 is a vocabulary map, and the measurement evidence here comes from separate samples per list. No joint measurement of FSMI, SDT, ANPS and Schwartz in one sample has been located; this is the empirical gap that would settle the minimal set.
- Any measurement evidence that the primitives are *drives* (state variables with dynamics) rather than trait dimensions. Every instrument above measures stable individual differences or satisfaction outcomes.

## 6. Operand/operator translation

- Operands: a drive-weight vector over the seven-item basis per agent, plus a state vector (satisfaction/frustration, SDT-style) per drive.
- Two kinds of coupling, to be kept separate: **trait covariance** (measured, e.g. Pedersen's table, FEAR-SADNESS .59) and **value compatibility** (Schwartz circle, signs). Different semantics; do not merge into one matrix.
- Needs are additive in well-being effects (Tay & Diener), so default to additive composition and make prepotency an optional gate, with the weight an editable setting.
