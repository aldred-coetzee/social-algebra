# Prior work 050: concordance of candidate drive lists, and which primitives have independent measurement support

Direction: D117

**Method and honesty note.** The work was done from the survey files plus a targeted pass at measurement evidence (factor structure, invariance, replication), because "recurs across theories" (file 15's cross-walk) is a count of vocabulary, not a measurement. ## 2. Measurement evidence, list by list

### 2.1 Fundamental social motives (FSMI)
- Citation: Neel, R., Kenrick, D. T., White, A. E., & Neuberg, S. L. (2016). Individual differences in fundamental social motives. *J. Personality and Social Psychology* 110(6), 887-907. DOI 10.1037/pspp0000068 (resolved at Crossref [read, metadata only]).
- Pick, C. M., Ko, A., Kenrick, D. T., et al. (2022). Fundamental social motives measured across forty-two cultures in two waves. *Scientific Data* 9, 499. DOI 10.1038/s41597-022-01579-w (DOI as stated on the Aberystwyth repository page [read]).
- [read, dataset page abstract] Seven motives: self-protection, disease avoidance, affiliation, status, mate acquisition, mate retention, kin care. 42 countries, N = 15,915, two cross-sectional waves (wave 1 mid-2016 to late 2019, 32 countries, N = 8,998; wave 2 April-November 2020, 29 countries, N = 6,917; 19 countries in both, N = 10,907). The page reports **no** factor-analytic or invariance result. It is a data descriptor; the structure claim is not tested there.
- [abstract/search] Billet, McCall & Schaller (2022, PSPB; found at PMC9989231) use the 11-subscale FSMI with alphas .77 to .94 [read, first 100k characters]. They do not test the factor structure. Their reported correlation between prioritising a motive and one's own score on it averages about .25, and for mate retention is .15 and .05 [read]. So the instrument has reliability but the sources I could reach give **no independent confirmatory factor analysis**.
- Verdict: the best-designed list for **social** drives, with a published instrument and a large cross-cultural dataset, but its structure was proposed and measured by one research programme (Kenrick, Neuberg, Schaller and students). Independent structural replication: **not found**. The 2016 priming-era caution in file 15 still applies to the experimental evidence, not to the questionnaire.
- Note on count: the instrument has 11 subscales for 7 motives (Billet et al. [read]); the mate-seeking and mate-retention and the self-protection splits are not just seven numbers. Treat "7" as a theoretical count.

### 2.2 Reiss 16 desires
- Reiss 2004, DOI 10.1037/1089-2680.8.3.179 [file 15, Crossref confirmed].
- [search] Development: 25 candidate motives, four exploratory factor analyses and one confirmatory analysis, yielding 16; the supporting studies are by Reiss and Havercamp (1998, 2003). A popular source claims a norm sample over 135,000; non-peer-reviewed.
- A search for an independent confirmatory factor analysis returned nothing. Direction D118 owns this and should be treated as open; this file does not resolve it.
- Verdict: **no independent measurement support found**. Also internally awkward for an algebra: the author's own claim is that the 16 are largely unrelated (file 15), so the list offers axes but no structure. Treat as a **candidate vocabulary**, not as evidence that 16 distinct drives exist.

### 2.3 Self-determination theory needs
- Chen, B. et al. (2015). Basic psychological need satisfaction, need frustration, and need strength across four cultures. *Motivation and Emotion* 39(2), 216-236. DOI 10.1007/s11031-014-9450-1 (as stated by a repository listing [abstract/search]).
- [abstract/search] Study 1: Belgium and China, N = 685. Study 2: Belgium, China, USA, Peru, N = 1,051. Adapted scale showed measurement equivalence across groups; each need's satisfaction predicted well-being and each need's frustration predicted ill-being, equivalently across the four countries.
- Verdict: **good, partly independent support for three-way separability and universality of effect**, in four countries, but the test is about **outcomes** (needs predict well-being), the criterion by which SDT defines a need. That is a circularity risk: a need is whatever, when thwarted, harms well-being. SDT's authors are heavily represented in the supporting literature. It does not show that the three are the only needs. Autonomy, competence and relatedness are also the most "vocabulary-broad" in file 15's table, and relatedness collapses into affiliation.

### 2.4 Panksepp systems, as measured by the ANPS
- Pedersen, W. S. et al. (2014). Testing different versions of the Affective Neuroscience Personality Scales in a clinical sample. *PLoS ONE* 9(10), e109394. DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0109394 (resolved at Crossref [read]).
- [read] N = 546 personality-disorder patients. Subscale internal consistency 0.74 to 0.87. Six-factor CFA of the full ANPS: chi-square 7828.18, df 3387, RMSEA 0.049 (0.048 to 0.050), TLI 0.752, CFI 0.759, WRMR 2.112: "poor to mediocre fit". Pearson inter-scale correlations (Table 3): PLAY-SEEK .50, PLAY-CARE .39, PLAY-FEAR -.22, SEEK-CARE .24, SEEK-FEAR -.28, SEEK-ANGER .14, CARE-SADNESS .36, FEAR-ANGER .22, ANGER-SADNESS .25, **FEAR-SADNESS .59**; PLAY-ANGER .01, PLAY-SADNESS -.06, SEEK-SADNESS -.07, CARE-ANGER -.03. The abstract states PLAY with SEEK and SADNESS with FEAR are "too highly correlated".
- Caveat: a clinical sample, one study; the shorter ANPS-S and BANPS fit better per the abstract.
- Marengo, D., Davis, K. L., Gradwohl, G. Ö., & Montag, C. (2021). A meta-analysis on individual differences in primary emotional systems and Big Five personality traits. *Scientific Reports* 11 (DOI 10.1038/s41598-021-84366-8 resolved at Crossref [read]; article number not obtained). [abstract/search] 21 samples; SEEKING with Openness, PLAY with Extraversion, CARE and low ANGER with Agreeableness, and FEAR, SADNESS and ANGER with Neuroticism; Conscientiousness weakly tied to all six. Exact pooled r not retrieved.
- An Italian ANPS 2.4 study (N = 843) reported a similar factor structure to the original [search summary]; I did not locate its citation.
- Verdict: **the animal circuit evidence is a separate line** (file 15/22) and stays unreplicated here. In humans, the self-report measure recovers SEEKING, CARE, PLAY, FEAR-ish, ANGER-ish systems **as trait dimensions that load on the Big Five**; FEAR and SADNESS are not separable in the measured data (r = .59, one sample), and PLAY and SEEKING partly merge (r = .50). This is the strongest direct measurement evidence in this file that two of Panksepp's systems fail to separate when measured by questionnaire. It does not refute them as brain systems; it shows the questionnaire route to "independent drives" fails for those pairs.

### 2.5 Schwartz values
- Schwartz 1992 (DOI 10.1016/S0065-2601(08)60281-6) and Schwartz et al. 2012 (DOI 10.1037/a0029393) [file 15, Crossref confirmed]. Schwartz, S. H. & Cieciuch, J. (2022). Measuring the refined theory of individual values in 49 cultural groups: psychometrics of the Revised Portrait Value Questionnaire. *Assessment* 29(5), 1005-1019. DOI 10.1177/1073191121998760 (as in the SAGE URL found by search [abstract/search]).
- [abstract/search] PVQ-RR across 49 cultural groups, N = 53,472, 32 language versions. 15 of 19 values reliably measured in the vast majority of groups, two more in most. Almost all values measured invariantly at the configural and metric level (not stated here: scalar invariance, which is what mean comparison needs). MDS reproduced the theorised circular order of the 19 values in every group.
- Verdict: **the strongest psychometric support in the whole concordance** for a structure, and the only one with an opposition geometry. Caveats: Schwartz's group authored the instrument and most validations (independence limited); the circle is imposed as an ordering rather than found; and values are not drives (file 15: they weight drives). Four of 19 values are unreliable at the group level.

### 2.6 Moral foundations
- Atari, M., Haidt, J., Graham, J., Koleva, S., Stevens, S. T., & Dehghani, M. (2023). Morality beyond the WEIRD: how the nomological network of morality varies across cultures. *J. Personality and Social Psychology*. DOI 10.1037/pspp0000470 (resolved at Crossref [read, metadata only]; a 2022 preprint at DOI 10.31234/osf.io/q6c9r). Abstract not obtained, so number of cultures and fit statistics are **not** quoted here.
- [search summary] Independent critiques: Iurino & Saucier (2020), 27 countries, did not replicate the five-factor model; Doğruyol, Alper & Yilmaz (2019) found the five-factor model held across WEIRD and non-WEIRD samples with differing item loadings; Atari, Graham & Dehghani (2020) reported scalar non-invariance between US and Iranian samples. De Buck & Pauwels (2023, *Psychologica Belgica* 63(1), 92-104, DOI 10.5334/pb.1188) [read, abstract level]: in 1,496 Dutch-speaking Ghent students the five-factor MFQ20 **failed to converge**, three-factor retained at CFI/TLI .720/.680, RMSEA .081; the Sacredness Scale's five-factor model fit better (CFI/TLI .940/.930, RMSEA .06). One convenience sample of students; translation-specific.
- Verdict: **mixed to weak**. Five or six foundations do not reliably emerge as separate factors outside their home samples. Weak candidate as primitives; better treated as a moral-evaluation layer on top of the drives (consistent with file 41).

### 2.7 Maslow and Kenrick
- Tay & Diener 2011, DOI 10.1037/a0023779 [read, Crossref metadata]. [abstract/search] 123 countries; need fulfilment associated with subjective well-being across world regions; associations of well-being with fulfilment of specific needs were largely independent of whether other needs were fulfilled. I could not confirm from the abstract which survey was used.
- Verdict: **against strict prepotency (D119's question)**, and consistent with needs being **separable and additive** in their well-being effects. It supports separability of the needs it measured, not the list as complete. Treat as support for a parallel-drive algebra rather than a hierarchy.

### 2.8 Lexical lists and Big Five (files 32, 34)
- [file 32] The psycholexical method gives a completeness test, not a list, and its structure for values disagrees with Schwartz's circle (open dispute). [file 34] The Big Five/HEXACO are traits from descriptor analysis; links to drives are post hoc. They serve as **measurement coordinates** for parameter vectors, and the Marengo et al. meta-analysis (2.4) gives the bridge between Panksepp scales and those coordinates.

Two patterns:

1. Nothing scores M on the **drive** level in the strict sense (independent lab, independent method, structure recovered). The M ratings sit on SDT needs and Schwartz values, which are the least "drive-like" of the lists (outcome-defined and value-defined).
2. The lists agree at the level of **pairs and families**, not individuals: [belonging + care + kin], [status + power + achievement], [threat: fear + self-protection + disease], [seeking + curiosity + autonomy], [mating]. Within each family, measurement does not separate the members cleanly (FEAR-SADNESS, PLAY-SEEKING, relatedness-affiliation).

## 4. Minimal surviving set

Proposed as a **working basis for the algebra**, to be held as editable data, not as a claim that these are the drives:

1. **Affiliation** (belonging, relatedness; absorbs attachment as the dyadic case)
2. **Status** (rank, power, achievement as a social product)
3. **Threat** (fear and self-protection; disease avoidance as a subtype; sadness/grief kept as an **outcome state** of threat or loss, not an independent drive, on the FEAR-SADNESS evidence)
4. **Seeking** (curiosity, exploration, autonomy/competence; play carried as its social expression, on the PLAY-SEEK evidence)
5. **Care** (kin and nurture; split from affiliation only if a task needs it, since CARE and affiliation are separately named in FSMI and SDT but not separated by a measurement I could reach)
6. **Mating** (acquisition and retention as two parameters of one drive until a model needs two)
7. **Anger** (reactive: a response operator keyed to blocked drives or violated status, not a standing drive; the lists disagree, file 15)

Rationale and honesty: seven in, eleven or so out. The set is smaller than any individual list except SDT because merged families are treated as one operand with sub-parameters. This is a *modelling* judgement, labelled as such. The measurement shows that the pair-level merges are supported (they fail to separate empirically); it does not show that the merged families are the true primitives.

Things deliberately kept out, with the reason:
- Values (Schwartz) and moral foundations: slow-changing weights on drives, and the best measured. Use Schwartz's circle as the **compatibility/opposition matrix between drive-weights** (file 15), not as operands.
- Reiss's extra axes: no independent support; keep as extension vocabulary.
- Big Five/HEXACO: parameter-vector coordinates (file 34), bridged to Panksepp via Marengo et al. 2021.

Consequence for later work: any simulation that relies on seven separate drives needs either a stated coupling or a stated correlation among them. Pedersen's Table 3 gives measured inter-scale correlations in one clinical sample (not general), and could seed a prior correlation structure for the five Panksepp-derived ones; nothing equivalent was found for the FSMI.

## 5. What exists vs what does not

**Exists**
- Large cross-cultural measurement for values (49 groups, N = 53,472) and a 42-country dataset for the seven social motives (N = 15,915) plus a four-country test of SDT needs.
- A published inter-scale correlation matrix for the ANPS, showing which Panksepp systems fail to separate in self-report.
- A meta-analytic bridge between Panksepp-style scales and the Big Five (21 samples).

**Does not exist (or was not found)**
- An independent confirmatory factor analysis of the FSMI seven-factor structure.
- An independent replication of Reiss's 16-factor structure (D118).
- One study that administers the lists to the same people and fits them jointly. The concordance in file 15 is a vocabulary map, and the measurement evidence here comes from separate samples per list. No joint measurement of FSMI, SDT, ANPS and Schwartz in one sample has been located; this is the empirical gap that would settle the minimal set.
- Any measurement evidence that the primitives are *drives* (state variables with dynamics) rather than trait dimensions. Every instrument above measures stable individual differences or satisfaction outcomes.

## 6. Operand/operator translation

- Operands: a drive-weight vector over the seven-item basis per agent, plus a state vector (satisfaction/frustration, SDT-style) per drive.
- Two kinds of coupling, to be kept separate: **trait covariance** (measured, e.g. Pedersen's table, FEAR-SADNESS .59) and **value compatibility** (Schwartz circle, signs). Different semantics; do not merge into one matrix.
- Needs are additive in well-being effects (Tay & Diener), so default to additive composition and make prepotency an optional gate, with the weight an editable setting.
