**Summary.**
1. The one operator TMT supplies, `mortality-reminder -> worldview-defence`, failed its largest and best-documented test: ML4 found Hedges' g of 0.07 to 0.13 with confidence intervals spanning zero (confirmatory analyses), against an original d of 1.34. A Bayesian multiverse found evidence against an effect in 29 of 33 model-averaged comparisons, and evidence for one in none that is better than 1.6 to 1.
2. That is one paradigm (pro/anti-US essay author ratings, US students, 2016-17), not the whole literature. The literature's meta-analyses are positive but are acknowledged to be inflated by publication bias; no one has produced a bias-corrected estimate for the worldview-defence hypothesis that is both credible and clearly non-zero. Treat the effect as **unestablished with a prior mass near zero**, not as refuted.
3. **Fear to affiliation does not need TMT.** Its support is Schachter, attachment theory and tend-and-befriend, which are independent of the mortality-salience route. The one direct test of the TMT version I found in this session (Treger et al. Studies 2-5) found that the mortality prime did not change measured fear or belongingness. So the TMT route to affiliation is empty and the other routes are unaffected.
4. Files 17, 25, 33 and 42 do not lean on TMT beyond what 17 allows. Two soft spots: 42's Meaning Maintenance Model entry (a "compensation operator" whose substitutability claim is the same kind of priming result), and a handful of factual slips in 17's TMT section, listed below.

## 1. The theory's operator, stated as the field states it

**Their vocabulary.** Mortality salience (MS: a reminder of one's own death), death-thought accessibility (DTA), cultural worldview, self-esteem, anxiety buffer, proximal and distal defence.

**Drives/operands.** One drive, fear of death (called "terror" or "anxiety"), and two buffers: faith in a cultural worldview and self-esteem earned by meeting its standards. From the 2015 chapter's abstract [from abstract, via search summary; chapter not read]: people manage the anxiety that awareness of mortality produces by maintaining (1) faith in the absolute validity of their cultural worldview and (2) self-esteem by living up to its standards of value.

**Operators / equations.** None. TMT is a verbal theory with three testable hypotheses [standard account, not read at source]:
- Mortality-salience hypothesis: reminding people of death increases defence of worldview and self-esteem.
- Anxiety-buffer hypothesis: strengthening a buffer reduces anxiety or defensiveness after a reminder.
- DTA hypothesis: threatening a buffer raises the accessibility of death-related thoughts.

The operator the algebra could take is therefore `defend(person, MS) : W_i -> W_i + delta`, with `delta` an unspecified positive function of the reminder and of buffer strength. The sources give no functional form; only a sign and a moderator list.

**Original anchor [read, via ML4].** Greenberg, Pyszczynski, Solomon, Simon & Breus (1994), *J. Pers. Soc. Psychol.* 67(4), 627-637, DOI 10.1037/0022-3514.67.4.627 (resolved). Study 1 as described by ML4: 59 US introductory psychology students in five conditions; the replication used the two conditions "own death salient" (write about the emotions thinking about your death arouses, and what will happen to your body as you die and once dead) and "TV salient" (parallel prompts about television). Outcome: ratings of the authors of a pro-American and an anti-American essay, composite = pro minus anti. ML4 gives the original effect as **d = 1.34**. An n of about 12 per cell producing d = 1.34 is exactly the profile of an inflated early estimate.

## 2. Many Labs 4 [read]

**Citations.** Klein, R. A., Cook, C. L., Ebersole, C. R., Vitiello, C., Nosek, B. A., et al. (38 authors per Crossref) (2022). Many Labs 4: Failure to replicate mortality salience effect with and without original author involvement. *Collabra: Psychology* 8(1), 35271. DOI 10.1525/collabra.35271 (resolved). Preprint v1, 11 Dec 2019, DOI 10.31234/osf.io/vef2c (listed in the OSF API).

**Design [read].** Labs randomly assigned to an Author Advised protocol (reviewed by Pyszczynski, Solomon and Greenberg; anti-American essay made more forceful; longer delay filler; relaxed-mood procedure) or an In-House protocol built from the 1994 paper alone. Data collected September 2016 to May 2017. Preregistration osf.io/4xx6w. Three participant exclusion sets (minimal; plus non-White or non-US-born removed; plus American-identity rating below 7 removed).

**Sample, and a discrepancy in 17.** 17 writes "21 labs, 2,220 participants". Both numbers are real but are the **recruited** totals: 21 labs, N = 2,220 (this is how Chatard et al. and the Haaf reanalysis describe it, and 2,211 remain after the minimal response exclusion in the reanalysis). The published abstract's **analysed** sample is **17 labs, N = 1,550 after exclusions** [read, Crossref abstract]; the preprint's Exclusion Set 1 gives n = 1,550 (699 Author Advised, 851 In-House), Set 2 n = 1,229, Set 3 n = 1,076. The 17-lab figure follows the preregistered rule of at least 60 participants per lab. 17 should quote 17 labs / 1,550 for the analysed sample and 21 / 2,220 for recruited.

**Results [read, preprint].**
- Random-effects meta-analysis, all sites: Set 1 g = 0.07, 95% CI [-0.03, 0.17], p = .187; Set 2 g = 0.09 [-0.03, 0.21]; Set 3 g = 0.09 [-0.04, 0.22].
- Heterogeneity not above chance: Set 1 Q(16) = 19.35, p = .251; Set 2 Q(16) = 21.72, p = .152; Set 3 Q(16) = 18.57, p = .292.
- Author-Advised sites only (exploratory): Set 1 g = 0.08 [-0.07, 0.23]; Set 2 g = 0.17 [-0.04, 0.37]; Set 3 g = 0.19 [-0.10, 0.49]; none significant.
- Five sites whose PIs reported "a lot" of TMT knowledge: Set 1 g = 0.02 [-0.18, 0.23].
- Power: the full sample had 95% power to detect d = 0.18 (Set 1), 0.21 (Set 2), 0.22 (Set 3).
- The authors' own statement: "This failure to replicate is quite precisely estimated, but it does not mean that the original effect was necessarily a false positive." They list history (the study ran around the 2016 US election), an effect smaller than prior studies indicated, and procedural subtlety as candidates, and note the single failure "does not overturn all of that prior work".

**What ML4 did and did not test.** It tested the final link only: prime to essay-author preference, under one delay structure, in US students. It did not measure fear, did not test affiliation, and did not test DTA. The In-House protocols mostly used no delay; the Author Advised ones used a longer delay, per both ML4 and Chatard et al. That difference is the single most plausible boundary condition and it showed no significant benefit.

## 3. The critique and the Bayesian reanalysis [read]

**Chatard, Hirschberger & Pyszczynski (2020)**, "A word of caution about Many Labs 4: if you fail to follow your preregistered plan, you may fail to find a real effect", OSF preprint, DOI 10.31234/osf.io/ejubn (from the OSF API listing). Not peer-reviewed as far as I found. Claims [read]: the preregistration set 40 per cell (80 per lab) as the power guideline, ML4 included 8 of 21 studies below it; effect was Hedges' g = 0.10 (SE 0.06) in the 13 studies at or above 40 per cell and g = -0.06 (SE 0.10) in the 8 below (Mann-Whitney U = 24.0, p = .046); after restricting to 40-per-cell, Author Advised labs, the effect is significant, "the data actually do replicate". They also object that the anti-US essay measure may not tap worldview in the post-election period.

**Haaf, Hoogeveen, Berkhout, Gronau & Wagenmakers (2020 preprint)**, "A Bayesian multiverse analysis of Many Labs 4: quantifying the evidence against mortality salience", DOI 10.31234/osf.io/cb9er [read in full]. Published as: Hoogeveen, S., Berkhout, S. W., Gronau, Q. F., Wagenmakers, E.-J. & Haaf, J. M. (2023). Improving statistical analysis in team science: the case of a Bayesian multiverse of Many Labs 4. *Advances in Methods and Practices in Psychological Science* 6(3). DOI 10.1177/25152459231182318 (resolved; author order and title from Crossref). **17 gives the preprint's author order with the 2023 venue and a title-less citation; use the Crossref order.** I read the 2020 preprint, not the published version; numbers below are the preprint's and the published ones may differ.
- Design: 18 exclusion constellations (3 participant x 3 study-size x 2 protocol), 11 unique after overlaps; Bayesian model-averaged meta-analysis with three effect priors (default Cauchy, an "Oosterwijk" t prior centred 0.35, a "Vohs" normal centred 0.3, all truncated to positive effects), plus hierarchical models.
- Result [read]: "29 of the 33 Bayes factors provide evidence against an overall mortality salience effect, ranging between 1.42-to-1 and 44.69-to-1"; the other four "provide only weak evidence for the presence of such an effect, ranging between 1.11-to-1 and 1.61-to-1", all from the two smallest datasets with the two optimistic priors. Overall effect estimates "between d = 0.03 and d = 0.18".
- Full-sample Bayes factors for the null (Klein's three analyses, default / Oosterwijk / Vohs): 12.60 / 44.69 / 16.64; 7.95 / 16.84 / 7.20; 4.18 / 4.01 / 2.49.
- Chatard-style subsets (7 Author Advised studies at or above 40 per cell, N = 700, 386, 229): default prior 3.82, 1.42, 1.45 for the null; optimistic priors 5.84 / 2.75, then 0.90 / 0.66, then 0.73 / 0.62. The authors call these "essentially inconclusive" and say the subsetting "thinned out the data so much" that it keeps about 10 per cent of the initial data.
- Their verdict on Chatard: subsetting cut power, and with no evidence of between-site heterogeneity there is no warrant to subset.

**Blunt reading.** Both camps are right about different things. ML4's full-sample null is robust to priors and exclusions (Haaf). The Chatard subset is not a replication; it is an uninformative sample (Bayes factors between 0.6 and 1.5), which shows the subset cannot discriminate rather than that the effect is there. The one thing ML4 cannot tell us is whether some narrower, more strictly controlled version of the manipulation produces a real effect; the Haaf estimates of d up to 0.18 do not rule out a small one, and the original-style studies (about 12 per cell) could not detect it.

## 4. Meta-analyses and other replications

**Burke, Martens & Faucher (2010).** *Pers. Soc. Psychol. Rev.* 14(2), 155-195, DOI 10.1177/1088868309352321 (resolved). [from abstract] 277 experiments from 164 articles; moderate effect, r = .35, across worldview- and self-esteem-related outcomes; larger for American samples (r = .37 per Treger et al.), college students, longer delay before the outcome, and people-related attitudes. Standing: the 2010 consensus estimate, **uncorrected for publication bias** and pre-dating ML4. For scale, r = .35 corresponds to d of about 0.75 (my conversion), against ML4's 0.07 to 0.13 and Schindler's bias-corrected range below.

**Schindler, Hilgard, Fritsche, Burke & Pfattheicher (2023).** "Do salient social norms moderate mortality salience effects? A (challenging) meta-analysis of terror management studies." *Pers. Soc. Psychol. Rev.* 27(2), 195-225, DOI 10.1177/10888683221107267 (resolved; preprint DOI 10.31234/osf.io/8y6me). [from abstract/fetch summary] 64 effect sizes; the hypothesised MS x norm interaction g = 0.34, 95% CI [0.26, 0.41]; bias-adjustment methods "suggested publication bias and/or flexible analytic choices" and produced smaller estimates, "in several cases no longer statistically significant" (corrected g from about -0.36 to 0.15). This is a meta-analysis of an interaction, not of the main effect, but the co-author Burke wrote the 2010 meta-analysis, so it is the nearest thing to the field correcting itself. This file is not cited in 17.

**Treger, Benau & Timko (2023).** "Not so terrifying after all? A set of failed replications of the mortality salience effects of Terror Management Theory." *PLOS ONE* 18(5), e0285267. DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0285267 (resolved). [read, partial] Five studies on five samples (MTurk, student, in-person), standard writing primes, delay task, essay-author evaluation, bail-for-prostitute measure. They also measured **fear and belongingness** as candidate mediators. Examples of what I read: Study 1 anti-US essay evaluation g = 0.01 [-0.30, 0.31]; Study 2 g = -0.10 [-0.48, 0.29] and MS-vs-control self-reported fear g = -0.26 [-0.64, 0.13] (the wrong sign for a fear route); Study 3 belongingness MS vs control g = 0.06 [-0.20, 0.31], p = .68, which led them to drop belongingness as a mediator. Study 5 added uncertainty and social-pain primes beside MS and TV (cells of about 21 to 26 in the essay analyses). Their random-effects "mini meta-analysis" over Studies 1-5 (N pooled = 1,255 across measures; 651 for the anti-US essay): the anti-US essay estimate z = 0.33, p = .738, tau-squared = .0112, I-squared = 56.5%. **Standing:** modest N each, unreviewed by anyone but PLOS reviewers, but it is the only source I read that tests the intermediate states.

**Arendsen, Brugman, van Koningsbruggen & Fransen (2025).** "Terror management theory in the consumer domain: a systematic review and meta-analysis on mortality salience driven consumer responses." *Int. J. Consumer Studies* 49(3), e70056. DOI 10.1111/ijcs.70056 (resolved). [read] 112 experiments in the review, 70 studies / 125 effect sizes in the meta-analysis, **g = 0.21, 95% CI [0.14, 0.28]**, heterogeneity large (Q(124) = 462.58, p < .001; 32% of variance between studies). Sub-categories: "I want more" g = 0.13 [0.01, 0.24]; "pro-social" g = 0.22 [0.13, 0.31]; "protect my culture" g = 0.29 [0.19, 0.40]. **Three cautions for how 17 uses it.** (a) The outcomes are consumer responses (brand and product attitudes, spending, donating), not worldview defence on the essay paradigm, so it does not rescue ML4's outcome. (b) The authors say outright that they did **not** run publication bias checks, because the studies lacked a uniform hypothesis, and they "recommend interpreting our effect estimates with caution, as they may be smaller than our meta-analysis indicates". (c) Their review found 47% of 114 outcomes with no main effect and some outcomes contradicting TMT. Its "pro-social" category (g = 0.22) is the only fear-adjacent-to-affiliation number in the set and is subject to the same caveat.

**Chen, Benjamin, Lai & Heine (2022).** "Managing the terror of publication bias: a comprehensive p-curve analysis of the terror management theory literature." PsyArXiv, DOI 10.31234/osf.io/kuhy6; a Research Square version also exists (DOI 10.21203/rs.3.rs-1254756/v1, 2023). Resolved by Crossref as **preprints**; I did not read them. 17 cites Chen via Arendsen as showing that published effects are inflated; Arendsen, in turn, describes it only as showing publication bias and questionable practices. Do not cite as a published result.

**Related, not read.** Steinman & Updegraff (2015), delay and death-thought accessibility meta-analysis, *Pers. Soc. Psychol. Bull.* 41(12), 1682-1696, DOI 10.1177/0146167215607843 (resolved; the DTA link of the chain, untested by ML4). Burke, Kosloff & Landau (2013), *Political Psychology* 34(2), 183-200, DOI 10.1111/pops.12005 (resolved; MS and political attitudes).

## 5. Fear to affiliation: which routes exist

Three routes, and only one is TMT.

**(a) TMT route: MS increases affiliation with in-group or close others.** Relevant tests found by title only (not read): Wisman & Koole (2003), "Hiding in the crowd: can mortality salience promote affiliation with others who oppose one's worldviews?", *JPSP* 84(3), 511-526, DOI 10.1037/0022-3514.84.3.511 (resolved; no abstract returned); Florian, Mikulincer & Hirschberger (2002), *JPSP* 82(4), 527-542, DOI 10.1037/0022-3514.82.4.527 (resolved; close-relationship commitment as a terror management mechanism). These sit in the same priming paradigm family as the failed replications and have not, to my knowledge from this session, been through a multi-lab test. **Direct counterevidence read:** Treger et al., MS did not change belongingness (g = 0.06) or self-reported fear. **Competing account:** Hohman & Hogg (2011), *Eur. J. Soc. Psychol.* 41(6), 751-760, DOI 10.1002/ejsp.818 (resolved; abstract read): Experiment 1 (n = 187), MS strengthened national identification only among people uncertain about an afterlife, which they read as uncertainty-identity theory (file 18) rather than death terror. That reframes the operator's input from "fear" to "uncertainty".

**(b) Schachter and stress-affiliation.** Schachter (1959), *The Psychology of Affiliation*, Stanford UP, book, not read; classic small-sample shock-anticipation studies. Gump & Kulik (1997), "Stress, affiliation, and emotional contagion", *JPSP* 72(2), 305-319, DOI 10.1037/0022-3514.72.2.305 (resolved; not read). 42 already marks Schachter "recalled" and Gump & Kulik "DOI not located"; the DOI is now resolved. 17's matrix tags Schachter [M, classic] (measurement) which is more confident than 42's "classic, small-sample"; use the more cautious tag.

**(c) Attachment and tend-and-befriend.** Bowlby (1969); Taylor et al. (2000), *Psychol. Rev.* 107(3), 411-429, DOI 10.1037/0033-295X.107.3.411. Independent of mortality salience. 33 records proximity-seeking under threat as among the best-replicated claims in developmental psychology [that is 33's own judgement; I did not recheck it].

**Verdict on the operator.** `fear -> affiliation` as a signed positive coupling is supported by (b) and (c) without TMT, and 17 already says so ("independent of the contested TMT route"). `death-fear -> worldview-defence` should not be an axiom. Keep it, if at all, as a parameter with a prior centred near zero and an upper bound of about d = 0.2, flagged contested.

## 6. A fear-to-risk-posture operator that is not TMT (seed: Lerner & Keltner, Lu et al.)

The map lists these seeds here; they belong to a different operator, `emotion -> risk judgement`.

[standard account / search summary] fearful people give pessimistic risk estimates and make risk-averse choices; angry people give optimistic estimates and risk-seeking choices (appraisal-tendency framework).
- Lu, S., Efendić, E. & Feldman, G. Associations of fear, anger, happiness, and hope with risk judgments: revisiting the appraisal-tendency framework with a replication and extensions registered report of Lerner and Keltner (2001). *JPSP* 131(4), e1-e18, DOI 10.1037/pspp0000586 (resolved; Crossref says 2026, the Maastricht repository says online December 2025). [from abstract, via search and fetch summaries; Stage 2 full text and the PCI-RR recommendation page were blocked] N about 780 (US online sample), a close replication of Experiments 1-3. ## 8. Operand/operator translation for the algebra

- **Operand.** A person with a worldview-commitment scalar `W_i`, a buffer pair (worldview faith, self-esteem), and a fear level `F_i`. TMT adds the buffer structure and a "death-fear" input; no source provides dynamics for any of these.
- **Operator `remind(a, i)`.** Nominally `F_i` up and `W_i` up after a delay. The measured value of the second effect in the best confirmatory test is g of about 0.07 to 0.13, CI crossing zero. If kept, encode as a soft parameter with prior mean near 0.1 and a flag, not as a law.
- **Operator `affiliate`.** `F_i -> belonging_i` positive, form unknown, supported via Schachter (small samples), attachment and tend-and-befriend; independent of TMT.
- **Substitution (`compensate`).** Distinct threats feeding one defence variable. Priming evidence only; untested at scale. Keep out of the base layer.
- **Moderators worth carrying as parameters if a defence operator is wanted:** delay between prime and outcome (strongest theoretical moderator, largest observed methodological difference in ML4, no significant benefit in ML4), culture (Burke: US largest; Yen & Cheng per Treger: Asian samples smaller; both second-hand), and buffer strength (self-esteem; secondary).

## 9. What exists versus what does not

**Exists.** A 30-year literature of verbal hypotheses with a sign and a moderator list. A 2010 meta-analysis showing a moderate effect before replication-era corrections. One large, preregistered, multi-lab test of the central worldview-defence paradigm (ML4: 17 analysed labs, N = 1,550) with a precise near-zero estimate, a defensible but disputed critique, and a reanalysis showing that the critique's subset is uninformative. At least one other independent multi-study failure (Treger et al.) that includes the mediators. Evidence that fear raises affiliation without any mortality prime.

**Does not exist.** A bias-corrected, credible, non-zero estimate of the mortality-salience-to-worldview-defence effect. Any functional form: no equation relating the strength of the reminder, delay, buffer strength and the defence response. A multi-lab test of DTA or of the affiliation version of the MS hypothesis. A theorem or a derivable consequence beyond sign.
