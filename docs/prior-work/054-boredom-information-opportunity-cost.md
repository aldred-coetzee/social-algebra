**Summary.** Boredom has the most developed *informational* formalisation of any single drive-like state so far surveyed, and it is a small one: one family of models (Geana/Wilson/Cohen/Daw, then Agrawal et al.) treats boredom as the felt value of exploration, `Q + VOI`, with `VOI = Gain x Need`, and one rival family (Westgate & Wilson's MAC model) treats it as a conjunction of two independent deficits, attention and meaning, with no equation but a clean two-factor result. A third (Wolff et al.) defines it as near-zero reward prediction error. All are single-agent. None has a social operand. The key empirical weaknesses are: boredom induction and measurement are heterogeneous; the arousal question is unresolved (meta-analytic r = -0.13 in correlational studies, a null against neutral controls); and the "bored people shock themselves" paradigm has an unresolved alternative explanation (curiosity about the shock). For the algebra, boredom is the best candidate for a **regulator operand whose level is a gap between current information rate and an alternative's value**, and the only drive here whose *output* is explicitly undirected (a push, not a pull).

## 1. Eastwood, Frischen, Fenske & Smilek (2012): the definition

**Citation.** Eastwood, J. D., Frischen, A., Fenske, M. J., & Smilek, D. (2012). The unengaged mind: Defining boredom in terms of attention. *Perspectives on Psychological Science*, 7(5), 482-495. DOI 10.1177/1745691612456044 (Crossref-confirmed). Full text not read; definition confirmed `[read]` as quoted in Bench & Lench 2013 (p. 482) and Danckert et al. 2018, and as paraphrased from the abstract page `[from abstract/standard account]`.

**Their vocabulary.** Boredom; attention; engagement; "wanting to but being unable to engage in satisfying activity"; internal/external information; attribution to the environment.

**Definition `[read]` (second-hand quotation).** "the aversive experience of wanting, but being unable, to engage in satisfying activity". `[from abstract/standard account]` expanded: boredom is the aversive state that occurs when we (a) cannot successfully engage attention with the internal or external information needed for satisfying activity, (b) are focused on that failure, and (c) attribute the cause to the environment.

**Drives/operands.** A *desire to be engaged* is presupposed; the state is the frustration of that desire. Danckert et al. 2018 [read] gloss it as Tolstoy's "a desire for desires". Arousal is explicitly not part of the definition: boredom may be high-arousal (restless) or low-arousal (lethargic) and may oscillate `[from abstract/standard account, and Danckert et al. 2018 read]`.

**Operators / equations.** None.

**Theorems or derived predictions.** None formal. Qualitative: boredom differs from apathy because it includes motivation to change the situation [read, Danckert 2018].

**Empirical standing.** The definition is the common baseline of the field (Bench & Lench, Danckert, Westgate all adopt or build on it). Its defining component "attention failure" is itself contested: Wolff et al. argue disengagement is the function, not a failure (section 7).

**Operand/operator translation.** Operand: a person with a standing *engagement-demand* (a want to be engaged). The boredom state is a predicate on (person, activity): engagement-demand unmet. No operator yet.

## 2. Bench & Lench (2013) and (2019): boredom as a goal-switch signal that seeks novelty

**Citation 1.** Bench, S. W., & Lench, H. C. (2013). On the function of boredom. *Behavioral Sciences*, 3(3), 459-472. DOI 10.3390/bs3030459 (Crossref-confirmed). Read via PMC page summary (second-hand), PMC4217586.

**Citation 2.** Bench, S. W., & Lench, H. C. (2019). Boredom as a seeking state: Boredom prompts the pursuit of novel (even negative) experiences. *Emotion*, 19(2), 242-254. DOI 10.1037/emo0000433 (Crossref-confirmed). Abstract read `[read]` via Europe PMC.

**Their vocabulary.** Function of emotion; goal abandonment; novelty seeking; seeking state; "push" versus "pull".

**Drives/operands.** Boredom is a discrete emotion with a function: it "motivates pursuit of new goals when the previous goal is no longer beneficial" [read, abstract paraphrase]. It arises "as the intensity of an emotion fades" when a situation "is no longer stimulating". It does not specify the valence of the new goal [read]. It is distinguished from apathy (complete failure or helplessness, no motivation).

**Operators / equations.** None. The 2013 paper is a review with "limited evidence": the paper reports no effect sizes, p-values or sample sizes of its own; a cited meta-analysis count of 510 articles on happiness/sadness/anxiety/anger versus 12 experimental boredom studies [read, via summary].

**Theorems or derived predictions.** Proposed effects across behaviour, cognition, experience, physiology: novelty/risk preference, attention to novel stimuli, mind-wandering, negative affect, raised autonomic arousal [read, via summary]. Stated as untested.

**Empirical standing.** 2019 abstract `[read]`: three studies. Study 1: higher boredom after a neutral task predicted choosing negative experiences. Study 2: high-boredom condition desired novelty and so chose novel negative experiences. Study 3: participants bored by positive stimuli chose a novel experience more negative; those bored by negative stimuli chose a more positive one. Sample sizes not read. The key claim is that boredom pushes toward *difference from the boring experience*, not toward the negative per se. The 2013 evidence base (London & Monello clock manipulation, London 1972 skin conductance, Larsen & Zarate 1991) is old and thin by the authors' own admission.

**Operand/operator translation.** Operator: `Seek(difference)`, an undirected operator on the person's activity, triggered by boredom, whose output is "an activity differing from the current one". Boredom is an *emotion decay detector*: level rises as the response to the current stimulus decays.

## 3. Danckert, Mugon, Struk & Eastwood (2018) and Danckert & Eastwood (2020): self-regulatory signal and trait as failure to respond

**Citation.** Danckert, J., Mugon, J., Struk, A., & Eastwood, J. (2018). Boredom: What is it good for? In H. C. Lench (Ed.), *The Function of Emotions* (pp. 93-119). Springer. DOI 10.1007/978-3-319-77619-4_6 (Crossref-confirmed). Full text `[read]`. Book: Danckert, J., & Eastwood, J. D. (2020). *Out of My Skull: The Psychology of Boredom*. Harvard University Press. **Not read; cited only via the 2018 chapter and Wolff et al. (they cite p. 171 and chapter 2 "A Goldilocks World").** No DOI verified.

**Their vocabulary.** Self-regulatory signal; engagement; "desire for desires"; push; regulatory non-fit; boredom proneness as chronic failure to respond; directional fractionation; DMN, salience network, insula.

**Drives/operands.** "As a drive state, boredom is crucial for ... the 'push' to engage in something different" [read]. Boredom "represents a kind of self-regulatory failure in which the bored individual knows they want something to engage with but fails to see a viable avenue for goal pursuit". Explicit analogy to pain: "the function of pain is not to cause us to feel hurt ... Pain, like boredom, is a signal to the organism that a behavioral response is required" [read]. Two-stage: escape the sensation; articulate and pursue a goal that engages.

**Operators / equations.** None. One statement is nearly an operator: trait boredom *is* "a chronic failure to respond successfully to the self-regulatory signal of state boredom" [read]. Boredom proneness is therefore a property of the **response operator**, not of the signal.

**Theorems or derived predictions [read].**
- Arousal tracks *prospect of successful engagement* (control): first low, rising as the person tries to engage, then falling again as discouragement sets in. Evidence: Struk et al. 2015 rock-paper-scissors, in which always-winning participants reported the most boredom; always-losing participants showed initial arousal that dissipated; those told the opponent was exploitable reported less boredom than those told it was random (the mere prospect of control warded off boredom). No statistics in the chapter.
- Physiology: Merrifield & Danckert 2014 found HR up, SCL down, cortisol up in a video boredom induction compared to sadness. Jang et al. 2015 "partially replicated" decreased SCL.
- Neural: boredom upregulates DMN; insula anticorrelated with DMN when bored but not at rest. Dal Mas & Wittmann 2017: higher willingness to pay for music after boredom, associated with insula activity.
- Trait structure: two-factor BPS structure disappears when items are reworded; the short BPS (Struk et al. 2017) is one-factor.

**Empirical standing.** Small-sample lab studies; the chapter itself marks Danckert et al. "under consideration" results. The arousal debate is now tested meta-analytically (section 8). The "no subtypes" position conflicts with MAC's two types (section 4).

**Operand/operator translation.** Operand: person with (state boredom level, response-competence parameter). Boredom proneness = a low response-competence parameter. This separates a drive's *signal* from its *effector*, a distinction the other drive models in 16 mostly lack.

## 4. Westgate & Wilson (2018): the MAC model (meaning and attentional components)

**Citation.** Westgate, E. C., & Wilson, T. D. (2018). Boring thoughts and bored minds: The MAC model of boredom and cognitive engagement. *Psychological Review*, 125(5), 689-713. DOI 10.1037/rev0000097 (Crossref-confirmed). Full model text `[read]` from Westgate (2018), PhD dissertation, University of Virginia, "Boredom is interesting", DOI 10.18130/V37659F3F (via the repository page; Studies 1 and 2 and the model are stated there to be the published ones; Study 3 and the general discussion are not in the journal paper). The journal article itself was not read; abstract confirmed via PubMed.

**Their vocabulary.** Attentional component ("can I focus?"), meaning component ("do I want to?"), attentional fit, understimulation, overstimulation, low-level and high-level engagement, attentional boredom, meaningless boredom, mixed states, interest versus enjoyment.

**Definition `[read]`.** "boredom is an affective indicator of unsuccessful attentional engagement in valued goal-congruent activity". Boredom is experienced "when people feel either unable or unwilling to continue with their current activity".

**Drives/operands.** Two *independent* predicates on (person, activity):
- Attention `A`: fit between task demand `D` and available mental resources `R`. `A` succeeds iff `D = R` (both low or both high). Failure is `D < R` (understimulation) or `D > R` (overstimulation).
- Meaning `M`: congruence of the activity with currently activated *valued* goals ("current concerns", Klinger).

**Operators / equations [read].** No equation. The model is a 4 x 2 table (Table 2 in the dissertation) mapping (attention state x meaning state) to a profile and a predicted response:

| | Low meaning | High meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Understimulation (D < R) | Meaningless + attentional boredom -> seek interesting activity | Attentional boredom -> increase demand |
| Low-level engagement (D low, R low) | Meaningless boredom -> seek enjoyable activity | Enjoyment (low boredom) |
| High-level engagement (D high, R high) | Meaningless boredom -> seek interesting activity | Interest (low boredom) |
| Overstimulation (D > R) | Meaningless + attentional boredom -> seek enjoyable activity | Attentional boredom -> increase resources |

Cell labels transcribed from the dissertation. The "increase demand" / "increase resources" outputs are *regulation of the fit*; the "seek interesting/enjoyable" outputs are *switch of activity*.

**Theorems or derived predictions, with evidence `[read]` (dissertation numbers).**
- Independence of attention and meaning: an internal meta-analysis of 14 studies (N = 1,364; undergraduates and mTurk; paradigms: thinking alone, simulated air-traffic control, letter crossing) gave, for boredom regressed on difficulty-concentrating and meaningfulness (9-point scales): attention b = .34 [.28, .40]; meaning b = -.35 [-.41, -.28]; interaction b = .004 [-.03, .04], p = .79; heterogeneity nonsignificant. Most of the 14 studies are unpublished pilot data, flagged as such in the dissertation's Table 3.
- Study 1 (N = 226): manipulating attention and meaning each produced boredom; the attention manipulation (not meaning) predicted Inattention; meaning (not attention) predicted Disengagement, Dysphoric and Agitated affect and slowed time perception. Meaning-failure boredom is the high-arousal kind.
- Study 2: a **curvilinear** relation between difficulty and boredom after controlling for frustration (quadratic term b = .14 (.05), t(123) = 2.95, p = .004, d = .53), with frustration linear in difficulty (b = .72 (.09), d = 1.39); boredom and frustration were not strongly correlated (r = .12). This contradicts pure understimulation accounts, which predict a linear relation.
- Study 3 (dissertation only): different boredom types change subsequent preference between interesting and enjoyable activities.

**Empirical standing.** The strongest *structural* result on boredom: two largely independent factors, no interaction, a U-shape in difficulty. Caveats: self-report only; undergraduate and mTurk samples; the internal meta-analysis pools mostly unpublished studies by one lab with common 9-point items (so shared-method bias is possible); the quadratic term is correlational (Study 2). I found no independent replication in this session. The MAC model's "meaning" component is the least operationalised: "valued goals" are whatever is currently salient.

**Operand/operator translation.** Operand: a pair (person, activity). Boredom level `B = f(attention-misfit, meaning-misfit)`, with the two additive on the regression evidence (b of about 0.34 and -0.35, no interaction), i.e. a **linear-additive combination of two independent deficits**. This is a direct, evidence-backed *combination law* for the algebra, unlike Hull's product or Atkinson's.

## 5. Kurzban et al. (2013) and Geana, Wilson, Daw & Cohen (2015, 2016): opportunity cost

**Citations.**
- Kurzban, R., Duckworth, A., Kable, J. W., & Myers, J. (2013). An opportunity cost model of subjective effort and task performance. *Behavioral and Brain Sciences*, 36(6), 661-679 (target article; 661-726 with commentary). DOI 10.1017/S0140525X12003196 (Crossref-confirmed). Partly `[read]` via PMC3856320 (first ~100k of 276k characters; boredom passages and the model's formal statement only).
- Geana, A. (2015). *Information sampling, learning and exploration*. PhD dissertation, Princeton University (advisor J. D. Cohen). Chapter 4 `[read]`. Geana, A., Wilson, R., Daw, N., & Cohen, J. D. (2016). Boredom, information-seeking and exploration. *Proceedings of the 38th Annual Meeting of the Cognitive Science Society*. No DOI. Abstract-level only for the proceedings.

**Their vocabulary.** Opportunity cost; subjective effort; value of information; local patch versus global structure; prediction error; explore-exploit; decision horizon.

**Kurzban `[read]`, partial.** Effort and boredom are the felt outputs of cost-benefit computations about what a mental process could otherwise be doing. The model's only displayed formalism in the passage read is a two-task division-of-processing condition. With `beta` the fraction of utility retained when two tasks are done simultaneously and `U(a_i, a_j)` the utility of doing `a_i` and `a_j`: `U(a1, a2) = beta x (U(a1,a1) + U(a2,a2))`; relative utility of the next-best action `RU(a2) = U(a2,a2) / U(a1,a1)`; dividing is favoured when `beta + beta x RU(a2) > 1`. (The fetch tool's text was garbled; subscripts reconstructed, so check against the published article before building on it.) Boredom is mentioned only in passing (vigilance tasks, "qualia such as frustration, boredom, flow"); the boredom formalisation is *attributed to* Kurzban by Westgate and Geana ("Boredom is triggered when the cost of continuing the current task outweighs the benefit, particularly in comparison with currently available alternative tasks", Westgate's gloss, read) and is not itself formal in the target article as far as I read.

**Geana's Chapter 4 `[read]`.** Five experiments (the dissertation describes them as "five experiments"; I read experiments 4.1 to 4.3 and the model section).
- Exp 4.1 (number-prediction task, three informativeness conditions): boredom rating depends on how much a trial can teach; boredom also increases with time on task in all conditions (within a game and across games), with no interaction, which Geana attributes to task-level novelty loss, fatigue, or "an increased perceived opportunity cost of staying with the task".
- Exp 4.3: participants told they would next watch a YouTube video rated the bandit task as more boring than those told they would count words in an article, with the same task and duration. Boredom correlated with exploration of the ambiguous arm only in the long decision horizon (horizon 6: F(1,33) = 6.01, p = .02; horizon 1: F(1,33) = 1.88, p = .17), for both fitted decision noise and fitted information bonus. Geana notes this is "the first report of a correlation between a quantitative operationalization of boredom (as self-reported ratings) and a measure of exploration". Sample about 35 (from the F df).

**Normative model `[read]` (Geana Chapter 4.4).** An agent can stay in a local patch (exploit) or switch to a new patch (explore). Patch `i` has hidden mean `mu_i` and SD `sigma_i`; the agent predicts each step and is rewarded
`r_{t,i} = rho - PE_t` (eq. 1), `PE_t = Pr_t - N(mu_i, sigma_i)` (eq. 2),
with `rho` the maximum reward. Running mean update with learning rate `1/n`: `mu_{i,t} = mu_{i,t-1} + (1/n) PE_t` (eq. 4); variance estimate updated analogously (eq. 5), so a new datum's informativeness falls like `1/n`. On entering a new patch the agent's prior is the current global mean estimate `M`, so information in the current patch has value as better priors for future patches. The relative value of one more sample in the current patch versus switching is eq. 6, a function of `n` (position in patch), `N` (number of patches), the local variance `sigma^2` and global variance `S^2`; solved by dynamic programming. The typeset form of eq. 6 was garbled in extraction, so I do not transcribe it. Result: an information-sensitive agent should leave early patches earlier than a reward-maximiser, because early patches inform the global mean; this parallels Charnov's marginal value theorem but with information in place of reward. Dissertation-stage, unpublished as a journal article, as far as I could find.

**Empirical standing.** Small samples; self-report boredom; the horizon interaction is a single result at p = .02. The proceedings paper is a conference paper. Geana's own later fit (the model's boredom signal itself) is not tested against data in the pages read.

**Operand/operator translation.** Operand: an agent at a patch with state `(n, N, sigma^2, S^2)`. The boredom signal is the *relative value of switching*, positive when the marginal local information gain is below the global one. Operator: `Switch`, triggered when that relative value changes sign.

## 6. Agrawal, Mattar, Cohen & Daw (2022): the formal VOI model of boredom

**Citation.** Agrawal, M., Mattar, M. G., Cohen, J. D., & Daw, N. D. (2022). The temporal dynamics of opportunity costs: A normative account of cognitive fatigue and boredom. *Psychological Review*, 129(3), 564-585. DOI 10.1037/rev0000309 (Crossref-confirmed). Preprint: bioRxiv 10.1101/2020.09.08.287276 (v2, 2021). **Equations and text `[read]` from the preprint PDF**; the published version may differ in numbering.

**Their vocabulary.** Opportunity cost; value of information (VOI); Gain; Need; successor representation `M`; expected value of backup with cost (EVB_C); fatigue versus boredom; flow.

**Drives/operands.** Two distinct signals:
- *Fatigue* = value of offline computation (replay) relative to acting (Part 1, EVB_C).
- *Boredom* = value of **exploration in the external world**, i.e. the VOI of alternative tasks. "Boredom indexes the opportunity cost of foregoing exploration by continued engagement in the current task" [read].

**Operators / equations `[read]` (preprint eqs. 9-13).**
- Task utility: `Q_VOI = Q + VOI`, "the defined reward Q as well as the value of information VOI". A bored agent disengages to pursue a task of higher `Q_VOI`.
- `VOI(s, a_k) = Gain(s, a_k) x Need(s, a_k)` (9)
- `Gain(s, a_k) = E[ sum_{a in A} (pi_new(a|s) - pi_old(a|s_k)) q(s, a) ]` (10), with the expectation over the prior distribution of the obtained reward `r` (11): the expected improvement in policy value from learning `r`.
- `Need(s, a_k) = sum_{i=1..inf} gamma^i delta_{s_{t+i}, s_k}` (12) `= gamma x sum_{s'} P(s, a_k, s') M(s', s)` (13), the expected discounted number of future visits to the state, via the successor representation `M` (Dayan 1993).
- Footnote 5 `[read]`: these are "a partly myopic approximation to VOI"; they do not count the value of further learning at later steps.

**Theorems or derived predictions `[read]`.**
- Worked bandit example: arm 1 is a random walk starting at 0 (+/-1 with probability 1/2), arm 2 is constant at 1. If the agent keeps choosing the constant arm, the uncertainty of arm 1, hence its `VOI`, grows, until it is worth choosing arm 1 even though its expected reward is lower. This is offered as the explanation of why repetitive tasks become boring: the alternative's VOI rises while the status quo's VOI is zero.
- Easy task (no uncertainty to resolve) and impossible task (random rewards that teach nothing) both have low VOI, hence low `Q_VOI` and boredom: the U-shape, matching Geana's Certain and Random conditions and Westgate's Study 2. Flow is high reward and high VOI at once.
- Longer horizons raise `Need`, hence VOI, hence exploration; the model reproduces Wilson et al. (2014) bandit horizon effects (a different Wilson paper from the shock study; see the Unconfirmed section for the name collision). Simulated in a softmax choice, not fitted to human data.
- A T-maze with shuffling rewards: replay propagates VOI and produces alternating explore and replay. Speculative link to mind-wandering, flagged as speculative.
- Testable prediction: in Blain et al.'s multi-hour N-back study, a hard task should produce *fatigue* (impulsive choice rises) and an easy task *boredom* (pupillometry or subsequent bandit exploration), because boredom is greater in the easy condition. Untested by the authors.

**Empirical standing.** Strictly a theory with simulations; the authors note that the self-report scales may not index the computational signals, and that Milyavskaya et al. (2019) found a boredom induction raised self-reported *fatigue* more than an effort manipulation, which the model does not predict. No human data fitted in the parts read. Peer-reviewed (Psychological Review) and the best formal statement available.

**Operand/operator translation.** Operand: an agent with a policy over states and a posterior over values. Boredom level `= Q_VOI(best alternative) - Q_VOI(current)`. Operator: `Disengage` fires when this difference is positive. `Gain` is a function of the agent's uncertainty, `Need` of its horizon, so **horizon and uncertainty are the two dials**. This is the closest thing in the whole survey to a *drive equation* for boredom, and it gives boredom a clean dimension: it is a derived quantity of the agent's information state, not an independent store.

## 7. Wolff, Radtke & Martarelli (2024): inadequate function utilisation, near-zero reward prediction error

**Citation.** Wolff, W., Radtke, V. C., & Martarelli, C. S. (2024). Same same but different: What is boredom actually? In M. Bieleke, W. Wolff, & C. S. Martarelli (Eds.), *The Routledge International Handbook of Boredom* (pp. 5-29). Routledge. DOI 10.4324/9781003271536-3 (Crossref-confirmed). `[read]` from the revised Nov 2022 preprint.

**Their vocabulary.** Function utilisation; Goldilocks zone; preferences, capabilities, perceived energy (three nested ellipses); reward prediction error; undirected exploration; entropy; boredom proneness as self-regulatory failure.

**Drives/operands.** "boredom is a state where an organism's functions are not adequately utilized" (Schopenhauer-derived). Adequate utilisation = the activity lies inside the intersection of preferences, capabilities and perceived energy (a Venn diagram, not an equation). Mechanism: "We propose that boredom occurs when positive (but also negative) reward prediction errors are minimized" [read], i.e. a near-zero reward prediction error over an individual-specific time window.

**Operators / equations.** None stated. Definitions only: a reward prediction error is "the difference in the reward utility that is predicted for a state and the actual reward utility". Boredom drives **undirected** exploration, "increasing entropy" via mental or motor motion.

**Theorems or derived predictions [read].**
- Low-entropy (predictable) environments produce boredom, backed by Seiler et al. 2022 [abstract read]: subjects avoid the more monotonous of two stimulus sequences; the avoidance correlates with self-reported boredom; replicated across lab and online samples; "specific for the induction of boredom rather than curiosity"; the entropy of the experienced sequence predicts choice.
- Computational: Yu, Chang & Kanai 2018 (Frontiers in Neurorobotics 12, 88, DOI 10.3389/fnbot.2018.00088, Crossref-confirmed, abstract `[read]`): agents with a boredom ("devaluation") term outperformed other curious or exploratory agent variants on forward-model-learning benchmarks. An engineering result, not human data.
- Arousal is not part of the definition, because any state of near-zero prediction error can be high or low arousal.
- Attentional disengagement is "not the bug of boredom, but one of its core features" (Box 1). This reverses Eastwood et al.'s "attention failure" framing.

**Empirical standing.** Conceptual, with indirect support: one ERP study (Martin & Potts 2011) used as post hoc support, which the authors admit was "not designed to test the proposition". The reward-prediction-error definition is untested as such. It is also close to circular in practice: "everything is perfectly predictable" is how the environment is measured, and boredom is how the state is reported.

**Operand/operator translation.** Operand: an agent with a running estimate of reward prediction error `delta`. Boredom = a low windowed `|delta|` (and, symmetrically, low positive `delta`), with the window a trait parameter. Operator: undirected `Explore`. It matches Agrawal et al. in outcome (disengage, explore) but differs in mechanism: Agrawal's boredom is a *forward-looking value* (VOI of alternatives), Wolff's is a *backward-looking statistic* (recent `delta`). They make different predictions when an attractive alternative is announced but unavailable, as in Geana's Exp 4.3 (the YouTube-video manipulation), which favours the forward-looking account.

## 8. The arousal question and its meta-analysis

**Citation.** Stempfer, L., Stoll, S. E. M., Fries, J., Pekrun, R., & Goetz, T. (2025). A systematic review and multilevel meta-analysis of the relationship between boredom and arousal. *Communications Psychology*, 3, article 182. DOI 10.1038/s44271-025-00358-8 (Crossref-confirmed). Abstract and introduction `[read]` (pre-registered on OSF, 4 April 2024; data and code at osf.io/45zuh).

**Result `[read]`.** 214 effect sizes from 72 samples, N = 6,570. Correlational evidence (75 effect sizes): more intense boredom related to *lower* arousal, r = -.13, 95% CI [-.22, -.05]. Experimental evidence (122 effect sizes): boredom had lower arousal than control conditions, d = -0.40, 95% CI [-0.59, -0.22]. Significant heterogeneity. The relation was **not significant** when boredom measures included items denoting mixed or high arousal, when arousal was measured by heart rate variability, or when induced boredom was contrasted with a *neutral* control (waiting, doing nothing). The authors judged risk of publication bias minor to none.

**Implications for the algebra.** Boredom is mildly low-arousal on average, but the effect is small, depends on the instrument, and vanishes against a neutral baseline. That fits the Danckert (a dynamic, trajectory-dependent arousal) and Wolff (arousal not definitional) positions and contradicts Berlyne-type optimal-arousal accounts in which boredom is a pure arousal deficit with a rise to follow. **Do not make arousal a state variable of boredom.** The Wundt-curve account (file 17) maps better to a VOI account: U-shaped in difficulty, but the U is in information, not arousal.

## 9. Boredom with curiosity

**Seiler, J. P.-H., & Dan, O. (2024).** Boredom and curiosity: the hunger and the appetite for information. *Frontiers in Psychology*, 15, 1514348. DOI 10.3389/fpsyg.2024.1514348 (Crossref-confirmed). `[read]`, first 100k of 178k characters. A narrative review; no equations, effect sizes or own data.

- Boredom is "a non-specific drive" like hunger (any information relieves it, no prior estimate of quality needed); curiosity is "a directed drive" like appetite (needs a rough estimate of the information wanted; pulls toward a specific source). The first promotes exploration and the second exploitation; "independent, but coalesce to optimize behavior" [read].
- Trait boredom and trait curiosity correlate in a range of roughly -0.3 to +0.5 across studies (Eren & Coskun 2016 negative; Hunter et al. 2016 and Sung et al. 2020 positive) [read, via review; not checked at source]. This wide range is itself a finding: the two are not opposites.
- Curiosity peaks at intermediate uncertainty (Kang et al. 2009, others, cited) [read, via review].
- Wolff et al. add: boredom motivates attentional *disengagement*, curiosity attentional engagement with something else. Boredom outperformed curiosity in the Yu et al. agent benchmark [read].
- Berlyne's "diversive curiosity" (unspecific stimulation seeking to escape boredom) is the older name for what Seiler & Dan call boredom's push [read via Wolff et al.].

**Hunger/appetite template.** This is the one place a *drive pair* is stated as a two-component structure comparable to the Keramati-Gutkin drive (file 47): a non-specific deficit signal (boredom, "hunger") and a specific goal-directed signal (curiosity, "appetite"). It is a metaphor in the source, not a formalism.

**Operand/operator translation.** Operands: `Boredom` (non-directed, push) and `Curiosity` (directed, pull), distinct state variables on the same information-state substrate. Operator composition: boredom's output (`Explore_undirected`) is the input that makes a curiosity target visible (and, when found, `Curiosity` takes over). Not a derived result; a structural hypothesis.

## 10. Boredom with sensation seeking

**Sources.** Wolff et al. (above) `[read]`; Mercer-Lynn, Flora, Fahlman & Eastwood (2011), The measurement of boredom: differences between existing self-report scales, *Assessment*, 20(5), 585-596, DOI 10.1177/1073191111408229 (Crossref-confirmed; abstract `[read]`). Note Crossref lists the year as 2011 (online) with volume 20(5) belonging to 2013.

**Facts `[read]`.**
- Zuckerman's Sensation Seeking Scale contains a *boredom susceptibility* subscale, so any boredom-sensation seeking correlation is partly built into the instrument. Wolff et al.: "the empirical association between both concepts is a modest one, primarily driven by the boredom subscale of the sensation seeking scale"; some studies find no correlation, a few negative.
- The two main trait boredom scales do not measure the same thing (Mercer-Lynn 2011: Study 1 N = 837, Study 2 N = 233, Study 3 N = 137): higher Boredom Proneness Scale (BPS) scores go with neuroticism, experiential avoidance, attentional and nonplanning impulsivity, anxiety, depression, dysphoria, emotional eating; higher Boredom Susceptibility Scale (ZBS) scores go with motor impulsivity, reward sensitivity, gambling, alcohol use, and *lower* neuroticism and punishment sensitivity. So **BPS is the "failure to launch" boredom (anxious, avoidant) and ZBS is the "seeking" boredom (reward-driven, disinhibited)**.
- Conceptually: sensation seeking is a directed search for "novel, varied, complex, and intense sensations"; boredom is not directed (Wolff et al.). Both may involve faster habituation (boredom prone people habituate faster) and Zuckerman's under-arousal (high sensation seekers) [read, via Wolff et al.; the habituation claim not checked at source].
- Stempfer et al. 2025's introduction [read] notes that boredom susceptibility was "later integrated into sensation-seeking theory as a trait characterized by restlessness (i.e., a high-arousal state) under low stimulation" and that this is part of the contested arousal literature.
- Interpersonal: the Bieleke et al. overview (Artak & Pfattheicher chapter) states that aggression-based entertainment is a remedy for boredom, more likely with low self-control, impulsiveness and sensation seeking [read, chapter summary; AI-assisted summaries curated by the authors, as the volume says].

**Standing.** Weak and confounded by the instrument. The right algebraic reading is two parameters: a *threshold* for boredom onset (how fast information value is exhausted; habituation rate) and a *response style* (seek versus avoid; the BPS-ZBS split).

## 11. Shocks and negative seeking: the contested paradigm

- Wilson, T. D., Reinhard, D. A., Westgate, E. C., Gilbert, D. T., Ellerbeck, N., Hahn, C., Brown, C. L., & Shaked, A. (2014). Just think: The challenges of the disengaged mind. *Science*, 345(6192), 75-77. DOI 10.1126/science.1250830 (Crossref-confirmed; abstract `[read]`). 11 studies; participants typically did not enjoy 6-15 minutes alone thinking; many preferred to shock themselves ("two-thirds of men and about a quarter of women" in the search summary `[from abstract/standard account]`, not checked at source).
- Havermans, R. C., Vancleef, L., Kalamatianos, A., & Nederkoorn, C. (2015). Eating and inflicting pain out of boredom. *Appetite*, 85, 52-57 (page range from memory; DOI 10.1016/j.appet.2014.11.007 Crossref-confirmed). Abstract `[read]`: N = 30, within-subject, 1 h of a neutral documentary versus 1 h of one repeated clip; the boring condition increased chocolate eating and shock self-administration; conclusion: "the drive to escape monotony", not a desire for rewarding stimulation.
- Nederkoorn, C., Vancleef, L., Wilkenhoner, A., Claes, L., & Havermans, R. C. (2016). Self-inflicted pain out of boredom. *Psychiatry Research*, 237, 127-132. DOI 10.1016/j.psychres.2016.01.063 (Crossref-confirmed). Abstract `[read]`: N = 69; monotonous, sad or neutral film; boredom produced more and stronger self-shocks than neutral or sad (sadness had no effect), more so with NSSI history.
- Eder et al. (2022), Motivations underlying self-infliction of pain during thinking for pleasure. *Scientific Reports*, DOI 10.1038/s41598-022-14775-w (Crossref/Europe PMC-confirmed; abstract `[read]`). Four experiments, N = 254: a large proportion again shocked themselves, but strong shocks were chosen even when a no-shock or mild-shock option existed, and even when pleasant thoughts were assisted; they conclude the shocks were attractive because participants "were curious about the shocks".
- Buttrick et al. (2019), JPSP 117, e71-e83, DOI 10.1037/pspp0000198 (Crossref-confirmed): a 12-site, 11-country replication of the thinking-versus-doing *enjoyment* result, N = 2,557 [from search summary, not read]. This replicated the enjoyment contrast, not the shock result.

**Reading.** The boredom to negative-seeking link has three converging lab results (Bench & Lench 2019; Havermans 2015; Nederkoorn 2016) and one serious alternative (curiosity about novelty, Eder 2022). Under the VOI model they are the same thing: a low-information state makes *any* source of information (including pain) valuable, which is just what "boredom is undirected" means. **Curiosity about the shock and boredom are not exclusive in the model: both are VOI of an unexplored option.** This is a case where the two accounts differ in labelling more than in prediction.

## 12. Operand and operator inventory for the algebra

Built only from what was read above.

**Operands.**
1. `B(person, activity)`: boredom level, a derived predicate, not a stored quantity, in the Agrawal model: `B = Q_VOI(alternative*) - Q_VOI(current)`, where `Q_VOI = Q + VOI`.
2. Inputs to the VOI: the person's *uncertainty* about alternatives (Gain), their *horizon* (Need), and the current task's *residual learnable information*. These are observable-ish.
3. MAC predicates `A(D, R)` (attentional fit) and `M(activity, valued goals)` (meaning). `B` increases with attention misfit and meaning misfit, additively, independently (b about 0.34 and -0.35, interaction zero).
4. Person parameters: **response competence** (Danckert; trait boredom as a failed response) and **habituation rate** (Wolff; the window over which prediction error is averaged), and the BPS/ZBS **style**.

**Operators.**
1. `Disengage` (from current activity) / `Switch`: fires when `B > 0`.
2. `Explore_undirected`: output of boredom. Its target set is *anything differing from current* (Bench & Lench 2019).
3. `Regulate_fit` (MAC): raise demand when understimulated, raise resources when overstimulated.
4. `Curiosity_target` (directed): appears after `Explore_undirected` finds something; the appetite component.

**Laws we can state, with strength of evidence.**
- (L1) Boredom rises with time on a fixed task. Strong across studies (London & Monello 1974; Geana Exp 4.1; Danckert), mechanism contested (novelty loss, fatigue, opportunity cost).
- (L2) Boredom is U-shaped in task difficulty after controlling for frustration. One study (Westgate Study 2), consistent with the VOI model; needs independent replication.
- (L3) Boredom is higher when an attractive alternative is *announced*, even if unavailable. One experiment (Geana 4.3, N about 35); the key support for the opportunity-cost reading versus a pure habituation reading.
- (L4) Exploration correlates with boredom, but only with long horizons. One effect, p = .02.
- (L5) Boredom prompts seeking of difference, including negative. Three labs.
- (L6) Arousal: r = -.13 correlational, d = -0.40 experimental, unstable across instruments. Do not use as a state variable.

**Social operands: what exists.** Essentially nothing formal. Items touching social structure that were read: a boredom induction raised ingroup favouring and out-group harshness, and nostalgia use (Van Tilburg & Igou 2011 PSPB 37, 1679-1691, DOI 10.1177/0146167211418530; Van Tilburg, Igou & Sedikides 2013 *Emotion* 13, 450-461, DOI 10.1037/a0030442; both Crossref-confirmed, and known here only via Bench & Lench's description "no sample sizes or statistics reported"); aggression as a boredom remedy; a homelessness conceptual model with a boredom to social-exclusion vicious cycle (Bieleke et al. overview, summary only). The group-level analogue of "the value of an alternative" is an obvious extension (a group with an announced better option becomes bored with its present activity) but I found no source that states it.

## What exists vs what does not

**Exists.**
- A formal, peer-reviewed boredom model: `Q_VOI = Q + VOI`, `VOI = Gain x Need` (Agrawal et al. 2022), plus a normative patch-switch model with equations (Geana 2015).
- A two-factor additive descriptive model with regression coefficients and a U-shape result (Westgate & Wilson 2018).
- A convergent consensus on *function*: an undirected push to change activity (Bench & Lench, Danckert, Wolff, Agrawal, Seiler & Dan).
- A meta-analytic verdict on arousal (Stempfer 2025).
- A functioning computational result that boredom helps learning (Yu et al. 2018) and a human choice study of entropy (Seiler et al. 2022).

**Does not exist.**
- Any boredom model with a **social** operand: no group-level boredom, no boredom of a pair, no transmission of boredom between people, no theory of how a group's shared activity decays in value.
- A **single** accepted measurement: BPS and ZBS measure different things; state boredom is induced in incompatible ways.
- An independent replication of the MAC regressions, or of the Geana horizon and alternative-task effects.
- A direct test of Agrawal's VOI model against human boredom ratings.
- A discriminating experiment between "recent low prediction error" (Wolff) and "high value of announced alternatives" (Agrawal; Geana 4.3), other than the one small study.
- A numerical **coupling** between boredom and curiosity or sensation seeking: only qualitative (hunger/appetite) and a correlation range of -0.3 to +0.5.
