## Summary

1. **Status and balance are not shown to generate signs; both are reduced forms of one learned model.** That model is logistic regression on counts of the 16 directed signed two-step path types plus seven degree features. Balance (product of undirected signs) is accurate at the resolution it is written at and its learned coefficients transfer across the three sites. Status (sign folded into direction, additive on an order) is accurate once edges are canonicalised and does not transfer. As hand-set deterministic predictors, weak balance beats status on Epinions (0.823 v 0.808) and Slashdot (0.870 v 0.717), status beats weak balance on Wikipedia (0.668 v 0.608), and strict balance is last on two of three.
2. **At whole-network scale status is found and balance is not, but only by heuristic search.** A status ordering satisfies 79-85% of edges against 63-66% for a degree-preserving null. The two-faction balance search does no better than its baselines and, counting directed edges as Table 1 does, falls below the trivial one-cluster value on Epinions and Wikipedia. Facchetti, Iacono & Altafini (PNAS 2011, doi:10.1073/pnas.1109521108; read on PMC in the validation pass) bound the optimum on the same three networks, made undirected: their best partitions leave 7.2%, 14.8% and 14.2% of edges unbalanced, far below sign-shuffled nulls, with lower bounds within 1% (Epinions, Wikipedia) and 5% (Slashdot). That implies "no balance" is at least partly a property of LHK's heuristic. It is a near-optimum with bounds, not an exact one, and Facchetti et al. do not discuss LHK's test.
3. **Most of the lift above chance is person-level counts, not triads.** In my recount the source's own majority sign alone gets 0.79 (balanced, well-embedded edges) on Epinions and Slashdot (the paper's own Figure 3: about 0.83 and 0.81) against 0.934 for the full learned model; the triad features add about 2 to 6 points over the seven degree features. On Wikipedia the target's received majority is the best cheap predictor for weakly embedded edges.
4. **The headline accuracies (0.934, 0.935, 0.802) cover a minority of edges, and a smaller minority of negative edges.** They are for edges with at least 25 common neighbours: 25.1% of Epinions edges, 4.1% of Slashdot edges, 26.5% of Wikipedia edges, but only 14.6%, 1.1% and 14.4% of the negative edges [computed]. The paper does not say so. Over all edges the learned model is about 0.87, 0.80 and 0.83 [fig].
5. **Guha et al. (2004) already states the rivalry.** Section 3.3 sets "multiplicative" distrust propagation (the enemy of my enemy is my friend) against "additive" (do not respect someone not respected by someone you do not respect). Only the multiplicative form was run. The best scheme treats distrust as a gate applied once at the end, not as a sign that chains. The largest effect among its 81 schemes is the rounding step, which reads the source's own labelled ties, not the propagation law.
6. **Everything is online, cumulative, and 1999-2009.** Epinions to 12 Aug 2003, Wikipedia votes closed 17 Sep 2004 to 6 Jan 2008, Slashdot crawled Feb 2009. Neither paper intervenes on anything. Magnitudes are era- and medium-bound; the forms (the 16-type table, the two theorems, the operator words) are not.

## 1. Leskovec, Huttenlocher & Kleinberg 2010 (WWW) at source [read in full]

**Citation.** Leskovec, J., Huttenlocher, D. & Kleinberg, J. (2010). Predicting positive and negative links in online social networks. *Proc. 19th International World Wide Web Conference (WWW '10)*, Raleigh NC, 641-650. ACM. doi:10.1145/1772690.1772756. Preprint arXiv:1003.2429 (physics.soc-ph, cross-listed cs.AI and cs.CY). Funding NSF IIS-0705774, an IBM Faculty Award, a Microsoft Research gift and a Yahoo! Research alliance grant.

**Their vocabulary.** Signed directed link; sign prediction (the edge sign prediction problem: hide s(u,v), infer it from the rest of the network); embeddedness C(u,v) (common neighbours in the undirected sense); degree features; triad types [F|B][F|B][p|m][p|m]; a "theory of triad types" f; structural balance; weak balance (Davis); status; status-consistent; the models All23, 16Triads, Degree, BalanceDet, WeakBalDet, StatusDet, BalanceLrn, StatusLrn; heuristic predictors Balance, StatDif, OutSign, InSign, Always+; "satisfied" edges; permuted-signs and rewired-edges baselines.

**Drives/operands.** No drive, motive or affect is named or measured. The operands are users and signed directed ties s(x,y) in {+1, -1, 0}. The only person-level quantities are signed degree counts. The paper speaks of "principles that drive the formation of signed links" (abstract), meaning the two structural theories, not motives.

### 1.2 Operators and equations, exactly [read]

All restated in my notation.

- **Signs.** s(x,y) = +1 for a positive edge x to y, -1 for negative, 0 for no edge. s-bar(x,y) is the direction-free sign: +1 if there is a positive edge in one direction and a positive edge or no edge in the other, -1 analogously for negative, 0 otherwise (including opposite-signed edges in both directions, "rare in our datasets").
- **Seven degree features** for the edge (u,v): d+_in(v), d-_in(v), d+_out(u), d-_out(u), C(u,v), total out-degree of u, total in-degree of v. (The printed text lists "d+_in(u)" where d+_in(v) is meant.) **Sixteen triad features:** for each common neighbour w of u and v, the type is the direction of the u-w edge (F: u to w; B: w to u), the direction of the w-v edge (F: w to v; B: v to w), and the two signs (p, m); the feature vector is the count of each of the 16 types. The type FBmp is u negative to w and v positive to w.
- **Model.** P(+|x) = 1 / (1 + exp(-(b0 + sum_i b_i x_i))), logistic regression, 10-fold cross-validation, accuracy and AUC. Two evaluation sets: the full network (about 80% positive) and a **balanced** set (for every negative edge a random positive edge is sampled, so guessing scores 50%). Results are reported on the balanced set; edges are restricted to **minimum embeddedness 25** "if not stated otherwise", with Em = 0, 10, 25 shown in Figures 1-2. The set-up is described as leave-one-out ("all but one of the edge signs visible"), following Guha et al.
- **A theory of triad types** is a map f from the 16 types to {+1, -1, 0} (evidence for a positive edge, for a negative edge, or none).
- **Balance** [read]: f_balance(tau) = s-bar(u,w) s-bar(v,w); (u,v) takes the sign that gives the triangle on u, v, w an odd number of positive signs, regardless of direction. Balance "has generally been applied as a theory of undirected graphs"; ignoring direction is standard (their ref. Wasserman and Faust).
- **Weak balance** (Davis; the paper cites Davis 1963, AJS 68:444-462): agrees with balance on pp, pm, mp and makes no prediction on mm.
- **Status** [read]: a positive edge (x,y) means x regards y as higher in status than herself; a negative edge means lower. "Assuming that all participants in the system agree on this status ordering", flipping an edge's direction flips its sign. To get f_status(tau): flip the edges so they run u to w and w to v (flipping signs as the directions flip), then f_status(tau) = sign(s(u,w) + s(w,v)). It makes no prediction when the two signs cancel, so it predicts on 8 of the 16 types and balance on all 16.
- **Reduced models.** Balance, undirected: 4 triad types (pp, pm, mp, mm), counts of undirected signed triads. Status, canonicalised: reverse each negative edge into a positive edge pointing the other way, which leaves only positive edges and 4 types from the two directions. Positive-only subgraph: delete negative edges, 4 types FFpp, FBpp, BFpp, BBpp.
- **Heuristic predictors** (section 3.4): the balance heuristic picks the sign that puts (u,v) in more balance-consistent triads; the status heuristic sigma(x) = d+_in(x) + d-_out(x) - d-_in(x) - d+_out(x), predict + iff sigma(u) <= sigma(v); the out-degree heuristic predicts + iff d+_out(u) >= d-_out(u); the in-degree heuristic predicts + iff d+_in(v) >= d-_in(v).
- **Theorem 1** (Cartwright-Harary, as the paper states it): in a signed undirected *complete* graph in which every triangle has an odd number of positive edges, the nodes split into two sets (one may be empty), positive inside, negative across. **Theorem 2** (the paper's own, with proof): in a signed *tournament* in which every three nodes are status-consistent, the nodes can be ordered v1..vn so that every positive edge (vi,vj) has i < j and every negative edge has i > j. The proof reverses each negative edge into a positive edge, notes that no three-node subgraph of the result is a directed cycle, applies the known theorem that a tournament with no directed triangle is acyclic, and takes a topological order. The paper says both generalise to non-complete graphs through arbitrary cycles and does not prove that.
- **Global objectives.** Balance: partition the nodes into two sets to maximise (positive edges inside a set) + (negative edges across); heuristic: random start, repeatedly flip a random node if that raises the objective, many restarts, "within a few hundred" of the best over runs. Status: after reversing negative edges, find an ordering maximising edges pointing earlier-to-later (maximum acyclic subgraph; a random order gets half); heuristic: random start, repeatedly swap a random pair if that raises the objective, take the best of several runs. **Baselines:** permuted signs (same graph, signs shuffled) and rewired edges (random graph keeping each node's numbers of incoming and outgoing positive and negative edges).
- **Positive-edge presence** (section 5): for a positive edge (a,b) pick a non-adjacent pair (c,d) with the same embeddedness; classify edge versus non-edge from either the 16 signed triad counts or the four positive-path counts.

### 1.4 What the numbers cover: embeddedness and the headline [computed]

The paper reports "relatively few edges" with embeddedness well above 25 but not how many. From the public files (edges with self-loops dropped; Wikipedia rebuilt as above, 103,689 edges after 58 self-votes):

| Site | Edges with C >= 10 | C >= 25 | Negative edges with C >= 25 | Share negative: all edges, then C >= 25 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Epinions (840,799 edges) | 42.6% | 25.1% (211,000) | 14.6% (18,109) | 14.7%, then 8.6% |
| Slashdot (549,202) | 9.4% | 4.1% (22,637) | 1.1% (1,364) | 22.6%, then 6.0% |
| Wikipedia (103,689) | 56.3% | 26.5% (27,502) | 14.4% (3,157) | 21.2%, then 11.5% |

Consequences [derived]: (i) The balanced Slashdot evaluation at Em = 25 holds about 2 x 1,364 edges, a small sample for a 23-feature model under 10-fold cross-validation, with no interval reported. (ii) **Negative ties are systematically less embedded than positive ones**, so the triad evidence is thinnest for exactly the edges a conflict model cares about. (iii) The 6.58% error against Guha et al.'s 14.7% compares different populations: Guha's figure is a balanced set of 996 edges drawn at random from all edges (no embeddedness filter), LHK's is edges with C >= 25. At Em = 0, All23 on Epinions is about 0.87 [fig], an error of about 13%, against Guha's 0.853: a gain of about 2 points, inside the noise of Guha's 996-edge test (standard error about 1.1 points, section 2.4).

**Recomputed simple heuristics [computed]** (balanced accuracy = mean of the accuracy on positive edges and on negative edges, over all edges meeting the threshold, so no sampling noise; counts exclude the target edge; ties go to +; reciprocated opposite-signed pairs get s-bar = 0). This is a partial recount of Figure 3, not a replication of the logistic models. ### 1.5 Group size and composition: what the paper says

- **Size.** No analysis by group or network size. The sizes span 7,118 to 119,217 nodes with no size-dependence reported. The only size-like variable is embeddedness, the number of common neighbours of a pair, which acts as the amount of triadic evidence: it helps the learned triad model on Epinions and Slashdot, does not on Wikipedia, and the simple triad heuristics peak and then fall. The authors' own scale statement is that triad rules are local approximations and that the global signatures differ (section 1.3).
- **Composition and definition.** The sign means different things on each site (trust in reviews, liking comments, a vote on fitness). The paper's evidence that this matters: balance aligns with the learned model better on Epinions and Slashdot than on Wikipedia, and the best simple predictor flips from OutSign to InSign on Wikipedia. Half of Wikipedia's votes come from existing admins, a composition split the paper does not analyse.

- Text says 841,000 edges, Table 1 841,200; Table 1's Epinions counts differ from the public file (section 1.1).
- The introduction says boosts on "all two of the three datasets"; Table 9 supports two of three.

### 1.7 Theorems, derived predictions, empirical standing

- **Theorems.** The two in section 1.2. Both are about complete graphs (Theorem 1) or tournaments (Theorem 2); none of the three networks is either, and the extension to arbitrary cycles is asserted, not proved. Theorem 1 is restated for complete graphs only; Cartwright and Harary's own statement is not so restricted (096; [sa] here, because the 1956 paper was not read for this file). Theorem 2 is the paper's own, with proof, and its only new result of that kind.
- **Derived predictions.** Each theory maps the 16 types to a sign (Table 2); balance predicts two opposed factions globally, status a total order. The paper derives a status explanation after the fact for the negative BB coefficient on the positive-only subgraph.
- **Empirical standing (blunt).** One paper, three same-era online sites, one preprint read (identical to the proceedings text; section 0). I found no independent replication on post-2009 data in this session. A search in the validation pass finds later work that reuses the task or the features on later data, none of it read here: West, Paskov, Leskovec & Potts (2014), TACL 2: 297-310, doi:10.1162/tacl_a_00184, combining the signed network with vote text on Wikipedia RfA votes from 2003 to May 2013; Colbaugh, Glass & Johnson (2012), arXiv:1301.0006, described in search results as reusing LHK's two feature sets; Sotiropoulos, Byers, Pratikakis & Tsourakakis (2019), TwitterMancer, arXiv:1904.11119, on Twitter interactions. Whether any of them replicates LHK's numbers is unconfirmed. Chiang et al. (2014) re-ran sign prediction on the same three networks and found low-rank global methods better than local triad features. Their Epinions and Slashdot match the public files, but their Wikipedia network (7,065 nodes, 103,561 edges) matches neither public version. Kumar et al. (2016) tested a related weight task on six networks: three from platforms that post-date the LHK data and three from Epinions and Wikipedia again (section 5). No interval or significance test is reported anywhere; the headline numbers apply to well-embedded edges; the Epinions file analysed is not the public one.

### 1.8 Operand/operator translation for the algebra

Operands: persons as nodes; signed directed ties s(x,y) in {+,-,0} (a relation layer); node-level aggregates (signed in- and out-degree; sigma(x), an estimate of status computed from the same signs it is used to predict). Operators: (i) composition of oriented signed steps along two-step paths, 16 types = {F,B} x {p,m} per step; (ii) balance = product of undirected signs on a path; (iii) status = oriented sum on an order; (iv) the learned operator = log-odds sum of b_tau times path-type counts plus person-level terms. Gap: the status "order" is not independent of the ties, and no operator produces a sign from anything other than other signs and degrees.

## 2. Guha, Kumar, Raghavan & Tomkins 2004 at source [read in full]

**Citation.** Guha, R., Kumar, R., Raghavan, P. & Tomkins, A. (2004). Propagation of trust and distrust. *Proc. 13th International Conference on World Wide Web (WWW '04)*, New York, 403-412. ACM. doi:10.1145/988672.988727. Authors at IBM Almaden Research Center and Verity Inc. The acknowledgements thank Epinions for the data, and the first author thanks Naval Ravikant, Benchmark Capital and August Capital "for helping create Epinions": a disclosed tie between an author and the data source. Copyright on the PDF: the author(s).

**Their vocabulary.** Web of trust; trust matrix T and distrust matrix D (entries in [0,1], in {0,1} in the experiments); belief matrix B (T, or T - D); atomic propagation; direct propagation, co-citation, transpose trust, trust coupling; combined atomic propagation matrix C_{B,alpha}; k-step propagation matrix P(k); final beliefs F; eigenvalue propagation (EIG) and weighted linear combinations (WLC); trust only, one-step distrust, propagated distrust; global, local and majority rounding; multiplicative and additive distrust propagation; prediction error epsilon (all trial edges) and epsilon_S (the balanced subset S).

**Drives/operands.** No drive is named. The operands are users and two non-negative matrices of trust and distrust. Trust is framed as an epistemic and economic relation: whom to believe about quality, a defence against disinformation and gaming. Distrust is defined by what it does to inference: under the one-step model "when a user distrusts somebody, they also discount all judgments made by that person". That is a gate on another person's influence on one's beliefs, not a reversed influence.

### 2.1 Operators and equations, exactly [read]

- **Atomic propagations** (Table 2), as operator matrices on a belief matrix B: direct propagation B; co-citation B^T B; transpose trust B^T; trust coupling B B^T. Conclusions are read from B times the operator: direct gives B^2 (the path i to j to k); co-citation gives B B^T B (the path i2 to j2, back to i1, forward to j1: if i1 trusts j1 and j2 and i2 trusts j2, i2 should trust j1). The paper gives these two derivations; the same reading for the other two is mine.
- **Combined operator.** C_{B,alpha} = alpha_1 B + alpha_2 B^T B + alpha_3 B^T + alpha_4 B B^T.
- **Three models for B and the k-step matrix** (section 3.2.1). Trust only: B = T, P(k) = C^k. One-step distrust: B = T, P(k) = C^k (T - D), so distrust enters only in the last multiplication. Propagated distrust: B = T - D, P(k) = C^k, so signed values multiply along every walk.
- **Final beliefs.** EIG: F = P(K). WLC: F = sum over k = 1..K of gamma^k P(k), with gamma "a constant (that is smaller than the largest eigenvalue of C)" as printed; the experiments use gamma = 0.5 and 0.9, K = 20, and the paper calls gamma a discount on lengthy propagation.
- **Rounding** (section 3.2.3). Global: i trusts j iff F_ij is within the top tau fraction of row i of F, tau set from the overall trust-to-distrust ratio of the input. Local: the same with tau set from i's own ratio. Majority: take the set J of users i has labelled (trust or distrust), order J together with the unlabelled j by F_ij', and predict j's label as the majority label in the smallest neighbourhood of j in that order where a majority is defined. Local and majority rounding are "i-centric".
- **Multiplicative and additive distrust** (section 3.3). All the definitions above, applied to the plus/minus values themselves, implement the multiplicative notion. The additive notion is obtained by replacing each non-zero entry m_ij by exp(m_ij) before iterating (zero entries stay zero).
- **Experimental grid.** Distrust model (3) x iteration (EIG, WLC gamma 0.5, WLC gamma 0.9) x rounding (3) x basis vector alpha (e1 = direct only, e2 = co-citation only, e* = (0.4, 0.4, 0.1, 0.1)) = 81 schemes, K = 20 for Table 3.

**Theorems.** None. Remarks only: under multiplicative propagation a directed cycle whose values have a negative product makes iterated propagation lead "a user to distrust himself", and iteration can generate a belief that "negates and overwhelms" the expressed one; the principal eigenvector of a signed trust matrix "need no longer be real", which blocks the Markov-chain route to a global score. The authors deliberately seek a pairwise trust from any node to any other, not a global one.

### 2.2 The transitivity-of-distrust passage: the rivalry stated in 2004 [read]

Section 3.3 gives two readings of "i distrusts j, who distrusts k". Either j's value model is so misaligned with i's that anyone j distrusts is more likely to be trusted by i ("the enemy of your enemy is your friend"), or j's judgments are simply inferior to i's own and j has concluded the same about k, in which case i should strongly distrust k ("don't respect someone not respected by someone you don't respect"). The former is **multiplicative**, the latter **additive**. This is balance's product rule against a status-style order: if distrust means "ranks lower", then two successive distrusts compose to a distrust. The paper does not use the words balance (as a theory), status, Heider or Cartwright (checked by search of the text; the only hit is "balanced subset"), and its reference list has none of them. LHK (2010, section 3.2) say the status theory is "implicit in the work of Guha et al.", which this passage supports. **Only the multiplicative form was run** (the three distrust models above all use matrix products of plus/minus values); the exp transform is stated and never evaluated.

### 2.3 Data, era, medium [read]

Epinions: users write reviews of consumer goods, media, colleges and holiday spots, rate other authors' reviews, and may declare trust or distrust of another user. Trust information is used twice by the site: to choose which items a user sees in a category and which reviews of an item. Reviewers are paid royalties by how often their reviews are read, which produced many efforts to game the system, and distrust "was introduced about six months after the initial launch, in part to deal with this problem". So the distrust relation in this data was introduced as a counter-measure in an incentive system; it is not a pure sentiment.

Data: 131,829 nodes and 841,372 edges, each trust or distrust; 85.29% trust (trust = +1.0, distrust = -1.0). In- and out-degree distributions are power laws of exponent about -1.7 ("quite different" from the web's usual -2.0 or below); a strongly connected component of 41,441 nodes (the next has 15), 39,888 nodes pointing into it and 30,823 pointed to by it; an undirected giant component of 119,130 nodes. The period is not stated in the paper. LHK date their slightly different 841,200-edge Epinions network 1999 to 12 Aug 2003, and the public 841,372-edge file whose counts equal Guha's is credited by SNAP to LHK's CHI 2010 paper (2010a). **Medium:** online review site, ties used to rank content. **Scale:** relation scale inside a crowd-size population. **Consent:** observational, no intervention; the web of trust is itself an exposure-steering mechanism of the site, but the authors do not manipulate it. **Size and composition:** no analysis by either. A recount of the public file [computed]: 95,039 sources and 840,799 non-loop edges, a mean of 8.8 out-edges per active source. That mean hides a skew (validation recount [computed]). The median source has one out-edge (55% have exactly one), but trial edges are drawn at random over edges, so a trial edge's source has a median of 74 other labelled ties (mean 176). Only 6% of edges come from a source with no other tie. The abstract's "a small number of expressed trusts/distrust per individual allows us to predict" therefore describes the typical user, not the typical test case. The number of known edges is not varied in any experiment: one edge is hidden at a time.

### 2.4 Results [read] and how to read Table 3 [derived]

**Protocol.** 3,250 randomly masked edges, each scored by each of 81 schemes (263K computations, by Lanczos-style matrix-vector products). epsilon is the error over the trial edges; the naive always-trust predictor scores 15%. Because of the 85/15 split, the largest balanced subset S is used as well: all 498 distrust edges in the trial set plus 498 random trust edges (996 edges), naive error 50%, error epsilon_S.

**Headline.** Errors "as low as 6.4%" on the whole trial set and "as low as 14.7%" on S, both for one-step distrust with EIG and alpha = (0.4, 0.4, 0.1, 0.1), majority rounding. Co-citation alone ("quite surprising") does well although simple transitivity does not apply to it; the combined e* is the best overall. One-step distrust is the best of the three distrust models under EIG in all nine (rounding x alpha) cases. Under WLC distrust "is clearly helpful" but one-step or propagated may win. Direct propagation (alpha = e1) is "the only situation in which distrust actually hurts". Majority rounding beats local, which beats global, "in all cases"; that phrase covers the two best settings compared in Figure 6. Across all 27 cells of Table 3, local beats global everywhere on epsilon_S, but majority beats local in only 24. It ties at WLC gamma 0.5, e1, one-step, and loses at WLC gamma 0.5 and 0.9, e1, propagated (0.295 against 0.199, and 0.227 against 0.211) [derived in the validation pass]. The paper calls the rounding step "quite critical". Table 4 (iterations K = 1-7, a different sample of 1,000 trials, caption "EIG with gamma = 0.9", though EIG has no gamma in section 3.2.2): for trust-only direct propagation epsilon_S falls from 0.300 at K = 1 to 0.153-0.156 at K = 4-7; for one-step and propagated distrust with e* it stays at 0.197-0.209 and 0.184-0.209 throughout. The paper's reason: the shortest path between most test pairs has length two.

**Reading Table 3 [derived from the printed values; the 27 x 3 x 2 grid is not reproduced here].**
- Trust-only with global rounding is the constant "trust" predictor: epsilon = 0.153 = 498/3250 and epsilon_S = 0.500 in all nine cells. Trust-only cannot say anything about distrust except through rounding.
- Range of epsilon_S over the 27 (iteration x alpha x model) cells: global rounding 0.221-0.500 (median 0.284); local 0.199-0.430 (median 0.238); majority 0.147-0.295 (median 0.177). At the best propagation setting (EIG, e*, one-step) the three roundings give 0.253, 0.220, 0.147. **Rounding moves the result by 7 to 11 points at the best propagation setting; under majority rounding the three distrust models differ by under 2 points in median (0.174, 0.171, 0.187), with a worst case of 0.295.**
- Majority rounding reads the labels of the source's own other ties, distrust labels included, in **every** scheme including "trust only". So "trust only with majority rounding" is not a no-distrust baseline. Its best epsilon_S is 0.152 (WLC gamma 0.9, e1), against 0.147 for the overall best: **five edges of 996**, at a standard error of about 1.1 points (sqrt(0.147 x 0.853 / 996)). The standard error of epsilon at 0.064 on 3,250 edges is about 0.4 points.
- Under majority rounding one-step distrust beats trust-only in 4 of the 9 (iteration, alpha) cells and loses in 5 (three of the four wins are EIG; WLC gamma 0.5 loses in all three, by 1.3 to 4.2 points); propagated distrust beats it in 1 (by 0.003), ties in 1 and loses in 7, four of them by under one point and one by 13 points (WLC 0.5, e1). Under local rounding one-step distrust wins in 9 of 9 and propagated in 8 of 9. So distrust helps clearly where rounding does not already use the source's own labels, and by a margin comparable to noise where it does.
- The best scheme's implied error on trust edges is about 2.7% and on distrust edges about 27% [derived by solving epsilon = (2,752 e_trust + 498 e_distrust)/3,250 and epsilon_S = (e_trust + e_distrust)/2 with the printed 0.064 and 0.147, assuming S is an unbiased subsample]: about three in four distrust edges are caught.
- The 81 schemes were all scored on the same 3,250 edges and the best is reported with no held-out set and no interval; selection of the best of 81 on the test set inflates it a little. Table 4's sample (about 1,000 trials, so S of about 306 edges, standard error about 2 points) is too small to resolve differences of 0.01.

### 2.5 Empirical standing

First large empirical study of a deployed web of trust on this question, on data supplied by the site. I found no replication as a propagation study in the sources opened; the follow-up that mattered (LHK 2010) re-used the task and data and replaced propagation by local features. A search in the validation pass finds later trust/distrust prediction work in this line, for example DuBois, Golbeck & Srinivasan (2011), "Predicting trust and distrust in social networks", IEEE PASSAT/SocialCom, 418-424, doi:10.1109/PASSAT/SocialCom.2011.56, which Kumar et al. 2016 cite. It was not read, so whether it re-runs Guha's 81-scheme comparison is unconfirmed. Single dataset, single era (to 2003), one site whose "distrust" was a late, incentive-driven addition. No temporal hold-out: random edge masking in a cumulative snapshot. The concluding claim that distrust, rounding and similar choices "have significant effects" is supported for rounding and, by a smaller and noisier margin, for distrust.

### 2.6 Operand/operator translation for the algebra

Operands: persons; two relations T and D (or one signed relation T - D). Operators: the four words in B and its converse (direct, co-citation, transpose, coupling) as a relation algebra on a single relation; a linear combination of them (C); powers or discounted sums of C as K-step walk counts; one-step distrust as a **gate** (distrust blocks, and does not chain); rounding as a decision operator that compares a score against the source's own labelled ties. For F = C^K with alpha = e1, F is the count of K-step walks along trust edges, the familiar transitive trust. The signed multiplicative form composes signs by product along walks, so it is balance's operator. The additive form maps each non-zero entry m to exp(m) (trust e, distrust 1/e, both positive), so a walk's weight is the exponential of the *sum* of its signs. That is a status-like operator in which distrust is a weak positive and never flips a sign [derived from the section 3.3 definition]. The gate reading is the one the Epinions comparison favours (one-step beats propagated distrust in most cells), by margins that are small against the noise under majority rounding; it matches v0.1's separation of a sign input from gates.

### 3.2 Which generator wins, and where

- **Hand-set rules.** Weak balance wins on Epinions and Slashdot, status on Wikipedia; strict balance is last on Epinions and Wikipedia and in the middle on Slashdot. The one site where status beats balance is the one with public, consequential votes, which is where the authors conjecture status matters most; on Wikipedia all three hand-set rules are within 17 points of chance (0.578-0.668 at Em >= 25) and below the target's majority received sign at low embeddedness (0.706 at C >= 0 [computed]).
- **Learned reductions.** The balance reduction beats the status reduction on all three sites, by 7.1, 13.6 and 4.0 points [derived], and only the balance reduction transfers between sites. So the transportable local information is the undirected signed-neighbour counts; the status-type information is site-bound.
- **Global scale.** The reverse: a status ordering is detected, a two-faction balance partition is not (section 1.3), under a heuristic search.
- **Neither is shown to generate the sign.** Both are re-expressions of one 16-type table with a weight per type, fitted to the same signs they predict. The authors say this themselves: each theory is "essentially accurate at the granularity in which [it is] most naturally formulated", and the full model goes beyond both.

### 3.3 What carries the prediction

1. **Person-level counts.** The seven degree features alone reach 0.886 on Epinions (Em >= 25) and the source's majority sign alone 0.789 [computed]. The authors' own heuristics show OutSign beating InSign on Epinions and Slashdot, and InSign beating OutSign on Wikipedia at low embeddedness. Chiang et al. (section 5 below) say the same of the degree features: "a node that is predisposed to make positive relationships, biases the classifier".
2. **Triad type counts.** On top of the degree features they add about 2 to 6 points (Epinions 3.3 at Em = 0 and 4.9 at 25; Slashdot 1.9 and 5.8; Wikipedia 2.1 and 2.7 [derived from Figure 1 by eye and Table 6]). The weight sits on forward chains (FF) and two mixed-sign types (FBmp, BFpm).
3. **Embeddedness itself**: more common neighbours means more triadic evidence on Epinions and Slashdot, none on Wikipedia.
4. **In Guha**: the rounding step (the source's own labelled ties, ordered by the propagated score), then the choice to use distrust once rather than chaining it; the basis vector and iteration count matter less.

### 3.4 Person-level structure in the sign data [computed from the public files]

- **Who gives and who receives negative ties.** Epinions: 19.4% of sources (those with an out-edge) have ever issued a negative tie; 37.7% of targets have received one; among sources with at least two ties, 73.3% are all-one-sign (71.3% all-positive, 2.0% all-negative). Slashdot: 33.2% of sources, 41.7% of targets; 58.7% of sources with two or more ties are all-one-sign.
- **Concentration is mostly activity.** The top 1% of Epinions sources by out-degree produce 32.3% of all edges and 49.9% of negative edges (negative share inside them 22.7% against 14.7% overall); the 1,711 sources with out-degree 100 or more issue 43% of edges with a negative share of 20.7%, against 8-12% for sources of lower degree. In Slashdot the negative share rises steadily with source activity, from 9.6% (one out-edge) to 29.2% (100 or more). The "source propensity" the OutSign heuristic exploits is partly a property of very active users and of the site's rating culture, not a clean trait.
- **Target side.** In Wikipedia the majority of received votes is a better predictor of a vote than the voter's own history at low embeddedness (0.706 against 0.576): votes on one candidate agree with each other, which reflects candidate quality, shared information and visibility of earlier votes, and the data cannot separate them.

### 3.5 Independence of the two papers on Epinions

Guha et al. and LHK both use the Epinions network (Guha's counts equal the public file's; LHK's differ slightly, by an unstated reprocessing), so Epinions is one test seen twice, not two. LHK's added value is Slashdot and Wikipedia (2009 and 2008 snapshots) and the cross-site transfer; Guha's is the operator vocabulary and the rounding finding. Chiang et al. also use the public Epinions file.

The sign-generator should therefore be carried as a **form with site- and era-fitted weights**: a log-odds sum over 16 directed signed path types plus person-level terms, with a validity record per fit. The only cross-setting evidence is three same-era online sites: the balance-type core moved between them with a loss of under one point on the two easy sites (Epinions and Slashdot) and one to three points on Wikipedia, and the status-type part did not.

## 6. Operand/operator translation for the algebra

1. **Relation layer, as the sources use it.** Operands: persons; signed directed ties; for Guha, two relations T and D. Operators on two-step paths: product of undirected signs (balance), oriented sum on an order (status), the 16-type table as a language for either, and a fitted log-odds sum over path-type counts as the general form. Operators on relations: the four words in B and B^T, linear combination, powers, one-step distrust as a gate.
2. **What the data say about the operators.** The (-)(-) cell is not "+" (undirected mm coefficient slightly negative, FFmm clearly negative); forward chains behave as a conjunction on {-,+}; direction matters (BB types are weak, FBmp and BFpm strong; the paper's reduced balance model throws direction away and still transfers, the reduced status model keeps it and does not transfer); distrust composes better as a gate used once than as a sign that chains.
3. **Where a person-level layer enters.** Source propensity and target reception are the strongest cheap terms. In algebra terms they are per-person scalars (a sender effect and a receiver effect) added to the path-count sum, which is the same shape as v0.1's per-person set point plus pairwise coupling [derived analogy, not a claim of the sources]. They are parameters of persons in this data, not derived from drives.
4. **Carry as modular and versioned (fluidity rule).** One law, fitted per setting: log-odds = b0 + person terms + sum of b_tau times counts of 16 path types, with a validity record (site, years, medium, embeddedness range, sign semantics). The two theorems and the operator words are era-invariant given their hypotheses; every b is era-bound.

## 7. What exists vs what does not

**Exists.** (1) A precise, shared formal language (the 16-type directed signed path table, f from types to {+,-,0}) in which balance, weak balance and status are each one line, plus a theorem for each rule's global signature (Cartwright-Harary; the tournament ordering theorem, proved in the paper). (2) A three-site, same-era, online comparison: a strong learned local predictor; a balance-type core that transfers across sites and a status-type part that does not; hand-set rules ordered differently by site; a status ordering found globally and a balance partition not found by the heuristic used. (3) A relation algebra of propagation operators (direct, co-citation, transpose, coupling), an 81-scheme comparison on one real web of trust, and the finding that the rounding step and distrust-as-a-gate matter most. (4) Evidence, from the papers' own heuristics and my recount, that person-level counts carry most of the lift above chance.

**Does not exist in these sources.** Any derivation of a sign from a drive, motive or affect, or any measurement of one. Any longitudinal evidence on how a sign forms or flips (the networks are cumulative snapshots; Wikipedia has timestamps and is not used over time). Any face-to-face, small-group or non-evaluative sign data. Any data after 2009 in the two assigned papers. Any intervention, lever or manipulation. Any interval, standard error or significance test. A measure of status independent of the ties it predicts, or a test that separates status from latent target quality or popularity. A treatment of negative ties' thin embeddedness, or of voter class (admin or not) on Wikipedia. The additive-distrust propagation was stated and never run. A like-for-like comparison of LHK's learned model with Guha's best scheme on one edge set.

## Does it reduce to a drive basis?

**Mostly no for the laws; partly yes for the one strong predictor; and nothing in these two papers measures a drive.** "Drives as primitives" is the hypothesis under test; this tradition neither supports nor refutes it, because where it touches anything person-level it touches a count, not a drive.

**What can be recast as a person- or group-level quantity.**
- **Source propensity** (the source's own majority sign, d+_out and d-_out): the strongest cheap predictor on Epinions and Slashdot, 0.789 and 0.791 balanced among well-embedded edges, and 0.898 and 0.846 on the natural mix against 0.853 and 0.774 for always-positive [computed]. It can be written as a per-person parameter, a standing tendency to rate positively. It has no mechanism here: it may be generosity, a site norm, a rating habit or an activity effect (the negative share climbs with out-degree, from 9.6% to 29.2% on Slashdot), and the data cannot separate them. A drive reading would need the parameter to move when a state a drive theory names is manipulated. Not tested.
- **Target reception** (the target's majority received sign): a group-level aggregate about the target, the best cheap predictor on Wikipedia at low embeddedness (0.71). It reduces to shared information, conformity and visibility of earlier public votes, not to a drive of the target.
- **Status.** A candidate drive (file 055), but the "status" in these papers is an ordering inferred from the ties themselves (sigma(x) from signed degrees; a latent order in the global test), the same signs it predicts, and on these data it is indistinguishable from a latent target quality or popularity score. No source measures anyone's drive for status. The status generator reduces to a drive only by assumption.
- **Balance as a consistency pressure** (084): a person-level consistency drive could be written for it, but both papers use static cumulative snapshots and cannot tell a pressure from structure.
- **Distrust as a gate.** Guha's best use of distrust is a closed gate on another's judgments, used once. That is a person-level credibility mechanism rather than a drive, it matches v0.1's split between a sign input and gates, and it rests on differences of a few points at the noise level.

**What does not reduce.**
- The product and sum rules, the 16-type table, the two theorems and Guha's operator words are relation-layer structure, true or false of a sign pattern whatever the drives are.
- **The sign itself.** In all three datasets a sign is an *evaluation of a target's output* inside a site function: ranking reviews, filtering comments, granting admin rights. It is not an affective bond or a drive state, and on Epinions the distrust relation exists because of a royalty scheme. Whether face-to-face signs obey the same laws is untested.
- The global signatures (a status order, two factions) are emergent; nothing derives them from person-level quantities, and the faction one is not observed under the heuristic used.
- Embeddedness (the number of common neighbours) and its effects are properties of the graph.

**What would count as a test.** A joint model with per-person sender and receiver terms and with and without triad terms on longitudinal sign data, then an intervention on a state that a drive theory says moves the per-person term. None exists in these sources.

## What does it predict or control, and how well?

**Predicts.** The sign of one hidden tie in the same cumulative network snapshot, given every other sign. Not future ties, not group outcomes, not behaviour. Scale: relation. Era 1999-2009; medium online evaluation sites.

**LHK, how well.**
- Balanced accuracy of the full model 0.934 / 0.935 / 0.802 (Epinions / Slashdot / Wikipedia; edges with at least 25 common neighbours; error 6.58% on Epinions); AUC about 90% balanced and 95% on the natural mix (text only). Over all edges about 0.87 / 0.80 / 0.83 [fig].
- Against the best simple predictor in my recount, the lift of the full model is +14.5 (Epinions, OutSign 0.789), +10.5 (Slashdot, Balance heuristic 0.830) and +18.5 points (Wikipedia, StatDif 0.617) at C >= 25; over all edges about +12, +7 and +12 (against StatDif 0.758, OutSign 0.737 and InSign 0.706, each the best simple predictor at C >= 0 in my recount) [derived from Figure 1 by eye and computed]. Against the paper's own Figure 3, whose best simple predictors at Em = 25 are about 0.83 (Epinions, OutSign), 0.825 (Slashdot, Balance) and 0.66 (Wikipedia, StatDif), the lift is smaller on Epinions and Wikipedia: about +10, +11 and +14 points [fig; validation pass]. The triad terms add about 2 to 6 points to the degree features.
- Against its own rivals, learned balance 0.903 / 0.920 / 0.756 and learned status 0.831 / 0.785 / 0.716; hand-set weak balance 0.823 / 0.870 / 0.608, status 0.808 / 0.717 / 0.668, strict balance 0.771 / 0.841 / 0.578.
- **Out-of-sample evidence is cross-site only.** Trained on one site and tested on another, the full model loses little when tested on Epinions or Slashdot (0.925-0.935), and the balance-only model transfers; the status-only model does not. No temporal hold-out and no replication on later data in these papers; Chiang et al. (2014) and Kumar et al. (2016) are later checks on related tasks, not replications.
- Positive-edge presence: 0.56 to 0.59 on Epinions and Slashdot, 0.70 to 0.71 on Wikipedia (chance 0.50).
- Global: status ordering satisfies 79-85% of edges against 63-66% for a degree-preserving null; no balance partition better than baselines by a heuristic that falls short of the trivial value on two sites.

**Guha et al., how well.** Error 6.4% on a natural mix (always-trust 15.3% in the trial set) and 14.7% on the balanced subset (chance 50%); about three in four distrust edges caught at a false-distrust rate near 2.7% [derived]. The best of 81 schemes was chosen on the same 3,250 test edges with no interval, and the gain from propagating distrust over trust-only is five edges of 996 at majority rounding.

**Reliability.** No interval anywhere. Slashdot's Em = 25 test holds about 2,700 balanced edges. Folds are probably not independent (the same nodes recur across folds, and on the paper's framing a test edge's features use the signs of every other edge), so any nominal precision overstates; how the folds interact with feature computation is not described. The headline covers 25.1%, 4.1% and 26.5% of edges and 14.6%, 1.1% and 14.4% of negative edges.

**Failures.** Enemy of my enemy (FFmm) in all three datasets; backward-backward path types weak or negative; Wikipedia the hardest site for signs (and the easiest for edge presence); status coefficients that do not transfer; no global balance partition under the heuristic; negative ties, the ones a conflict model most needs, are the least embedded; accuracy at low embeddedness is mostly person-level counts.

**Controls.** Nothing. Neither paper intervenes; there is no lever, with or without consent. The applications the authors name (estimating one user's attitude toward another from the network, link recommendation, ranking reviews through a web of trust) are inference or exposure-steering by a platform, untested here, and attitude inference would proceed without the person's consent. For v0.1's lever table, this tradition gives no empirical support for how to *produce* a sign flip: it predicts signs, it does not change them.

**Bottom line.** What exists: a good local sign predictor built on one shared language, a balance-type core that transfers across three same-era sites, a status-type part that does not, a detected global status order, and a relation algebra of propagation operators whose best use of distrust is as a gate. Most of the lift over chance is person-level counts. What does not exist: any drive-level derivation, any longitudinal or post-2009 evidence in these papers, any interval, and any intervention. The headline accuracies describe a minority of edges and the thinnest slice of negative ties.

### Datedness and accelerated change

- **The magnitudes are 1999-2009 and unrefreshed.** 106 §4 says they are 17 to 23 years old. No re-estimate of the 16-type model on data collected after 2009 is in this set. Chiang et al. (2014) re-use the same three sites, and Kumar et al. (2016) use a different task on Bitcoin and other platforms. Under 113 §9 (S2) the right flag is `unrefreshed`, not a discount: nothing here measures drift in a coupling weight or a sign law (113 §9.2).
- **The shrinkage prior does not apply by default.** 113 S1 (x0.5) comes from single significant results in social and personality psychology. LHK's numbers come from 100,000 to 840,000 edges. The bias that does apply is selection: Guha's best of 81 schemes was chosen on the same test edges, and no selection step is reported for LHK's 23 features.
- **The sign's meaning changes with the platform.** On Epinions the trust list also decides which reviews a user sees (106 §2.3). On Slashdot the friend and foe tags change the comment scores a user sees (117 §3.2). wiki-RfA votes (2003-13) carry the voter's stated reason (117 §3.3). Bitcoin ratings follow trades (117 §3.4). Reddit's signs come from a classifier at 0.80 accuracy (117 §3.5). A negative tie in 2003 and a negative rating in 2016 are different acts, so the site is a moderator of any sign law, and a ranking that reads the sign back can change the sign it records. Digital mediation acts on the exposure weights, not the response law (114 §5).
- **Time constants have not been measured for signs.** Attention turnover on Twitter shortened by about 12% a year in 2013-16: the average stay in the top 50 hashtags fell from 17.5 h to 11.9 h (113 §6, citing Lorenz-Spreen et al. 2019). None of the sign laws here carries a time constant, and cumulative snapshots cannot estimate one. The Bitcoin formation counts are the first time-ordered closure counts in the set (median gap 59 days in OTC and 76 in Alpha; 117 §3.4).
- **Longitudinal sign data exist, but not for LHK's model.** 106 §7 says there is no longitudinal evidence on how a sign flips. wiki-RfA re-votes show 677 sign changes among 3,761 voter-candidate pairs that vote in more than one election year (117 §3.3). That tests conversion, not the learned 16-type model.
- **The check that matters most is already proposed.** 112 §6.5 (test 1) asks for a per-platform refit on Bitcoin OTC and Alpha and on wiki-RfA, with the forward-chain signs and FFmm < 0 as the prediction. 117 §3.4 counts the same enemy-of-enemy cell and gives the same direction, but it is exploratory: an undirected projection, a null that does not preserve degree, uncontrolled fraud rings and first ratings only. It is direction evidence, not a refit.
- **Validity record.** Each sign-generator line should carry site, years, medium, embeddedness range, sign semantics and the `unrefreshed` flag (112 §6.4; 106 §6 item 4). The ledger lines here carry era and medium; the rest of the record is in the notes.

### Verdict

The numbers and readings in 106 hold against the other files, with the conflicts listed above. The forms (the 16-type table, the two theorems, the operator words) are era-invariant given their hypotheses. The magnitudes are not, and the two cheap checks that could refresh them (a per-platform refit and a degree-preserving re-run of the formation counts) are named above. The conflicts that most change what is attributed to which dataset are the Epinions and Wikipedia shares (contradictions 5 and 6). The enemy-of-my-enemy claim (contradiction 1) most needs a scoped statement.
