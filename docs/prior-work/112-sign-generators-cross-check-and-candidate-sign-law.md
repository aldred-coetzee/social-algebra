**Question (D604).** Which drives generate the sign of a tie by an equation or a tested rule, for the six pairs the map names (power -> coalition; similarity -> attraction; exchange -> sentiment; shared desire -> rivalry; status -> attachment; threat -> in/out); when and in what medium was each tested; where the sources give one feature opposite signs, which is right; and what sign law can v0.2 carry, with what validity domain. ## Summary

1. **No pair is a drive-to-sign equation.** What each supplies, in the sources actually read: power -> coalition, a rule that picks which edge of a three-person group is positive from a strength vector (ordinal; the only equations are Shenoy's 1978 restatement; the "sign" is a binding agreement to split a prize, which the originators say is not an affective tie [105]); similarity -> attraction, a lab regression whose equation I could not verify, plus a population regularity (homophily) that is mostly opportunity structure and, in observational network data, cannot be separated from influence [read: Shalizi and Thomas]; exchange -> sentiment, five assumptions and twelve ordinal propositions with no functional form [read: Lawler 2001]; shared desire -> rivalry, a verbal conditional law (Hobbes) with one group-scale field test (Sherif, 22 boys, 1954) and payoff models (Hiller) [read]; status -> attachment, a definition of the sign inside Leskovec et al.'s status model, with Gould's formal theory closed and unread [read; 110]; threat -> in/out, rally effects in trust in institutions, and an out-group branch that appears only under zero-sum competition with an identifiable rival [read].
2. **The one equation I found that maps signed pairwise inputs and sizes to an outcome tested against the world is Axelrod and Bennett's landscape theory (1993). No earlier file had read it (083 met it only through Marvel et al.).** It is in section 2.3: frustration F_i = sum_j s_j p_ij d_ij, energy E = sum_i s_i F_i, single-actor downhill moves, two real-world hindcasts and one forecast. I recomputed its Unix case from the printed sizes and rule (two minima, equal energy, equal basins) and its chance probabilities (0.0047, 0.00027, 1/16) [computed]. Two conditions bite: its no-cycle theorem is proved for symmetric propensities (better-response cycles in 495 of 600 random asymmetric instances, none in 600 symmetric [computed]; an independent generator at validation gave 499 and 0 of 600), and it extends, by the same footnote-2 argument with a weighted energy, to propensities that are symmetric after a positive per-actor rescaling [derived at validation, 2.3], so it does not transfer to v0.1's deliberately asymmetric a_ij unless that asymmetry is only a per-actor scale; and the WWII test is a hindcast with hand-coded equal-weight factors. Galam's published comment says the model has no frustration; the Unix instance printed in the paper has two all-negative cliques and is frustrated [computed], so the comment fails there; the 17-country matrix is not printed, so it is unchecked.
3. **Cross-check verdicts that change earlier files** (section 2.1). "Weak unite against the strong" is right as an observed regularity in the 4-3-2 triad, stable at 60 to 67 per cent from 1957 to the 2020s (59 of 88 [checked in Shenoy's table]; 35 of 52 and 52 of 80 [checked in Wissink et al.]; the 60 per cent is Cantiani et al. 2024, two-person coalitions in a game that allowed the grand coalition [105 only]), wrong as a general reading of Caplow's table, and the mechanism is an equity expectation rather than power [105]. "The enemy of my enemy is my friend" is wrong as a local sign rule on all three Leskovec sites (coefficient -0.73, -0.59, -0.77 where balance says +1) [checked]. The map's equation for Byrne and Nelson (Y = 5.44X + 6.62) is unverified: the paper is closed. **Three seed DOIs in the map are wrong** (Caplow 1956, Vinacke and Arkoff 1957, Byrne and Nelson 1965); corrections in 0.4.
4. **One feature takes opposite signs in different sources, and the sources name the moderators** (section 2.2): shared object (excludable or not), similarity (of taste or of claim), the strength gap (deference in evaluation, exploitation in contest, courtship in coalition choice), two successive negatives (undetermined, slightly negative), threat (zero-sum rival or universal). A fixed-sign feature library is wrong; a gated library is what the sources support.
5. **Person-level propensities carry most of the predictable variation wherever it was measured, and no source ties them to a drive.** The source's own majority sign alone scores 0.79 balanced accuracy on Epinions and Slashdot against 0.93 for the full model [106, computed there]; in two speed-dating samples (2005, 2007) pre-meeting traits and preferences predicted 4 to 18 per cent of actor variance and 7 to 27 per cent of partner variance and none of the dyad-specific part [read: Joel et al. 2017].
6. **Datedness is not a clock** (section 6.4). Three things are mixed under "datedness": drift (the world changed), shrinkage (the first estimate was inflated) and domain mismatch (the law was never tested where it is applied). The one result re-run under a comparable design across eras, the weak-pair share, did not drift. Where magnitudes fell, the evidence shows shrinkage or mismatch, not drift [051, 107, 109]. So a fluid sign law tags each term with the moderators it depends on (medium parameters, population, legitimacy of weights, exposure structure) and discounts by moderator change first and by age second.
7. **Candidate sign law (section 6).** A registry of gated sign laws, not one equation: a default person-plus-triad logistic form for online evaluation networks, and six conditional laws (joint-task exchange, contest for an unshareable object, coalition choice at n = 3, bloc alignment from symmetric propensities and sizes, information-only similarity, threat), each with a domain, a switch-off list and a validity record; the sign is a state with an adaptation rate, not an input. Three entries (exchange, contest, similarity) are admitted as sign only; the default form, the coalition share and the threat amplitudes carry numbers I read; the alignment layer is admitted as a theorem only for symmetric propensities or propensities symmetric after a per-actor rescaling.
8. **Drive basis.** Drives supply the motive for approach and avoidance in every tradition read, and in none do they fix the sign: the same drive state yields opposite signs across settings. The largest predictive terms (sender and receiver propensities) have no mechanism. No sign dataset measures a drive level beside the sign, so "drives as primitives" cannot be tested on existing data (final sections).
9. **Control.** Nothing outside the lab. The only demonstrated sign reversal (Sherif's superordinate goals, 1954) was a covert manipulation of 22 boys; Lawler's levers are between-group lab manipulations of structure; the large-scale lever found in 109 (composition and exposure) acts openly but on tie formation, not sign.
10. **Asked of v0.1/v0.2 (proposals, not adopted; section 6.6).** Keep the affect sign and the influence polarity apart; add an Object sort with excludability and parity, a Setting vector and interface parameters; make the sign a state; give the alignment layer symmetric propensities, or propensities symmetric after a per-actor rescaling, and say how a_ij is symmetrised if it is neither.
11. **Blocked or unread:** Caplow 1956/1959/1968, Vinacke and Arkoff 1957, Gamson 1961, Byrne and Nelson 1965, Lawler and Yoon 1996, Cook and Emerson 1978, Gould 2002, Stein 1976, Coser 1956, Girard, Sack 2021 are closed. I found no legitimate open copy of any. No single acquisition would change a verdict above; if one is bought, Stein 1976, because its list of conditions for "conflict raises cohesion" is the one piece neither 111 nor I could recover.

### 2.3 Landscape theory (Axelrod and Bennett 1993): the one equation from signed pairwise inputs and sizes to an outcome

**Their vocabulary.** Actors with sizes s_i > 0; pairwise propensities p_ij (positive if they get along, negative if they have sources of conflict), assumed symmetric; configuration (a partition); distance d_ij (0 same side, 1 across, in the two-bloc version); frustration; energy; landscape; local minimum; basin of attraction. Two assumptions: actors are myopic (they evaluate each other pairwise, independent of everyone else) and incremental (one actor at a time switches sides).

**Equations [read], restated.** F_i(X) = sum over j != i of s_j p_ij d_ij(X) (their eq. 1); E(X) = sum_i s_i F_i(X) = sum over ordered pairs of s_i s_j p_ij d_ij(X) (eqs. 2-3). Predictions: (1) configurations move downhill by single-actor changes that lower the mover's own frustration; (2) the stable configurations are the local minima, "exactly those that are in Nash equilibrium"; (3) with symmetric propensities there are no cycles (their footnote 2 proves E(X) - E(Y) = 2 s_k (F_k(X) - F_k(Y)) for one actor k switching). They note the equilibrium need not be the global optimum, that three mutual enemies leave someone frustrated in any two-bloc split, and that early history can fix which basin the system enters.

**The propensity rule [read].** For 1936-39 Europe: five factors with equal weights ("the least arbitrary"): ethnic conflict, a border disagreement and a recent history of war, each -1; similarity of religion (+1 within Catholic, Protestant, Orthodox, Muslim, Atheist; -1 across Christian, Muslim, Atheist, by population shares); similarity of government type (democratic, fascist, communist; +1 same, -1 different); economic ties omitted for lack of data. For 1990: ideology dropped, +1 for mutual membership of the European Community. For Unix: +1 for complementary attributes (specialist with generalist), -1 for conflicting ones; summed, a specialist-generalist pair is 0 and a same-type pair -1. Those are the paper's stated values; a literal sum of its attribute scores, with -1 for every pair of designers, would give -2 for same-type pairs. The inconsistency is in the source and is harmless here: it rescales every non-zero entry and leaves both minima and basins unchanged [computed at validation]; footnote 46 gives a second derivation of 0 and -1. Sizes: Correlates of War capability index (states), 1987 market share (firms), an expert median (AT&T).

**Results [read].** 17 European states, 65,536 configurations, 1936 sizes: two local minima, with basins of 47,945 and 17,591 configurations. Configuration 1, the global minimum, differs from the Allied-Axis alignment in two states (Poland and Portugal) and is right for states holding 96% of total size; Configuration 2 is a pro-Soviet against anti-Soviet split. 1938: only Configuration 1 survives; 1939: a single minimum, wrong for one state (Marvel et al.'s caption names Portugal; Poland has moved to the anti-German side). Between 1936 and 1939 the only propensity input that changed was Romania's government type, so the change in the landscape came almost entirely from sizes (their footnote 37). Baselines: with every propensity -1 (the realist model) 209 stable configurations for the 17 states; a size-weighted average-linkage clustering finds the Soviet Union and Greece against the rest. 1990: 19 states (computed with Benelux as one unit and Spain with Portugal as another, sizes as of 1985; their footnote 41), a prediction from the 1989 alignment that the Soviet Union would be deserted by all its European allies but Bulgaria; the authors count one error (Romania) as of 1991-92. The paper does not say when the run was made, and the events it is scored against (November 1991 to January 1992) precede publication (April 1993), so it is not shown to be prospective. Unix: 9 firms, 256 configurations, two minima of equal energy and equal basin; with the first move (AT&T with Sun) known, Configuration 1 gets 6 of 7 later commitments (IBM wrong), 97% of total size.

**My checks [computed].** The Unix landscape reproduces from the printed sizes, types and rule (2.1). The chance probabilities reproduce. The identity behind the no-cycle theorem holds for symmetric p and fails for asymmetric p; better-response cycles occur in 495 of 600 random asymmetric instances and in none of 600 symmetric ones. The Unix instance contains an all-negative clique on the five specialists and one on the four generalists, which cannot be two-coloured with every negative edge across; the best split leaves 6 of the 16 negative pairs inside a bloc. The two minima exist because specialist-generalist pairs are 0: the optimum splits each type class into near-equal halves (29.9 against 30.0 for the specialists, 32.3 against 31.5 for the generalists) and the pairing of the two splits is free. So the "two predictions" are one free bit, and the first move fixes it. [This paragraph was re-run at validation with an independent script: the same two minima at -3,828.9, basins 128 and 128, 6 of 16 negative pairs inside a bloc, and 14 all-negative triangles.]

**Galam's comment [read].** It says the model is of Mattis type, so a gauge change turns it into a ferromagnet with no frustration, and that most of the authors' conclusions (frustration, instability) do not follow; the demonstration is in Physica A 230 (1996), not read. For the Unix instance the claim is false (above). For the WWII matrix I cannot say: it is not printed in the paper, nor in the arXiv text of Marvel et al., who use it (caption of their Figure 2C: the correct Allied-Axis split except Denmark and Portugal, and Axelrod and Bennett's own theory misclassifies only Portugal). A balanced (Mattis) signed array would be one that Cartwright and Harary's theorem 2-colours with no frustration, which is v0.1's polarisation case.

**Limits.** Propensity is a willingness to be in the same coalition, symmetric by assumption, not an affect or an influence; the factors are hand-coded, equally weighted, and were coded in 1993 for events of 1939 (a hindcast, with room for coding choices); the two-bloc form is assumed; the five factors include ethnic conflict, border disagreement and recent war, which already encode antagonism, so the test cannot separate a sign generated by something else from a sign put in (111 makes the same point [111, derived]); no replication is reported and I found none (at validation the one later publication found is the full Unix analysis, Axelrod, Mitchell, Thomas, Bennett & Bruderer 1995, *Management Science* 41(9): 1493-1508, doi:10.1287/mnsc.41.9.1493, Crossref-confirmed, not read; its abstract describes the same nine firms and the 1988 alliances, so it is the same test, not a replication; no open search was possible at validation); Axelrod and Bennett replied to Galam (*Br. J. Polit. Sci.* 28(2): 412-414, doi:10.1017/S0007123498220990, Crossref-confirmed; closed, not read [111]); and the Unix case turns on a free bit. It is nevertheless the only read source that builds the sign array itself from non-drive facts by a stated additive rule and tests the resulting alignment against outside outcomes, and its sizes are the only read treatment in this cluster of the weight of a member.

**Translation [ours].** Operands: persons (or states, firms), sizes, a symmetric signed propensity array, a configuration. Operators: frustration, energy, single-actor switch. In v0.1 terms: the propensity array is a candidate for sgn(a_ij) (symmetrised), sizes are an operand v0.1 lacks, and the dynamics are a second, discrete alignment dynamics beside the Altafini state layer. It is a potential game for symmetric p (their footnote 2) and, by the same argument, for any p with mu_i p_ij = mu_j p_ji for some positive mu_i: then Phi(X) = sum over i != j of mu_i s_i s_j p_ij d_ij(X) gives Phi(X) - Phi(Y) = 2 mu_k s_k (F_k(X) - F_k(Y)), a weighted potential in the sense of Monderer and Shapley (1996, *Games Econ. Behav.* 14(1): 124-143, doi:10.1006/game.1996.0044, Crossref-confirmed, not read), so better-response cycles are impossible [derived at validation; the identity and the absence of cycles checked on 1,000 random instances, n = 3 to 7]. Generic asymmetric p, and any pair with opposite signs, is outside both. v0.1's a_ij is asymmetric by design (section 2.2 of `models/00-v0.md`), so importing the theorem needs either that per-actor-scale structure, which is testable on a fitted a_ij, or a symmetrisation rule that is a decision (6.6).

## 3. The generators, in the house format (compact; detail is in the sibling files)

### 3.1 Power -> coalition (D597; 105)

- **Vocabulary.** Strength or resources, triad, coalition, isolate, quota, cheapest winning coalition, "strength is weakness".
- **Drives and operands.** Persons with a scalar strength; control over others is Caplow's stated motive (assumptions A.1-A.4: members differ in strength; each seeks control, two over one over none; strength is additive; coalitions form in a triad with a pre-coalition condition) [105 via Shenoy; checked in Shenoy section 6 at validation]; a divisible prize.
- **Operators and equations.** A weighted majority game [q; a, b, c]; a control relation; Caplow's index kappa(P)(i) = p(P)(i) / sum_j p(P)(j), p(P)(i) the number of players i controls; Gamson's index gamma(P)(i) = a_i / (weight of i's coalition) if it wins, else 0; a dynamic solution from all-singletons; Shenoy's Theorem 7.1 that, in a proper weighted majority game, the solution under gamma is exactly the structures containing a cheapest winning coalition [105, reading Shenoy; checked in Shenoy at validation; the equations are Shenoy's, who says his formulation is implicit in Caplow 1956, not stated there].
- **Predictions.** Eight types; in Type 5 (4-3-2) AC or BC and never AB, with the 1956 text declining to choose; Gamson chooses BC; Chertkoff (1967) gives BC twice as often as AC and AB never [checked in Shenoy at validation], and Walker (1973) 58, 33 and 8 per cent for BC, AC and AB [105].
- **Empirical standing.** 1957: BC 59, AC 20, AB 9, none 2 of 90 games (Shenoy's Table 2, checked). About 2018-20: BC 35 of 52 triads, w = 0.78 (lab, 156 of 180 undergraduates, 79% female), and 52 of 80, 65% (MTurk, 240 adults, 50% female) [checked]; Study 3 (320 Prolific participants, a hypothetical 4(322) political scenario) tests first offers and expectations, not coalition frequency [read, not studied]. 60% of pairs in a Dutch probability sample [105]. It flips or vanishes with earned weights, a competing outside triad, persisting structure (families) and n of 4 or more [105].
- **Scale, size, consent.** Group decision; n = 3 exactly; weights assigned by the experimenter and disclosed.
- **Translation.** AB|C is a block partition; the sign pattern (+AB, -AC, -BC) is ours; a coalition here is a joint-action agreement, not an affective tie ("coalitions are not the same as affective cliques", Grusky et al. via 105).

### 3.2 Similarity -> attraction (D601; 109)

- **Vocabulary.** Co-orientation, A-B-X, consensual validation, homophily (baseline against inbreeding), perceived against actual similarity, focus.
- **Drives and operands.** The originators' mechanism is a learned need to be right and predictable; no study measures it [109]. Operands: persons, stances on objects, estimates of others' stances, foci.
- **Operators and equations.** None verified. Newcomb 1953 contains no equation [109]; Byrne's line is [sa]; McPherson et al. define baseline homophily (what the opportunity set produces) and inbreeding homophily (over and above it) [read].
- **Empirical standing.** Information-only lab, 1959-60: liking at the extremes of agreement is enormous (d about 7.6) [109]. Free choice: baseline (opportunity) patterns "strongly shape networks" [read: McPherson]; the split between opportunity and bias is quantified in Kossinets and Watts and Chetty et al. [109]. Face to face, initial attraction, 2005 and 2007 speed-dating (163 and 187 undergraduates, 4-minute dates): actor and partner variance partly predictable (4 to 18%, 7 to 27%), dyad-specific desire not at all from reported traits and preferences [read: Joel 2017]. Observational networks: homophily and contagion generically confounded [read: Shalizi and Thomas].
- **Scale, size, consent.** Membership (tie formation); dyad to 70 million; no intervention except the exposure lever in Chetty et al. [109].
- **Translation.** Signs become state (attraction and perceived similarity are coupled in a loop, Newcomb 1953 [109]); the exposure term is a group-structure operand, not a drive [109].

### 3.3 Exchange -> sentiment (D600; 108)

- **Vocabulary.** Global feelings, attribution to self, other or social unit, nonseparability, shared responsibility, productive, negotiated, reciprocal and generalised exchange, positive and negative connection.
- **Drives and operands.** Assumptions 1-3: exchange produces positive or negative global feelings; they act as internal rewards or punishments; actors strive to reproduce the positive and avoid the negative. Assumptions 4-5: feelings trigger attributions, and in joint tasks partly to social units [read].
- **Operators and equations.** None. Propositions 1-2 (nonseparability raises shared responsibility, which raises attribution to the unit), 3-7 (productive > negotiated, reciprocal > generalised in feeling and attribution), 8 (success: pride and gratitude together; failure: anger at the other above shame), 9 (attachment to stable, controllable sources of positive feeling; detachment from stable, uncontrollable sources of negative), 10-12 (positive and negative connection, nesting) [read]. Scope: ongoing, recurring, renegotiable at will; spot exchanges among strangers excluded; three or more actors.
- **Standing.** The tested chain (frequency, emotion, cohesion, commitment) is lab-only, anonymous, about 1992-2005, n = 2 to 5, mostly same-occasion self-report [108].
- **Scale, size, consent.** Membership and group; lab manipulations of structure with deception about the setting, debriefed [108].
- **Translation.** The tie is a slow variable moved by joint-task outcomes through an attribution gate; no rate is read [108].

### 3.4 Shared desire -> rivalry (D599; 107)

- **Vocabulary.** Competition, diffidence, glory; emulation and envy (Hobbes); mimetic desire, mediator, rivalry (Girard); superordinate goal (Sherif).
- **Drives and operands.** An appetite for the same thing; Hobbes names three motives that can each produce enmity: gain, safety, reputation [107, read there]. Operands: persons, an object with excludability, groups.
- **Operators and equations.** Hobbes XIII, verbal: "if any two men desire the same thing, which neverthelesse they cannot both enjoy, they become enemies" [read]. Hiller: strength n_i = own strength plus that of friends; a negative link is never reciprocated in equilibrium; equal strength -> friends, unequal -> the stronger extends the negative link (Proposition 1); all-friends and "n - 1 friends against one" are equilibria (Proposition 2, homogeneous agents); any strong Nash equilibrium has two maximal cliques and a single weakest agent (Proposition 5, homogeneous agents) [read]. Axelrod and Bennett: p = -1 for same-niche rivals [read].
- **Standing.** Sherif (checked): at the end of the competition stage 93.6% and 92.5% of friendship choices stayed in-group; out-group ratings were 53.0% and 76.9% unfavourable; after superordinate goals out-group friendship choices rose from 6.4% to 36.4% and from 7.5% to 23.2%, rejections of out-group members fell from 75% to 15% and from 95% to 47.1%, unfavourable ratings to 4.5% and 22.6%. Contact without interdependence did not help. The authors state the law as "the nature of functional relations between" the groups. Girard's imitation-without-scarcity claim is untested [107].
- **Scale, size, consent.** Group; two matched groups of 11 strangers; covert manipulation (the staff posed as counsellors, situations designed so the boys could not trace them to the staff; parents consented to the camp) [read].
- **Translation.** An Object sort with excludability is needed (107); the sign is not a function of the desire alone.

### 3.5 Status -> attachment (D602, D598; 106, 110)

- **Vocabulary.** Status (an implicit ordering), balance, weak balance, status-consistent, embeddedness.
- **Drives and operands.** No drive named in Leskovec et al. Signed directed ties; the status of a node is inferred from the ties it predicts [106].
- **Operators and equations [read].** A positive edge (x, y) means x regards y as higher; orient the path u -> w -> v and the status rule predicts sign(s(u,w) + s(w,v)), silent when they cancel (8 of 16 types). Theorem 2 (theirs): in a signed tournament in which every triple is status-consistent the nodes can be ordered so every positive edge points forward and every negative edge backward. The learned model is a logistic regression on 16 directed signed path-type counts plus seven degree features.
- **Standing.** Accuracies in 2.1. Person-level counts carry most of the lift [106, computed there]. Gould 2002 (closed): per its abstract, actors adjust status-conferring gestures to collective attributions with a brake on unreciprocated gestures, and the ranking is an equilibrium [110, abstract level].
- **Scale, size, consent.** Relation inside crowd-size populations; observational.
- **Translation.** The status order is inferred from the very ties it explains, so it is indistinguishable here from target quality or popularity [106]; where status competition produces negative ties the evidence is group-level stereotypes and one fitted triad model [110].

### 3.6 Threat -> in/out (D603; 111)

- **Vocabulary.** Rally round the flag, institutional and interpersonal trust, realistic and symbolic threat, common ingroup identity, mortality salience, asabiya.
- **Drives and operands.** Fear and security; persons, a group, an external threat or rival, institutions.
- **Operators and equations.** None read. Turchin's asabiya ODE, dS/dt = r (1 - A/(2b)) S (1 - S), is restated in a second paper (Cheon & Poghosyan, arXiv:1512.07715v2, eq. 2; the form read and matched at validation; Turchin 2003 itself unread) and its parameters are not measurable [072].
- **Standing (read by me, except where a file number is given).** Seven West European countries, 2 March to 3 April 2020, about 1,000 respondents each: after a country's lockdown began, trust in government was 2.4 to 3.2% of the scale higher, satisfaction with democracy 2.8 to 3.2%, vote intention for the prime minister's party 4.1 to 4.3 points; no effect on left-right position; the authors credit the lockdown, not the pandemic's incidence [Bol]. These are adjusted estimates from before and after groups (4,605 and 2,183 observations) that differ in composition (university degree 38.6% before, 29.2% after); the raw means in the appendix differ in size and, for satisfaction with democracy, in sign (6.41 before, 6.16 after on a 1-11 scale), so the headline depends on the adjustment [read]. The attribution to the lockdown is disputed: Schraff (2021, Dutch panel, March 2020) applies the same method and finds the lockdown term disappears under a flexible time trend, with cumulative case counts predicting trust [111, read in part]. Sweden, 24 February to 10 March against 31 March to 14 April 2020, 7,206 paired respondents: institutional trust +0.098 on a 0-1 scale (95% CI .094 to .103, d_z .53); interpersonal trust +0.012 (CI .008 to .016, d_z .07, n = 7,184) [Esaiasson]. Netherlands, autumn 2017 against May 2020, 997 analysed: anti-immigrant sentiment fell (mean difference -0.35, SD 1.63, t(964) = -6.70) and perceived pandemic threat was unrelated to the change (B = 0.08, SE 0.06, p = .20); 4.7% of variance in the change explained [Muis and Reeskens]. Sherif's camp (3.4) is the zero-sum case. Mortality salience -> worldview defence: g 0.07 to 0.13 in Many Labs 4 (2016-17, US students) against d 1.34 in the original [051].
- **Wider reads, in 111 [111].** Pooled over 16 war-exposure studies (villages and communities, wars of 1991-2010; Bauer et al., working paper): cooperation index +0.07 to +0.08 SD; prosocial game behaviour toward in-group partners +0.24 to +0.25 SD; toward out-group partners +0.04 SD, not significant, "no indication of negative effects towards the out-group". Libyan battalions (2011): 97% of 179 fused with their own battalion. Brewer (1999, read in full there): in-group regard and out-group hostility are separable, citing a correlation near .00 across 30 East African groups [quoted in Brewer]. Hammond and Axelrod's simulation: the out-group move is non-cooperation, not harm. Defeat in Sherif's camp produced friction inside both groups for hours to days. Raids needed the leader's approval and far fewer were carried out than were proposed. 111's conclusion, which mine matches: a one-line law "threat -> + inside, - across" is not supported; a two-term conditional law is, with the default across a boundary at zero and a negative tie requiring a perceived source or a competitive functional relation.
- **Scale, size, consent.** Crowd (opinion) for the rally studies; group for Sherif; the rally studies are natural experiments of time or policy, not interventions; Sherif was covert.
- **Translation.** Threat moves the in-group tie toward institutions and leaders, hardly toward strangers, and moves the out-group tie only when the threat is competitive and attributed to an identifiable rival; the default across a boundary is zero [111]. A leader-approval gate on cross-boundary hostile acts (111's S-D) is not in v0.1. Stein 1976 is closed and only an excerpt of Coser 1956 was read (111), so the conditions the map names them for remain unrecovered.

### 3.7 Two generators the map did not name

- **Person-level propensities and exposure.** A sender effect (how positively i rates anyone) and a receiver effect (how positively j is rated) are the strongest cheap predictors where measured: the source's majority sign 0.789 and 0.791 (balanced accuracy, embeddedness at least 25, Epinions and Slashdot) and the target's majority sign 0.706 on Wikipedia at low embeddedness [106, computed there]; speed-dating actor and partner variance [read: Joel 2017]. Exposure (foci, baseline homophily) sets who can tie at all [read: McPherson; 109]. Neither is a drive; both are parameters of persons or of the group's structure.
- **Local sign structure (the 16-type table).** For each common neighbour w of u and v, the type records the direction of u-w and w-v and the two signs; the counts enter the logistic model. The balance-type core, reduced to four undirected types, transfers across sites with a loss of under one point on Epinions and Slashdot and one to three points on Wikipedia; status-type weights do not [checked: Tables 6-7].

## 5. What changes with group size (n) and with composition or definition

- **Leskovec et al.** n is 7,118 to 119,217 nodes with no size analysis; the only size-like variable is embeddedness (common neighbours), the amount of triadic evidence: it helps the learned triad model on Epinions and Slashdot and not on Wikipedia, and the simple triad heuristics peak and fall. Definition: the sign means trust in reviews, liking of comments or a vote on fitness for admin rights; half of Wikipedia's votes come from existing admins, which the paper does not analyse [checked; 106].
- **Axelrod and Bennett.** Size enters as a weight s_i in the frustration of everyone else, and the configuration space has 2^(n-1) points; three mutual enemies leave someone frustrated whatever the split; the 1936-to-1939 change in predictions came from sizes alone (the Romania exception aside). Definition: two blocs assumed; a state's "side" is defined by who invaded or declared war on whom [read].
- **Hiller.** Proposition 2 (all friends, and n - 1 against one, are equilibria) holds for every n of 3 or more; a strong Nash equilibrium, where one exists, has two maximal cliques and one weakest agent [read]; multi-clique equilibria need cliques that grow at least geometrically for sharp contests [107].
- **Caplow-type triads.** The result is n = 3, equal-prize, all-pairs-win; at n of 4 or more the strongest is included more often, and no source gives the switch size [105]. Composition: sex changes whether a coalition forms, not the weak-pair preference; earned weights, a rival triad and persisting groups reverse or erase it; "strength" is an assigned weight, a perceived control, a probability of winning or an influence depending on the study [105].
- **Exchange.** Agreement .62 in dyads against .42 in triads, and the cohesion-to-investment link fails at n = 3 (cross-experiment); humans never above 5 [108]. Lawler 2001 assumes three or more actors [checked].
- **Robbers Cave.** Two groups of 11; matched and homogeneous by selection; the law is stated between groups and says nothing for n = 2 or crowds [checked].
- **Homophily.** Smaller categories are more inbred than the opportunity set predicts, so a group's relative size sets the baseline [read: McPherson]; meeting bias grows with school size while preference bias is not significantly size-dependent, and friending bias rises with group size and peaks near an even mix [109].
- **Speed-dating and couples.** Homogeneous undergraduate samples (2005, 2007); Western couples [read].
- **Rally and threat.** Whole-country samples; subgroups that perceive more threat (women, first-generation immigrants, the elderly) show no more out-group negativity [read: Muis and Reeskens].
- **Collected elsewhere:** 109 section 7 (Currarini, Chetty), 110 section 5 (Chase and Lindquist: attribute and loser effects predict dyads but not groups of four), 108 section 5, 091.
- **What this says about v0.1's rule that n enters only through composition operators.** Consistent with every size effect read here: size acts through weights (Axelrod and Bennett), exposure (Currarini, Chetty), embeddedness (Leskovec) and the structure of the game (Caplow, Lawler), not as a property of a person. Composition is not a property of a person either: the same person has different friending bias in a school and in a religious group [109].

## 6. Candidate sign law for v0.2 [ours]

### 6.1 What the cross-check requires of any sign law

1. **The sign is an evaluation or an agreement, directed, and possibly asymmetric.** The sources' "signs" are an evaluation of a target's output (Leskovec), a liking (Newcomb), a coalition agreement (Caplow), a willingness to be in the same bloc (Axelrod and Bennett, symmetric by assumption) and a link whose undirected sign is positive only if both directions are (Hiller). v0.1's sgn(a_ij) is an influence polarity under the Altafini form. The step from affect to influence is an assumption with no read evidence (the influence weights in 103 are non-negative).
2. **Person-level propensities come first.** They carry most of the predictable variation (3.7).
3. **Dyadic generators are conditional on the setting.** One feature takes opposite signs across settings (2.2); the setting is an input.
4. **Signs are state.** Roughly 30% of dyadic ties in the Newcomb house changed state at the first transition (including changes to and from null; no sign-to-sign rate is printed) and the rate fell over about seven weeks [109, citing 104]; Sherif's reversal took days; Lawler's chain builds over episodes [108].
5. **Size acts through weights and exposure** (section 5).
6. **Symmetry, or symmetry after a per-actor rescaling, is a hypothesis of the alignment theorem** (2.3).
7. **Forms are stable and magnitudes are bound to era, medium and population** (section 4).
8. **No source measures a drive level beside a sign**, so drive-to-sign weights are unidentified.

### 6.2 The module

    z_ij(t)     =  sum over r in R of  pi_r( sigma_ij(t) ) * z^r_ij(t)                (S1)
    zbar_ij(t+1) = (1 - lam) * zbar_ij(t) + lam * z_ij(t)                              (S2)
    s_ij(t)     =  +1 if zbar_ij > theta_plus;  -1 if zbar_ij < -theta_minus;  0 else  (S3)

sigma_ij(t) is the setting vector of the pair: evaluation network, shared focus, information-only, contested object (excludability rho in [0,1] and parity of hope), recurrent at-will joint task, triad with public unearned strengths, rival group present, exogenous threat level, interface version. pi_r >= 0 are gate weights summing to 1, equal to 0 outside law r's registered domain and soft (a setting g_r in [0,1] for how far outside the tested range the law is trusted, with a width). lam in (0,1] is the adaptation rate; theta_plus and theta_minus are dead-zone widths. R-T and R-A return a structure (a pair, a partition) that the module converts to signed contributions on the implied edges.

    R-E (default; online evaluation networks)
        z^E_ij = a_i + b_j + sum over tau of c_tau * n_tau(i,j)
        a_i sender propensity, b_j receiver reception, n_tau the count of common neighbours in each of the 16
        directed signed two-step types; admitted sign pattern for forward chains u->w->v:  pp +, pm -, mp -, mm -;
        FFmm negative on all three sites; backward-backward types small.                      [checked: Leskovec et al.]
    R-X (recurrent at-will joint task)
        z^X_ij = k_X * G_ij * ( q_ij - q0 )
        q_ij an exponentially weighted agreement rate in recent joint tasks; G_ij in [0,1] the attribution gate,
        rising with nonseparability and shared responsibility, 0 for spot exchange among strangers;
        failure branch negative with anger at the other above shame; detachment from stable, uncontrollable negative sources.
                                                                             [sign and gate form: Lawler 2001 read]
    R-C (contest for an unshareable object)
        z^C_ij = - rho_X * parity_X * ( 1 - iota_ij )
        iota_ij in [0,1]: the degree to which each side needs the other to reach a goal (superordinate interdependence).
        Conditions: same object, not jointly enjoyable, parity of hope, no common power.   [Hobbes, Sherif, Axelrod and Bennett read]
    R-T (coalition choice, n = 3)
        for public, unearned strengths, an equal prize and no outside rival: the weak pair of the [5;4,3,2] triad coalesces with
        probability q_T (default 0.65; checked 0.65 to 0.67 in three samples, pooled 146 of 220 = 0.66, 1957 to about 2020;
        the 0.60 of Cantiani et al. 2024, two-person coalitions with the grand coalition allowed, is [105] and does not
        set the number); positive contribution inside the pair,
        negative toward the isolate; off for earned weights, a rival group, persistence, n >= 4.
                                                                    [Shenoy Table 2 and Wissink checked; conditions 105]
    R-A (bloc alignment; symmetric layer)
        p_ij = p_ji = sum over k of c_k f_k(i,j)   (candidate factors: similarity of religion or government type +;
        ethnic conflict, border dispute, war history -; complementarity of function +; same-niche rivalry -)
        X* = local minimum of E(X) = sum over i != j of s_i s_j p_ij d_ij(X) under single-actor switches.
        Valid as a potential game for symmetric p, or for mu_i p_ij = mu_j p_ji with mu > 0 (2.3).
                                [Axelrod and Bennett read; identity computed; extension derived at validation]
    R-S (information-only similarity)
        z^S_ij = + k_S * ( perceived agreement_ij - q_S ),  pi_S -> 0 when i and j share a focus.   [sign only; 109]
        (gate read at validation in Kossinets and Watts Table 3: odds ratio 1.03 within a shared focus overall,
        but 1.11 and 1.26 for pairs in a shared focus at network distance 4 and 5 or more)
    R-H (threat)
        z^H_ij = + l_in * thr * 1[ j is the in-group's institution or leader ]
                 - l_out * 1[ j is in a rival group over a zero-sum object ]
        and 0 toward the out-group under a universal exogenous threat.
                                                          [Bol, Esaiasson, Muis, Sherif read; the zero-sum branch is Sherif's one camp]

### 6.4 Validity record, and why datedness follows moderators

| Law | Era of the evidence | Medium | n | Population and semantics | Moderators the sources name |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| R-E | 1999-2009 | online evaluation sites | 7,118 to 119,217 nodes | users rating each other's output; Epinions distrust added after launch against gaming | site semantics; embeddedness; public voting [106] |

| R-C | 1954; 1988 | camp; firms | 22 boys; 9 firms | matched strangers; a technology-standards market | excludability, parity, common power, superordinate goal |

| R-S | 1959-60 | paper stimuli | 2 | US undergraduates | information regime; shared focus; perceived against actual [109] |
| R-H | 2020; 1954 | online panels; camp | thousands; 22 | West European adults; boys | object of trust; universal against competitive threat |

Three things are mixed under "datedness": **drift** (the world changed), **shrinkage** (the first estimate was inflated) and **domain mismatch** (the law was never tested where it is applied). The evidence read:

- One result has been re-run under a comparable design across eras, the weak-pair share in the 4-3-2 triad (59 of 88 in 1957; 35 of 52 and 52 of 80 about 2018-20; 60% of pairs in a 2020s probability sample, in a game that allowed the grand coalition [105]). It did not drift while the medium changed from a board game to online strangers. What moved it was the structure and legitimacy of the game [105].
- The mimetic-copying effect came back at 45% of its earlier mean [107] and the mortality-salience effect at g 0.07 to 0.13 against d 1.34 [051]. The mortality-salience original had about 12 per cell, the profile of an inflated estimate [051]; for copying, 107 says regression of stimulus effects, a different population and noise cannot be separated. Neither is demonstrated drift.
- Byrne's information-only effect against the small free-choice effects, and the speed-dating null for the dyad part, are domain mismatch: the information regime is the moderator [109; read: Joel 2017].
- Where an interface sets a loop gain (Salganik et al.: counts shown in a popularity-sorted list raised inequality more than a grid) the term changes with a design decision, not with years [110].
- Drift is plausible and unmeasured where the dimension that sorts people moves (party label against race, religion falling and education rising as sorting lines [109, from abstracts and a review]) and where tie semantics change (one tap to follow or block).

113 splits "decay" into six kinds (E estimate shrinkage, P real drift, C composition of the sample, M changed yardstick, O the perceiver's illusion, R the finding changing the behaviour) and finds no source that re-estimates a tie sign, gate, coupling weight or inertia across eras; it proposes an `unrefreshed` flag rather than an age discount, and a default x0.5 shrinkage with slack 0.25 to 0.75 for single-study social-psychology effects (replication r .197 against .403 in the Open Science Collaboration; median d .60 to .15 in Many Labs 2) [113, read]. The three-way split used here is the E, P and C/M part of that list.

So the rule proposed for the registry is: tag each term with the moderators it depends on; flag it `unrefreshed` when no re-run exists; demote a term from "admitted" to "sign only" when the target setting differs from its validity record on a named moderator; apply a shrinkage prior to single-study magnitudes (candidate for R-X, R-C, R-S and the Sherif percentages; the x0.5 default is 113's proposal, not adopted); discount by age only for terms that depend on a medium parameter or an uncontrolled population, with the half-life an editable setting that has no read basis. Structural statements (6.2's forms, the potential-game identity, Hobbes's conditions) are not discounted by age.

### 6.5 Tests that could refute it (hand-off to D614, on D610's data)

The usable data are those 117 shortlists: wiki-RfA with vote text, Bitcoin OTC and Alpha, and, since 117's own validation, BlueTempNet (Bluesky follows and blocks, 2023-24) third, with the Reddit hyperlink network as runner-up; none records excludable objects or joint-task outcomes, so R-X and R-C are untestable there and need new data (Aldred's decision).

1. **Form transfer (R-E).** Fit the 16-type model per site on Bitcoin OTC and Alpha and on wiki-RfA. Prediction: forward-chain signs (pp +, pm -, mp -, mm -) and FFmm < 0 hold; refuted if mm > 0 with adequate support. Pre-test, counted not fitted [117]: in OTC triangles the closing sign follows the product of the first two in 89.3% (null 69.3%), but a negative-negative start closes positive in 76.8% against a null of 86.5%, which fits weak balance.
2. **Person terms dominate.** Held-out sign accuracy from a_i and b_j alone against triad terms alone. Prediction: person terms carry more; refuted if the triad terms add more than the person terms.
3. **Drift against a stable form.** Refit by year on wiki-RfA (2003-13) and OTC (2010-16). Prediction under the moderator reading: the sign pattern is stable while intercepts and person-term variance move with site changes; refuted if the sign pattern flips with year.
4. **Symmetry.** The share of reciprocated pairs with opposite signs; Leskovec et al. call it rare in 2003-09 data. If it is not rare in a modern medium, R-A must not be used there without a symmetrisation rule.
5. **Visibility.** On a public-vote site, whether the target's received majority outperforms the voter's own history at low embeddedness (Wikipedia 0.706 against 0.576 in 2008 [106]) and whether the gap moves with the date.

### 6.6 What it would change in v0.1 and v0.2 (proposals, not adopted)

- `models/00-v0.md` section 2.2, "Why the sign is an input": still true that no source derives the sign from drives; it should now say that conditional sign rules exist, list the registry, and say which are admitted for the testbed. Aldred's decision.
- Keep **two signs**: the affect or evaluation sign the sources generate, and the influence polarity v0.1's Altafini form uses (107 raised this for rivalry: copying a desire is positive influence with negative affect). A bridge from one to the other would need its own evidence.
- New sorts: an **Object** with excludability and parity (107), a **Setting** vector (109's focus, with size and composition), **interface parameters** (110). Members of the Setting such as the focus carry n and composition as operands, which is where 109 and the size tables put them.
- **Sign as state** with an adaptation rate (S2), against the fixed-sign hypothesis of the Altafini dichotomy, which 109 says is not supported at acquaintance and is a hypothesis for established ties.
- **Symmetrisation.** If the alignment layer is used, the symmetrisation of a_ij (mean, minimum, sign of the product) is a decision with a different consequence under each; it is not made here. None is needed where a fitted a_ij is symmetric after a positive per-actor rescaling (2.3); whether it is can be tested.

## 7. What exists vs what does not

**Exists.** Conditional verbal sign laws with at least one field or lab test each (Hobbes and Sherif; Lawler's chain; Byrne's information-only effect; Caplow-Gamson's coalition choice, replicated across 60 years and three media); a read, tested equation from signed propensities and sizes to alignments (Axelrod and Bennett); a learned local sign model with a balance-type core that transfers across three same-era sites and a status-type part that does not (Leskovec et al.); payoff models that derive signs from strength differences (Hiller); large-sample evidence that person-level terms and opportunity structure carry most of what looks like dyadic similarity effects; rally effects in trust in institutions with effect sizes and a null for out-group attitudes under a universal threat.

**Does not exist in anything I could read.** A sign derived from a measured drive level; a study that manipulates a drive-relevant state, with consent and a pre-measured sign, holding structure fixed; the equation behind Byrne's line, Gould's model, Caplow's own text, Stein's conditions; longitudinal sign data with re-signing beyond two partial cases (Newcomb's signs derived from ranks [104]; wiki-RfA's 677 sign changes across repeat candidacies, which re-rate a candidate rather than a standing tie, found at 117's validation; Bitcoin OTC and Alpha record formation only [117]); face-to-face or small-group data on negative ties beyond Newcomb's house and Sherif's camp that I read (such data exist, for example liking, disliking and bully-victim networks in Huitsing et al. 2012, *Social Networks* 34(4): 645-657, doi:10.1016/j.socnet.2012.08.001, and negative relationships in Labianca, Brass & Gray 1998, *Acad. Manage. J.* 41(1): 55-67, doi:10.2307/256897; both Crossref-confirmed by title at validation, not read); any test of the potential-game result on a case other than the two (the 1995 *Management Science* paper is the same Unix case, 2.3); any read evidence on how a rally decays (one study exists, 2.2); any dataset with excludable objects and ties; any test of a sign law on a medium with a non-human member (D607).

## Does it reduce to a drive basis?

**Partly: drives supply the motive, and in none of the six do they fix the sign.** "Drives as primitives" is the hypothesis under test, and no sign dataset in this cluster measures a drive level beside a sign, so existing data cannot test it. What can be said is where a drive appears and what else the sign needs.

**What reduces to drives of persons or groups.**
- *Reward-seeking and affect regulation* is the engine of the exchange tradition: global feelings act as internal rewards and punishments and actors strive to reproduce the positive (assumptions 1-3) [read]. Commitment is an output, not a drive [108].
- *Control-seeking* is an explicit axiom of Caplow's theory (each member seeks control over others) [105 via Shenoy]; the sign pattern it yields is decided by the strength vector and an equity expectation, not by the strength of the wish.
- *An appetite for the same unshareable thing*, with diffidence and glory, is Hobbes's route to enmity and the engine of Sherif's result [read]; Hiller and Axelrod and Bennett get negative links from payoffs or coded rivalry.
- *A need for validation and predictability* is the originators' reading of similarity-attraction [109]; unmeasured.
- *Fear and security* underlie the rally and the competitive-threat branch; the mortality-salience version of the fear-to-worldview operator did not replicate [051].
- *Status* appears as an inferred order in Leskovec et al. and as an emergent fixed point in the formal models; no read source puts a measured status drive into a sign law [106, 110].

**What does not reduce.**
- *Sender and receiver propensities*, the largest predictive terms (0.79 balanced accuracy from the source's own majority sign; 4 to 27% of actor and partner variance in speed-dating): no mechanism, no manipulation.
- *The structure of the object and the game*: excludability and parity, dependence and alternatives, nonseparability and shared responsibility, public strengths and a prize that any winning pair shares, a common power or a superordinate goal. The same drive state gives opposite signs when these change (2.2).
- *Exposure and opportunity*: foci, baseline homophily, group size and composition [read: McPherson; 109].
- *Perception and attribution*: perceived rather than actual similarity [109]; the attribution gate [read: Lawler].
- *Category membership and the universality of the threat*: the out-group branch needs an identifiable rival [read: Sherif; Muis and Reeskens].
- *Legitimacy and expectation*: earned against assigned weights, equity pricing [105].
- *The medium*: interface-set gains, tie semantics [110, 106].
- *Structural theorems*: Cartwright and Harary's, and the potential-game identity, hold whatever the drives are.

**Verdict.** If "drives as primitives" means "drive state is sufficient to fix the sign", the reads refute it wherever they let the question be asked (shared object, strength gap, threat, two successive negatives, exchange: the same motive gives opposite signs when the structure changes); in the other two cases (similarity, status) the effect depends on an information regime or on the role of the tie, and no read source separates it from structure. If it means "drive states are among the necessary inputs", it stands but is unmeasured. The sign needs at least an object sort, a setting, a perception operand and an exposure operator beside any drive. **What would count as a test:** manipulate a need, appetite or threat with consent, with a pre-measured sign and the structure held fixed, and observe the sign change; none exists here (Sherif manipulated structure, covertly).

## What does it predict or control, and how well?

**What it predicts, by pair.**
- *Local sign of a hidden tie in a cumulative snapshot* (relation scale; 1999-2009; online): balanced accuracy 0.934 / 0.935 / 0.802 at embeddedness at least 25, which covers about 25%, 4% and 27% of edges and a smaller share of negative edges [106, computed there]; about 0.87 / 0.80 / 0.83 over all edges, read off figures [106]. Most of the lift over chance is person-level counts.
- *Which pair coalesces in a 4-3-2 triad* (group decision): the weak pair 60 to 67% in four samples across 1957 to the 2020s; an equity expectation is the best-supported mechanism [105].
- *Bloc membership of 17 states and 9 firms* (group): 15 of 17 for 1936 sizes, 16 of 17 for 1939, 6 of 7 for Unix given the first move, 18 of 19 states in the 1990 forecast as the authors count; chance probabilities 0.0047, 0.00027, 1/16, all conditional on a coding made after the events (for Unix the authors say the two attributes were fixed before the firms were sampled) [read; computed].
- *Intergroup hostility and its reversal* (group): Sherif's percentages; one camp.
- *Exchange chain* (membership and group): same-occasion self-report paths .58 and .78 [108]; no independent replication.
- *Initial attraction* (membership): actor and partner parts, 4 to 27% of variance; dyad part none [read].
- *Rally* (crowd): trust in institutions +0.098 on a 0-1 scale (d_z .53, a standardised paired change), interpersonal trust +0.012 (d_z .07), about nothing in out-group attitudes under a universal threat [read]; the cause of the 2020 rally (lockdown or case counts) is disputed [111: Schraff]. War exposure raised prosocial behaviour toward in-group partners by +0.24 to +0.25 SD and toward out-group partners by +0.04 SD (not significant) [111: Bauer et al.].

**Out-of-sample evidence.** Cross-site transfer for the balance-type core (loss under one point to Epinions and Slashdot, one to three on Wikipedia) [checked]; cross-era stability of the weak-pair share [105]; one forecast from the 1989 alignment, judged by its authors against 1991-92 events and not shown to be prospective (2.3) [read]. No time hold-out for the sign models, no replication of landscape theory found, no replication of Sherif with strangers and a pre-competition baseline [107], and nothing predicts a person's behaviour: every analysis is at tie, triad, bloc or population level.

**Effect sizes.** As above. Where an effect has been re-run it either held (weak pair) or shrank to 45% or less of its earlier mean (copying [107], worldview defence [051]).

**Failures and soft spots.** The enemy-of-my-enemy rule; learned status weights that do not transfer; the global balance partition not found by the heuristic used; the dyadic part of attraction unpredictable before meeting; the cohesion-to-investment link at n = 3 and the independent-lab disagreement on exchange forms [108]; the weak pair erased by earned weights, a rival triad or n of 4 or more [105]; the potential-game theorem failing outside symmetric (or per-actor-rescaled symmetric) propensities; a Galam claim that fails on the one instance that can be checked; accuracies reported with no intervals or time hold-outs [106].

**What it controls, if anything.** Nothing outside the lab or the camp. The reversal of hostility by superordinate goals was a **covert manipulation** of 22 boys (the staff designed the situations so the boys could not trace them to the staff; parents consented to the camp, not to the manipulation) [read]. Lawler's levers (equal power, mutual dependence through worse outside options, productive task form, voluntariness, shared responsibility) are between-group lab manipulations; making outside options worse to raise commitment is lock-in if applied to someone and manipulation unless the parties choose it jointly [108]. Axelrod and Bennett name where leverage would move an alignment (the pass between basins) and intervene on nothing. Leskovec et al. name the inference of one user's attitude to another from the network, which proceeds without that person's consent. The only large-scale lever found, composition and exposure of friendship groups [109], acts openly on tie formation, not on sign. **Consent-compatible in principle:** shared task design, equal-power structures and voluntariness (disclosable and agreeable), superordinate goals chosen jointly, composition of foci. **Manipulation:** covert common goals, engineered dependence, ranking interfaces that set loop gains without the user's knowledge.

**Bottom line.** What exists: a read, tested model that turns signed propensities and sizes into blocs, a learned local sign predictor dominated by person-level terms, a replicated coalition-choice regularity at n = 3, and conditional verbal laws for exchange and rivalry with lab and field tests. What does not: a sign derived from a measured drive, any intervention that changes a sign with the knowledge and consent of the people it acts on, and any evidence after 2020 on the sign laws themselves (the 2020 studies measure trust in institutions, not tie signs). The registry in section 6 is a form with fitted weights and a validity record per term; its drive content is the motive each law names, not an equation.
