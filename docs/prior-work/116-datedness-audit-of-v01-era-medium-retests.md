1. **Dated base.** v0.1 cites 46 empirical-result lines (the dropped L1793 excluded; L3978 and L4284 are dropped in the same sentence of v0.1 section 9 and are kept here because their sources are read through L4353-L4355 and L4439-L4442; excluding all three gives 44 lines and 3 post-2015 lines [validation]). The 2010-2015 row is partly inference: the three Friedkin-Jia-Bullo lines have only an upper bound (received November 2015) and L4460 only the publication years of the studies it cites, so by the rule in 2.3 they are unknown; the same rule moves the two Madhyastha lines, which also rest on an undated second sample (item 3), from "1999 or earlier" to unknown. Strictly the rows are 12, 11, 3, 4 and 14 unknown, with the simulation and the multi-decade line unchanged [validation]. The four post-2015 lines are one experiment on one campus in one month (Cantoni et al., 24 June to 15 July 2016).
2. **Publication year is not data year.** 25 of the 46 lines cite a paper published in 2014 or later. 15 of those 25 rest on data collected in 2009 or earlier or of unstated date, 1 spans 1945-2013, and 9 rest on data from 2010 on [derived from the table in 2.3]; of those 9, only Cantoni's 4 and Sels's 1 have data dated from 2010 on, and the other 4 have an upper bound or publication years only (item 1), so the strict count is 19, 1 and 5 [validation]. v0.1 reads as current because its sources are recent; its measurements are not.
3. **One cohort carries the dyad scale.** 10 lines (22% of all 46) trace to the Gottman laboratory, 8 of them to one cohort of about 125-130 Seattle-area newlywed couples, each videotaped once in a 15-minute conflict conversation; the other 2 (L1806, L3944, Madhyastha et al. 2011, abstract only) fit that cohort together with a second laboratory sample, "a study of couples (N = 124)" [abs; corrected in validation]. No open text gives the calendar years; the Gottman papers are closed, and the evidence I could open puts it at the early 1990s or earlier [inf]. The set-point and inertia definitions themselves (Cook et al. 1995) were built on an older sample, 73 couples studied in 1983 and 1987 (Gottman & Levenson 1992) [abs, both abstracts; identified in validation]. The 2016 refit (L4367-L4370) is **the same cohort re-analysed**, not a new sample, and the cohort's counts of 124 (Carrère & Gottman 1999 [abs]), 125, 129 and 130 couples are the same study under different exclusions; the ledger's own 124 (L1806, L3944) is Madhyastha's other sample, not the cohort [validation].
4. **Four findings have been re-tested on new samples in work published 2015 or later and held, each in a weaker or different form than v0.1 states** (section 3; the data in those studies mostly end before 2016, and only the nation-state study, which runs to September 2018, is certain to reach past it): (a) partner coupling in couples is near zero, in a laboratory cohort recorded before about 1994 [inf] and in smartphone studies from before November 2015 (Sels et al. 2016; Study 2 of Sels et al. 2020 is the same 50 couples, and that paper's Studies 1 and 3 add 79 and 101 couples, years unstated [validation]); (b) the *direction* of balance (unbalanced triads convert to balanced ones) holds in media-coded nation-state data to 2018, in a 2007-09 trading firm and on re-analysed older data, while forced two-camp polarisation does not; (c) a committed minority of about a quarter tips a convention in 20-30 person online groups (a different task from v0.1's threshold gate, run with confederates); (d) what group size does depends on the payoff structure: in 1989-91 groups of 40 and 100 contributed more than groups of 4 and 10 at a return of 0.30 but not at 0.75 [corrected in validation, section 3.5], and by 2012 going from 4 to 8 persons raised contribution at a low return and lowered it at a high one.
5. **Not re-tested on new data after 2015, as far as these searches reach:** the O-jive gate and the link from uninfluenced steady state to divorce (one cohort, in-sample, no cross-validation); the two-regime inertia (same cohort); the Friedkin-Johnsen map with measured weights (one lab, one population); the uninformed-individuals-return-control-to-the-majority result in any human group; Walker & Hill's fission result; Palla et al.'s size-dependent stability rule; Mathieu's cohesion-performance asymmetry. Section 3 lists what was searched (validation re-tested these with Europe PMC and Crossref queries only, and found none; see "What exists versus what does not").
6. **Drift is visible inside the evidence, not only between it and today.** Nonviolent campaign success fell from above 40% (1960s to about 2010; 65% in the 1990s) to under 34% since 2010, while mean peak participation fell from 2.7% (1990s) to 1.3% (since 2010) [read, Chenoweth 2020]; the 3.5% regularity's author writes that nobody knows whether it holds once people aim at it [read]. In the trading firm the share of classically balanced triads fell over the period, which the authors tie to the 2008-09 crash, and the last quarter-to-quarter transition was the least stable [read, Askarisichani 2019].
7. **Signs flip with the setting.** Four of v0.1's load-bearing results change sign with a moderator found in the sources: the crowd response to others' expected turnout (prior belief below or above 17%; Cantoni), the group-size effect on contribution (return to a contribution, 0.3 or 0.75; Nosenzo), the effect of membership turnover on community lifetime (community size; Palla), and the over- or under-representation of the enemy-of-my-enemy triangle (which platform; Leskovec). The first is already a setting operand in v0.1; the other three are not.
8. **Cross-check against the primary texts** (section 2): the numbers I checked, which v0.1 takes from Cantoni, de Haan-Rietdijk, Sels, Askarisichani, Leskovec, Friedkin-Jia-Bullo, Couzin and Chenoweth, match the source, with two exceptions found in validation: Leskovec's sentence on the under-represented ++- triad (75% and 50%) does not match the paper's own Table 3 (84%, 81% and 80% [derived]), and Chenoweth's 83% of successes below 3.5% is of nonviolent successes only. What was missing is era, medium, population and four scope statements: (i) Cantoni's experiment is June-July 2016; (ii) Palla's headline is a *size-dependent* rule that L4468 omits; (iii) the Friedkin-Johnsen correlation of 0.872 uses weights each subject reports **after** the discussion, so it is not a prospective prediction; (iv) the 3.5% rule is from maximalist campaigns only and its author flags reflexivity.
9. **Structural versus era-bound** (section 4): the theorems (balance structure theorem, Altafini dichotomy, certificate 6.1 and its Euler-turn proof, cascade 6.3, merge closure 6.4, the composition theorems in models/01) are era-invariant given their hypotheses; every magnitude that instantiates a hypothesis is era-, medium- or population-bound, and for the certificate and the composition theorems **no magnitude has been measured at all** (no gain matrix, no enemy weights).
10. **Validity column** (section 5): fields, rules and a populated table for 25 v0.1 terms, plus ledger back-fill values (`era`, `medium`, `population`) for all 46 lines. The new fields beyond models/01 section 5 are independence count, evidence type, sample status (in-sample, cross-validated, randomised), sign-stability, reflexivity and instrument-binding, because this audit found each of them mattering.
11. **Drive basis:** only the person-level *level* term (the set point) has support that survives a change of era and sample, and it is a baseline of expressed affect, not a measured drive. The load-bearing content that moves group outcomes in these data is structure, payoffs and composition (final section).

**Classes used for the audit** (also the vocabulary of section 5):

- **S** structural: a theorem, identity or counting fact; era-invariant given its hypotheses.
- **F** form: a functional shape fitted or assumed (relaxation to a set point, a gate, a threshold map); the shape can survive when the numbers do not.
- **M** magnitude: a fitted number, rate, share or effect size; bound to the era, medium and population that produced it.
- **I** instrument or definition: what counts as a tie, a negative tie, a campaign, a fission; results bound to the definition.
- A **sign flag** is added wherever a source shows the sign of an effect changing with a moderator.

### 2.2 Omissions and scope corrections found

1. **No cited line carried era or medium; four load-bearing dates were missing.** Cantoni: 24 June (surveys), 30 June (treatment), 1 July (march), 15 July 2016 (attendance) [read]. Askarisichani: 1 October 2007 to 31 March 2009 [read]. Leskovec: Epinions to 12 August 2003, Slashdot crawled February 2009, Wikipedia administrator elections to January 2008 [read]. Chenoweth: NAVCO 1.1 is 1900-2006, 1.2 is 1945-2014 (389 maximalist campaigns 1945-2013) [read].
2. **Palla's headline is a size flip, and L4468 omits it.** "For small community sizes the highest average life-span is at a stationarity value very close to one" (static membership is best); "the peak in <τ*> is shifted towards low ζ values for large communities" (continual turnover is best) [read; quotation restored in validation]. v0.1 should carry community size as a moderator of any leave or lifetime law, and models/01 Corollary 3.3 (merger tolerance falling with sizes) should be compared with it.
3. **Friedkin-Johnsen weights are measured after the outcome.** Each subject allocates 100 units of influence "post-discussion" [read]; W is then used with initial opinions to predict final opinions (rho = 0.872; opinion change rho = 0.811, with "no fitting"). That is a consistency check on a model whose weights are reported by the people whose opinions it predicts, not a forecast. v0.1 already says the weights are "self-reported after the outcome" (section 3); the correlation should not be quoted without it.
4. **The 3.5% regularity is a statement about maximalist campaigns, from a period when it was unknown.** Chenoweth: "The rule is derived from—and therefore applies to—only a specific kind of campaign" (overthrow or independence; quotation corrected in validation), "At least until 2013, the rule was not part of a conscious organizing strategy", and "No one knows whether the rule will hold if people consciously try to mobilize to achieve the threshold" [read]. v0.1 correctly says it is not a term; this is the reason to keep it out of the validity domain of any steering lever.
5. **The "contradiction" L3911 is two different comparisons.** Mathieu et al.'s abstract: a meta-analysis of 17 cross-lagged panel studies supports reciprocal relations; the asymmetry (cohesion to performance larger, growing over time) is from a new sample of 205 members in 57 student teams over 10 weeks, mostly replicated in a second student sample [abs]. Mullen & Copper's older meta-analysis is the performance-led side; its own abstract says the more direct effect "may be from performance to cohesiveness rather than from cohesiveness to performance" (Psychol Bull 115:210-227, 10.1037/0033-2909.115.2.210) [abs in validation; was sa]. The disagreement is between a meta-analysis and a student-simulation sample, in different decades; it is not a settled clash, and it is about performance, not splitting.
6. **The Gottman cohort's counts are one study.** 130 recruited, 124 coded in the 1999 first-three-minutes paper, 125 in the 1999 modelling paper (df 123), 129 in the 2016 refit, 17 divorced within six years [sa, UW News 1999 and the ledger]; the 124 newlyweds and the six-year follow-up are confirmed by Carrère & Gottman's abstract (Fam Process 38:293-301) [abs, validation]. v0.1 mixes the refit's 129 with the 1999 paper's F(1,123) as separate evidence; they share every couple. **Correction (validation):** the 124 in the ledger's Madhyastha lines (L1806, L3944) is not this count. Their abstract fits "data from a study of couples (N = 124) and newlyweds (N = 130)", two datasets, so those two lines rest on the cohort plus a second Gottman-laboratory sample whose identity and years are in the closed text.
7. **Spouse set-point correlation .60 (L4370) is cohort-bound and not separable from selection.** Couples who marry are alike on many things; the refit's authors report the correlation without a decomposition [read]. v0.1 §4.1 treats it as a dyad-level fact the lagged coupling does not produce; that is right, but the fact belongs to a 1990s Seattle newlywed sample.

**What is robust across the change of era.**
1. **Partner coupling is near zero.** Laboratory cohort recorded before about 1994 [inf], 6-second lag: 0.007 [-0.020, 0.035] (husband) and 0.018 [-0.007, 0.044] (wife) against inertia 0.60 below the set point [read]. The page I could reach adds three studies of 79 (lab, video-recall), 50 (smartphone sampling) and 101 couples (lab plus a week of sampling), temporal partner effects explaining 0 to 0.24% of variance in the sampling study, and 4-8% of participants beyond pseudo-couple bounds in the first two studies against 9-16% in the third [fetch, first half of the page only]. [Validation, full text read: all of this is confirmed; the 9-16% is the daily-life part of Study 3 (its lab part gives 3-5% and at most 3%). Study 1's 79 cohabiting couples were recruited at the University of Arizona. **Study 2 is the same 50 couples as Sels et al. 2016**, which the paper cites for it, so the three studies add two new samples, not three. No study states its calendar years.] This is the most robust empirical statement in the dyad cluster, and it is a near-null. It is bound to timescales from 6 seconds to about an hour: on daily self-reports Steele & Ferrer 2011 (Multivariate Behav Res 46:956-984, 10.1080/00273171.2011.625305) report "coupled influences in both positive and negative affect between partners", while Steele, Ferrer & Nesselroade 2014 (10.1007/s11336-013-9366-9) find independence the most likely style [abs, both; added in validation].
2. **Level beats dynamics.** Dejonckheere et al. 2019: across 15 studies and 1,777 people (smartphone sampling and daily diaries, 7 to 30 days, mean ages about 19 to 36, mostly European samples), "dynamic measures have little added value over mean levels of positive and negative affect" in predicting life satisfaction, depressive and borderline symptoms [read, abstract and data table]. [Validation: the abstract is confirmed; the data table was not re-opened. Sels et al. 2020 cite Dejonckheere et al. 2019 for their 50- and 101-couple samples, so the 15 studies are not independent of the Sels evidence.]
3. **What relationship science can predict at all.** Joel et al. 2020: 43 longitudinal couple datasets from 29 laboratories; relationship-specific self-reports predicted up to 45% of variance in relationship quality at baseline and up to 18% at the end of each study; change in quality over a study was "largely unpredictable" from any combination of self-report variables [abs]. **n and composition.** The unit is a dyad; spouses' set points correlate .60 [.46, .73] (a composition fact, cohort-bound); couples differ: per-couple intervals exclude zero for 10.1% and 6.2% (husband-to-wife, wife-to-husband) against 5% by chance, after shrinkage [read]; Sels et al. 2020 find coupling varies with timescale and context [abs].

**Verdict.** Forms F; magnitudes M, one cohort, in-sample, calendar years unverified: **quarantine** the gate K- and **hold as hypothesis** r- and r+ until a second sample refits them; the "decoupling" statement (6.2's dyad-scale content) is the one result here that two samples, two decades and two countries agree on. Sign flag: none found.

**Online signed networks (L4196, L4202-L4203, L4214-L4218, L4299).** ERA: Epinions "from the inception of the site in 1999 until August 12, 2003" (119,217 nodes, 841,000 edges, 85.0% positive); Slashdot crawled February 2009 (82,144 users, 549,202 edges, 77.4%; Slashdot Zoo launched 2002); Wikipedia administrator-election votes extracted from the January 2008 dump (2,794 elections, 103,747 votes, 7,118 users, 78.7%) [read, WWW paper]. Everything here is a re-analysis of old data or a different kind of "person" (nations, commenters) except the ICEWS study and the Bitcoin work.

**n and composition.** Triads are counted inside networks of 7,118 to 119,217 nodes (Leskovec), 66 (firm), 25-155 countries (Kirkley), 250 countries (ICEWS; 134 in the analysed core), 17 (Newcomb) and 34 (Zachary). **Verdict.** S for the structure theorem and the Z2 cycle-space invariance; **M and I** for every empirical frequency, because the sign is defined by the platform; sign flag **yes** for the +-- cell. Weak balance is the form to carry; strict balance is refuted as a total rule (v0.1 already holds this).

### 3.4 Crowd scale (scale: crowd; mobilisation and opinion)

## 4. Era-bound magnitudes and structural results

**Structural (S): hold in any era, given their hypotheses.** Cartwright-Harary structure theorem (1956 mathematics) and the Z2 invariance L4290; Theorem 6.4 (merge closure); Altafini's dichotomy and the Proskurnikov equilibrium line; the Euler-turn proof of 6.1; Theorem 6.3 and Lemma 6.3a (monotone cascade); the small-gain composition theorems 2.1, 3.1, 3.2 and Corollaries 3.3 and 3.4 of models/01; theta_c = 2 arccos(r); the counting fact that balance is rare; the Granovetter non-identifiability (two crowds with near-equal mean and spread, opposite outcomes). **What is not structural is whether a real group satisfies their hypotheses.** For 6.1 that is w_i > 2E_i (no enemy weights measured anywhere: Friedkin-Jia-Bullo cannot report a negative weight, L4450); for models/01 it is rho(Gamma) < 1 and the cross-gains (no gain matrix measured anywhere).

**Forms (F) with the shape surviving across samples:** relaxation to a set point with state-dependent inertia (one cohort); a gate on incoming signals (cohort; the crowd gate has Cantoni's sign problem); decoupling at short lags (two samples agree); weak rather than strict balance (four media).

**Magnitudes (M), each bound to the era, medium and population in section 3:** r- = 0.60-0.61 and r+ = 0.26-0.29, thresholds 0.07-0.08 on the SPAFF composite; K- = -7.71 against -5.36 (husband, one of ten in-sample tests); partner term below 0.044; FJ self-weight 0.643 and its centrality slope; Cantoni's -2.1 and +6.4 percentage points and the 17% crossing; the balance frequencies (75%, 50%, 0.75 stationary mass; validation: the 75% and 50% are Leskovec's sentence, which their Table 3 does not bear out, 2.1); Palla's lifetime curves; NAVCO's 88.89% and its post-2010 decline; Couzin's P = 0.018.

**Instrument-bound (I):** the sign of a tie (rank recode; absence; distrust votes; "foe" tags; alliance versus dispute); SPAFF composites (L4384); "maximalist campaign" and "peak participation"; "fission"; community by clique percolation.

**Sign-unstable:** Cantoni (prior level, protest type); Nosenzo (return); Palla (size); Leskovec's +-- cell (site).

**Drifting inside the evidence:** NAVCO success and participation; trading-firm balance over 2008-09, tied by the authors to the crash; the wedge measured in Russia 2015 and again 2020-22 (validation: the 2023 authors attribute their larger naive gap to "artificial deflation" of list estimates, not to a change in the wedge [abs]).

### 5.2 Rules for the status (all thresholds are editable settings)

1. **S lines** are exempt from decay. They carry a `hypotheses` list, and **each hypothesis is a separate M or I line with its own record.** A theorem whose every hypothesis is `none-found` is still true, and still empty for prediction. This is the situation of 6.1 and the models/01 theorems today.
2. **M lines** with `retest` equal to `none-found` or `same-data`, **or** `sample_status` equal to in-sample, enter v0.2 only as a **prior with a stated interval**, never as a point value in a prediction; v0.2 outputs that depend on them carry "domain: prior".
3. **Age.** `review_by` default = data end + 15 years for face-to-face laboratory magnitudes; + 5 for anything platform- or feed-mediated; + 3 after a rule becomes public (reflexive); S none [ours]. The motivation is the span in which this audit saw material change: a decade for campaign success, a quarter for firm balance during a crash, a year for the wedge in Russia (validation: the wedge case is weak, since the 2020-22 gap is read by its own authors as an instrument artefact, so no measured change of the wedge is shown, 3.4). These are defaults to be moved by data, not findings.
4. **Domain test.** A law is `in_domain` for a use if the use's medium equals the line's, the use's unit size lies within `size_range` widened by a factor set per class [ours: 1 for S, 2 for F, 1.5 for M], and the use's population differs from the evidence's only on factors the source says do not matter. Otherwise the algebra returns "out of domain", not a number.
5. **Sign flags force an operand.** Any term with `sign_stable = no` is carried as a setting operand in every equation that uses it (as v0.1 already does for the crowd gate's sigma and theta-bar) and its moderators and flip points go in the record.
6. **Independence counts.** Evidence counts are by sample. The Gottman cohort counts once, however many papers use it; a refit is `same-data`.
7. **Instrument-bound results** carry their instrument, and a use with a different instrument is out of domain until a bridge result exists (no source here bridges "absence of a significant positive tie" to "distrust vote").
8. **Publication-year illusion.** `era` is data years. A paper's year goes in a separate `pub_year` and is never used for age.

## Does it reduce to a drive basis?

"Drives as primitives" is a working hypothesis. Applied to the empirical base of v0.1:

**Reduces, in part, to a person-level term.**
- **The set point (D1).** The resting level of expressed affect in interaction is the strongest in-sample divorce correlate of ten parameters (F = 14.86 and 18.40) [ledger, closed text], and across 15 studies the average level of positive and negative affect carries the prediction of well-being while the dynamics add little [read, Dejonckheere 2019]. That is support for a person-level *level*. It is not support for a drive: it is a baseline of expressed behaviour, produced by drives and context alike, measured in one task; and spouses' baselines correlate .60, which is selection or shared context [read].
- **Closure to influence (FJ self-weight).** A person-level parameter, but it moves with structural position (it rises with influence centrality and shifts along the issue sequence) [read]; so it is not a stable trait.
- **Goal attraction.** A preference toward a target; in the only experiment, the preference was imposed by training fish [read].
- **The seven motives.** Measurable, varying across 42 cultures; no source read ties them to a group outcome.

**Does not reduce.**
- **The sign pattern.** The balance structure is graph mathematics; the empirical tendencies (weak balance, unbalanced-to-balanced conversion) hold across four media, but signs are defined by platforms (absence, distrust votes, foe tags) and the generators are unknown. In the firm the "negative" tie is the *absence* of a significant positive tie, so sparsity and closure give the same data as hostility.
- **Crowd thresholds.** Granovetter's thresholds are "net result of costs and benefits" with the origin of preferences excluded [ledger L3959]; Cantoni's sign flip comes from public-goods structure, crackdown probability and signalling [read]. These are payoffs.
- **Group-size effects.** Set by the return to a contribution, not by drive intensity [read].
- **Community stability, fission, campaign success, cohesion and performance.** Turnover and size (Palla), kin and household structure (Walker & Hill, abstract), participation share, defections, violent flanks and organisation (Chenoweth), reciprocal causation (Mathieu, abstract): structure and composition.
- **Updating rules.** Which rule dominates differs by population (10% against 50% Bayesian) [abs]: cognition by sample, not drive level.

**Net.** For the outcome in v0.1 (hold together or split, and who leaves), the audited evidence that moves group outcomes is structure, payoffs and composition, with the person's own baseline level as the one drive-like input that survives a change of sample, and it is only a prior. If the primitives are drives, then the drive parameters must be re-estimated with the era, because the one measured shift in a motive inventory (if the companion report holds) is within a year, and the one measured shift in a crowd outcome is within a decade.

## What does it predict or control, and how well?

**Membership (dyad; stay or leave).** In-sample association of the set point and of the husband's negativity threshold with divorce within about six years (F = 14.86, 18.40, 8.25; 17 of 125 divorced; ten parameters tested; no correction) [ledger]. **No cross-validation and no independent replication found.** Cross-validated divorce equations elsewhere lose much of their accuracy (89.7% to 69.3% correct; positive predictive value 65% to 29%, on the cross-sectional 1985 National Family Violence Survey [abs, fetch; read in validation]). Across 43 couple datasets the best self-report predictors explain up to 45% of baseline variance in relationship quality and up to 18% at the end of a study, and change is "largely unpredictable" [abs]. Coupling terms: near zero in two eras. Dynamics over means: little gain [read]. **Control:** none tested; no intervention in any of these data.

**Group (triads).** Directional: unbalanced triads convert to balanced ones, in a 2007-09 firm and in 1995-2018 nation data [read]; balance level is associated with performance ("an almost 30% increase in profits" from medium to high balance; no association in the bottom 25% of the data) [read], **correlational, firm-specific, and the absolute balance fell over 2008-09, which the authors tie to the crash**. Sign prediction from 23 learned features: 90-95% accuracy where guessing gives about 80%, within each of three 1999-2009 sites [read]; hand-set balance and status rules score lower (0.823 and 0.808 on Epinions, file 106). Fails: strict balance, the (-)(-) cell, forced polarisation (ICEWS). **Control:** none.

**Group (team).** Shiners: P = 0.018 in one animal experiment (11 informed fish plus 0/5/10 uninformed), no human test [read]. Friedkin-Johnsen: rho = 0.872 between predicted and observed final opinions, with weights the subjects reported afterwards [read]; settlement times 3-27 minutes. Fission: categorical tallies of causes, no predictor, no hold-out [abs].
