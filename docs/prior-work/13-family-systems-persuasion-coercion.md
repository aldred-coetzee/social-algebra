# Prior work 13: family systems, persuasion, coercive groups, radicalisation

Date: 2026-10-29. Strand: social-algebra. Not committed.

## Summary

This cluster is the largest body of informal "social algebra" in existence, and it was built by clinicians and propagandists who wanted to change groups, not describe them. Almost everything here is a **catalogue of named operators** (triangulate, detour, placate, double-bind, unfreeze, escalate symmetrically) acting on **a small set of operands** (person, dyad, triad, subsystem, boundary, role, group, outgroup). None of it is a calculus: there is no composition rule, no identity element, no stated closure, and almost no statement of which operators commute. The two nearest things to notation are Minuchin's family map and the genogram. Both are drawing conventions with no semantics. The nearest thing to a real dynamical rule is Bateson's schismogenesis (symmetrical and complementary escalation), which is a pair of feedback loops, and Watzlawick et al. building directly on it.

Evidence standing varies hard. The clinical frameworks (Bowen, Minuchin, Satir, Haley) are clinical-observational and largely untested as predictive systems. The experimental operators (Asch, Milgram, Sherif) have real effects but have been contested on mechanism and procedure. The coercive-control checklists (Lifton, Hassan) are descriptive, with Hassan's BITE having no validation evidence found. Radicalisation models are explicit that they are metaphors or mechanism lists.

**Method note and honesty about sources.** Every work below was confirmed to exist by at least one retrieved page. Most retrievals were Wikipedia or study-aid pages and the authors' own organisation pages, not the primary texts. Bibliographic details come from those pages and are as reliable as they are. "Unconfirmed" items are listed in a final section and are not asserted in the body. I did not read any primary text in full, so "their vocabulary" and "limits" are drawn from secondary summaries and flagged where thin.

## 1. Bowen family systems theory

- **Citation:** Bowen, M. (1978). *Family Therapy in Clinical Practice.* (Per Wikipedia's Bowen page, which says Bowen called it his most important publication.) Kerr, M. E. & Bowen, M. (1988). *Family Evaluation: An Approach Based on Bowen Theory.* Also Gilbert, R. M. (2004). *The Eight Concepts of Bowen Theory.* Leading Systems Press. (Validation: year corrected from 1992; 1992 is Gilbert's *Extraordinary Relationships*.) Bowen 1978 is Jason Aronson; Kerr & Bowen 1988 is W. W. Norton.
- **URLs checked:** https://www.thebowencenter.org/theory ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murray_Bowen ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triangulation_(psychology)
- **Their vocabulary:** The eight concepts, as listed by the Bowen Center: triangles; differentiation of self; nuclear family emotional process; family projection process; multigenerational transmission process; emotional cutoff; sibling position; societal emotional process. Core claim quoted from the Center: "A change in one person's functioning is predictably followed by reciprocal changes in the functioning of others." Also: anxiety, over-functioning/under-functioning, the member who accommodates most "absorbs" the system's anxiety (secondary source, sedonasky blog; treat as paraphrase).
- **Operands (translated):** person with a scalar "differentiation" level (a position on a spectrum between fused and separate); dyad; triad; the nuclear family as an emotional unit; generations (a chain); birth-order position (a role label); the society as a larger emotional system.
- **Operators (translated):** TRIANGULATE (when tension in dyad AB exceeds a threshold, draw in C to offload it; Bowen's triangle has two in the inside position and one out, and insiders shift); CUT OFF (remove a relationship to manage anxiety); PROJECT (parents' unresolved anxiety is placed on a child, who becomes the "identified patient"); TRANSMIT (carry a differentiation level to the next generation); OVER-/UNDER-FUNCTION (a reciprocal pair: if A over-functions B under-functions); ACCOMMODATE (absorb another's anxiety). **Formality:** concepts are named and informally directional. "Anxiety" acts like a conserved scalar that flows through the triangle. There is no formal calculus. The triangle is the closest thing to a primitive building block: Bowen's claim is that the triangle, not the dyad, is the stable unit.
- **Predictive/control claim:** Reciprocal change is "predictable". The therapeutic claim is that raising one member's differentiation and staying out of triangles changes the whole system. Empirical standing: the Bowen Center page itself hedges the evolutionary basis ("presumably") and says only that the theory "draws on knowledge from family research." I found no source in this session that reports controlled tests of the eight concepts. Wikipedia's Bowen page has no evidence section.
- **Limits:** differentiation is a clinical judgement, not a measured quantity in the sources I found; "anxiety" is not defined independently of the behaviour it explains; the nuclear-family frame is culturally specific.
- **Relevance to social-algebra:** Strong. Gives the **triad as primitive** (a good prompt for whether the algebra's atomic operand is a dyad or a triple), a conserved-quantity idea (anxiety flowing through the graph), and role pairs defined only by reciprocity (over/under-function).

## 2. Minuchin's structural family therapy and family-map notation

- **Citation:** Minuchin, S. (1974). *Families and Family Therapy.* Harvard University Press. (Cited on Wikipedia's structural family therapy page, ISBN 9780674292369.) The notation is attributed to p. 53 of that book by one secondary source (search result quoting a textbook figure caption, Figure 10.1 on Minuchin's symbols for family mapping; the book was not read).
- **URLs checked:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Structural_family_therapy ; https://genoeasy.de/blog-praxis-salvador-minuchin.php?lang=en ; the search-result summary of study aids.
- **Their vocabulary:** subsystem (spousal, parental, sibling); boundary (rigid, clear, diffuse) on a continuum from disengagement to enmeshment; hierarchy; coalition (stable, detouring, cross-generational); affiliation; over-involvement; conflict; detouring; triangulation. Joining, enactment, reframing are the therapist's moves.
- **Operands (translated):** subsystems (sets of members) nested in a family system, each with a boundary property; individual members; hierarchy levels (parent above child).
- **Operators (translated):** DRAW/ADJUST BOUNDARY (make rigid, clear or diffuse); FORM COALITION (two members align against a third, stable or cross-generational); DETOUR (a couple deflects conflict onto a child, either by overprotecting or scapegoating); TRIANGULATE (each parent demands the child side with them; study aids vary on terminology here, so treat the triangulation/unstable-coalition label as unresolved); AFFILIATE; OVER-INVOLVE; CONFLICT. Therapist operators: JOIN, UNBALANCE, RESTRUCTURE.
- **Notation, exactly as confirmed:** The only source I could fetch that states the line styles is GenoEasy, a genogram vendor, which states: clear boundary is a **dashed** line, diffuse boundary is **dotted**, rigid boundary is **solid**. A search summary of a genogram vendor's guide adds that a **double line marks affiliation** and a **triple line marks over-involvement**. One flashcard set gives the symbol set as: boundary types, coalition, conflict, detouring, over-involvement, with affiliation as `=`, coalition as a brace-type mark, and clear boundary as a dashed line. GenoEasy says its own thick blue line for coalition is its own convention, not Minuchin's. I could NOT confirm a zigzag for conflict, the coalition and detouring glyphs, or the exact subsystem outline conventions. GenoEasy warns of a symbol clash: a dotted line means "distant" in McGoldrick genograms and "diffuse" in Minuchin maps. The nature of the notation: members are drawn as boxes or circles, subsystems enclosed, and boundaries drawn as lines between enclosures, so it is a diagram of **set membership plus labelled edges**.
- **Predictive/control claim:** Structure explains symptoms (a symptom is read as the family's way of preserving a structure); changing structure removes the symptom. Empirical standing: not established in the sources retrieved. The Wikipedia page asserts no evidence base. I did not find outcome studies in this session.
- **Limits:** the map is the therapist's inference, not an observed record; the boundary is a continuum drawn with three discrete line styles; "normal" structure assumes a Western two-generation hierarchy; no composition rules.
- **Relevance:** The most direct precedent for the user's aim, since it is an actual notation with operands (subsystems) and typed edges. It shows the gap: edges are **states** (relations), the verbs (detour, coalesce) are in the prose, not in the notation. Social-algebra would turn Minuchin's edge types into operators and make "transition between maps" a first-class object.

## 3. Bateson: schismogenesis and the double bind

- **Citation:** Bateson, G. (1936). *Naven.* Cambridge University Press (reissued with new epilogue 1958). Bateson, G. (1972). *Steps to an Ecology of Mind.* Bateson, G., Jackson, D. D., Haley, J., & Weakland, J. (1956). "Toward a theory of schizophrenia." *Behavioral Science*, 1, 251-264. (The Wikipedia double bind page gives these details and no DOI.)
- **URLs checked:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schismogenesis ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_bind
- **Licence note:** summary prose in this section follows the Wikipedia articles 'Schismogenesis' and 'Double bind' (CC BY-SA 4.0).
- **Their vocabulary:** schismogenesis: "a process of differentiation in the norms of individual behaviour resulting from cumulative interaction between individuals" (quotation from the Wikipedia article 'Schismogenesis', CC BY-SA 4.0, seen via a search summary). Symmetrical vs complementary. Double bind with its listed ingredients.
- **Operands (translated):** two parties (individuals or groups) each with a behavioural norm; for double bind, a "subject" and authority figure(s), plus messages at two logical levels.
- **Operators (translated):** SYMMETRICAL ESCALATE (similar moves answered with similar moves: boasting begets boasting, arms race); COMPLEMENTARY ESCALATE (opposing, interdependent moves: assertion begets submission begets more assertion; the Tannen example is one party getting louder and the other quieter); DAMP (rituals such as Iatmul *naven* counter or balance schismogenesis; Bateson also suggested the two forms might balance each other); DOUBLE-BIND (issue a primary injunction plus a conflicting higher-level injunction, plus a tertiary one blocking escape, in a recurring intense relationship with no ability to metacommunicate). **Formality:** schismogenesis is the closest thing to a **dynamical rule** in the cluster: a positive feedback loop with a named sign (same-sign vs opposite-sign coupling). It is quantitatively informal, but structurally close to coupled-variable models. Double bind is a message-pair constraint, defined by its ingredients; it is formalisable as a logic of level-conflicting commands.
- **Predictive/control claim:** Both schismogenic forms are self-destroying unless checked; control comes from interventions that halt escalation (Bateson suggested studying methods that parties could use). Empirical standing: the Wikipedia pages give illustrative examples (arms race, class struggle, conversation) not tests. For double bind as a cause of schizophrenia, my retrieved page carried no empirical critique, although it lists a Koopmans (1997) review whose content I did not read. The double bind as a *cause* of schizophrenia is not supported and the aetiological claim is abandoned in modern psychiatry; Koopmans (1997) reviews it (validation: resolved from the validator's knowledge; the concept survives as a communication pattern, not an aetiology).
- **Limits:** examples are post hoc; no measurement procedure for "escalation"; double bind is defined by context and hard to falsify.
- **Relevance:** High. Schismogenesis suggests the algebra needs **sign-typed reciprocal operators** (same-sign vs opposite-sign response), and that an operator pair can be a loop with a growth law. Double bind suggests a typing/levels system (messages about messages).

## 4. Watzlawick, Beavin & Jackson, Pragmatics of Human Communication

- **Citation:** Watzlawick, P., Beavin [Bavelas], J., & Jackson, D. D. (1967). *Pragmatics of Human Communication: A Study of Interactional Patterns, Pathologies and Paradoxes.* W. W. Norton, New York. (Wikipedia, Paul Watzlawick page.)
- **URL checked:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Watzlawick
- **Licence note:** the axiom list and section prose follow the Wikipedia article 'Paul Watzlawick' (CC BY-SA 4.0); primary source Watzlawick, Beavin & Jackson (1967).
- **Their vocabulary:** five axioms: (1) one cannot not communicate; (2) every message has content and relationship aspects, the latter being metacommunication; (3) the nature of a relationship depends on punctuation of communicational sequences; (4) digital and analogic modalities; (5) interchanges are symmetrical (equality) or complementary (difference). [from abstract/standard account]
- **Operands (translated):** two (or more) communicators; messages with a content part and a relationship part; a sequence of events.
- **Operators (translated):** COMMUNICATE (any behaviour is a message, so the operator cannot be the identity: there is no null move); DEFINE-RELATIONSHIP (every message commands how it is to be taken); PUNCTUATE (assign a cause/effect cut in a circular sequence: each party tends to see the other as the cause); SYMMETRIC-MOVE / COMPLEMENTARY-MOVE (inherited from Bateson). **Formality:** axioms are stated as general laws; punctuation is the useful formal idea (a choice of where to cut a loop into cause and effect).
- **Predictive/control claim:** pathologies arise from disagreement in punctuation and rigid symmetrical or complementary patterns; paradoxical injunctions are used therapeutically. Empirical standing: the Wikipedia page has no criticism section; I found no testing evidence in the retrieved material. The axioms are conceptual.
- **Limits:** axiom 1 is almost definitional, so unfalsifiable; "relationship level" is never operationalised.
- **Relevance:** "You cannot have a null operator" is a real constraint for the algebra (no identity element in the interaction space unless silence is itself typed). Punctuation is a good precedent for the idea that **the same history parses into different operator sequences depending on the observer**, which the algebra must either permit or deliberately fix.

## 5. Satir's communication stances

- **Citation:** Satir, V. (1972). *Peoplemaking.* Science and Behavior Books. (attributed by secondary sources; a 2021 *Frontiers in Neuroergonomics* paper cites Satir 1972.)
- **URLs checked:** search results only; https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neuroergonomics/articles/10.3389/fnrgo.2021.768386/full appeared in results but I did not read it. https://mediationblog.kluwerarbitration.com/2025/06/12/mediation-and-the-satir-categories
- **Their vocabulary:** under stress people take a stance: **placater**, **blamer**, **computer** (super-reasonable), **distracter** (irrelevant), and the fifth, **leveler** (congruent). Secondary sources say stances are reflexive and not fixed personality types, and that one party's stance can pull a complementary or opposing stance from the other. Labels vary across sources.
- **Operands (translated):** a person-in-context, described by how they treat three things: self, other, context.
- **Operators (translated):** each stance is a **response function**: PLACATE (ignore self), BLAME (ignore other), COMPUTE (ignore both and feeling), DISTRACT (ignore everything relevant), LEVEL (acknowledge all three). Pairings (blamer meets placater) are a small interaction table. **Formality:** a 4+1 typology; the interaction table is implicit, not given.
- **Predictive/control claim:** stance under stress is predictable; congruence is the therapeutic target. Empirical standing: thin. A scale to measure the Satir model's self-transformation exists (Perceived Self-Transformation Scale, 17 items, 500 Chinese subjects, from search summary), but I found no psychometric validation of the five stances themselves.
- **Limits:** typology from clinical observation; the typology is described as stress-conditioned, so it cannot predict calm interaction.
- **Relevance:** gives an **operator-as-policy** (stance = a standing response function that fixes how a person acts on inputs) as opposed to an operator-as-act. This is useful: the algebra may need both acts and dispositions.

## 6. Haley: strategic therapy and power in families

- **Citation:** Haley, J. (1963). *Strategies of Psychotherapy.* Grune & Stratton. Haley, J. (1973). *Uncommon Therapy: The Psychiatric Techniques of Milton H. Erickson, M.D.* W. W. Norton. Haley, J. (1976). *Problem-Solving Therapy.* Jossey-Bass. Perverse triangle: Haley, J. (1967). "Toward a theory of pathological systems." In G. H. Zuk & I. Boszormenyi-Nagy (eds.), *Family Therapy and Disturbed Families.* Science and Behavior Books. (Validation: the 1977 date on the triangulation page is the reprint in Watzlawick & Weakland, *The Interactional View*, not the original.)
- **URLs checked:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jay_Haley ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triangulation_(psychology)
- **Licence note:** section prose follows the Wikipedia articles 'Jay Haley' and 'Triangulation (psychology)' (CC BY-SA 4.0).
- **Their vocabulary:** strategic therapy, in which the therapist takes responsibility for directly influencing people (Haley 1963, 1973, as summarised in the Wikipedia articles 'Jay Haley' and 'Triangulation (psychology)', CC BY-SA 4.0). Perverse triangle: two people at different hierarchical or generational levels form a coalition against a third, for example a covert parent-child alliance undermining the other parent. Minuchin called the same pattern the rigid triangle; Bowen the pathological triangle (per the triangulation page).
- **Operands (translated):** persons located on a hierarchy (generation or power level); triads.
- **Operators (translated):** CROSS-LEVEL COALESCE (the perverse triangle: join across a hierarchy against the third); DIRECT INFLUENCE (the therapist designs an intervention per problem); and, from the Erickson work, indirect and paradoxical directives (named in the book title only; I did not retrieve their definitions). **Formality:** a hierarchy ordering is an explicit structural primitive, which is rare in the cluster.
- **Predictive/control claim:** problems are maintained by hierarchy violations; a therapist can intervene to realign them. Empirical standing: Wikipedia's page makes no evidence claim, and, per the Wikipedia article 'Jay Haley' (CC BY-SA 4.0), Haley disclaimed having a theory of therapy.
- **Limits:** power is treated as hierarchy position, not as an exchange; my retrieved sources did not reach Haley's actual treatment of power in families. Search for it failed (returned unrelated results). **Unconfirmed in detail**.
- **Relevance:** the perverse triangle is the cleanest example of an operator whose **legality depends on the operands' ranks** (coalition across a level boundary). This suggests a typed or ordered operand domain.

## 7. Genograms (McGoldrick)

- **Citation:** McGoldrick, M. & Gerson, R. (1985). *Genograms in Family Assessment.* W. W. Norton. 2nd ed. (1999, with Shellenberger) and 3rd ed. (2008, with Petry): *Genograms: Assessment and Intervention.* 4th ed. (2020): McGoldrick, Gerson & Petry, *Genograms: Assessment and Treatment.* (Validation: the earlier note here, that "Assessment and Intervention" was only a subtitle of the 2016 *Genogram Casebook*, was wrong; it is the title of the 2nd and 3rd editions, which the Casebook accompanies.)
- **URL checked:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genogram
- **Licence note:** section prose follows the Wikipedia article 'Genogram' (CC BY-SA 4.0).
- **Their vocabulary:** per the Wikipedia article 'Genogram' (CC BY-SA 4.0), a diagram of simple symbols for gender and various lines for family relationships. The page says standardisation was a joint effort across fields and names contributors in psychiatry (Bowen, Bradt, Guerin, Stagoll, Tomm), psychology, social work (Carter, McGoldrick, Hartman, Pinderhughes) and family medicine. It does not describe the glyphs.
- **Operands (translated):** persons by sex; couples; children by birth order; across generations.
- **Operators (translated):** none; it is a **pure data structure** (a labelled graph with generation layers). Relationship lines (close, conflictual, distant, cutoff) are edge types.
- **Predictive/control claim:** none; it is an assessment tool. I did not retrieve its evidence base.
- **Limits:** static snapshot; one clash of symbol meaning with Minuchin's maps (dotted: distant vs diffuse, per GenoEasy).
- **Relevance:** the **de facto standard graph encoding** of a family, with a user community. A useful import format or at least a test that the algebra's states can be rendered as a genogram.

## 8. Yalom's therapeutic factors in groups

- **Citation:** Yalom, I. D. & Leszcz, M. (2005). *The Theory and Practice of Group Psychotherapy* (5th ed.). Basic Books. (Wikipedia group psychotherapy page.)
- **URL checked:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_psychotherapy
- **Licence note:** the list of twelve factors and section prose follow the Wikipedia article 'Group psychotherapy' (CC BY-SA 4.0); primary source Yalom & Leszcz (2005).
- **Their vocabulary:** twelve factors as listed on that page (Yalom's own list is often given as eleven, so counts vary by source): universality, altruism, instillation of hope, imparting information, corrective recapitulation of the primary family group, development of socialising techniques, imitative behaviour, cohesiveness, existential factors, catharsis, interpersonal learning, self-understanding.
- **Operands (translated):** member; group; therapist; the group's cohesion (a group-level property).
- **Operators (translated):** these are **mechanisms of benefit**, not acts: UNIVERSALISE, GIVE (altruism), MODEL (imitation), RECAPITULATE (re-enact family roles on the group), FEEDBACK, CATHARTICALLY EXPRESS. Cohesiveness is described as a candidate primary factor from which others flow. **Formality:** a list; no structure.
- **Predictive/control claim:** the factors explain why group therapy helps. Standing: the Wikipedia page reports no direct tests of the individual factors, only a study (Joyce, Piper & Ogrodniczuk 2007) of alliance and cohesion as predictors of outcome in short-term group therapy.
- **Limits:** self-report-derived, benefit-focused (no harm operators), and the group is a managed setting.
- **Relevance:** the **positive pole**: these are the same primitive operations (modelling, feedback, belonging) that coercive groups use for harm. A neutral algebra should have one operator set where benign and coercive uses differ in parameter or context, not in kind.

## 9. Lifton's eight criteria of thought reform

- **Citation:** Lifton, R. J. (1961). *Thought Reform and the Psychology of Totalism.* (Original source; named by the sources I read, not itself retrieved.) The eight-criteria text I read is Lifton, R. J., "Cults: Religious Totalism and Civil Liberties," in *The Future of Immortality and Other Essays for a Nuclear Age* (Basic Books, 1987), as reproduced by Steven Hassan.
- **URL checked:** https://stevenhassan.freedomofmind.com/p/robert-jay-liftons-eight-criteria-of-thought-reform-brainwashing-mind-control
- **Their vocabulary:** milieu control; mystical manipulation (planned spontaneity); demand for purity; cult of confession; sacred science; loading the language; doctrine over person; dispensing of existence.
- **Operands (translated):** a member; the group environment (information channels, language, doctrine); the member's inner communication.
- **Operators (translated):** CONTROL CHANNEL (milieu control); STAGE-MANAGE (planned spontaneity); PURIFY/SPLIT (pure vs impure); CONFESS (guilt and shame via criticism and self-criticism); SACRALISE (doctrine claiming scientific truth); COMPRESS LANGUAGE (loaded terms, slogans); SUBORDINATE EXPERIENCE (doubt = own evil); DEHUMANISE/DENY EXISTENCE (non-members tainted). **Formality:** a checklist of properties of a total environment, not a sequence of operators. Eight orthogonal-seeming features with no ordering.
- **Predictive/control claim:** the more of the eight a group exhibits, the more totalist it is. Standing: descriptive and derived from interviews of people with experience of Chinese thought reform; I did not retrieve tests of predictive validity. Wikipedia's coercive-persuasion page (wording follows the Wikipedia article 'Coercive persuasion', CC BY-SA 4.0) records that the APA's board in 1987 rejected the DIMPAC task force report on rigour grounds, and that US courts have rejected mind-control testimony under Frye (*US v. Fishman*, 1990). That context applies to the whole "brainwashing" lineage here (Lifton, Hassan, Singer), though Lifton's work is distinct from Singer's.
- **Limits:** checklist with no thresholds; applied retrospectively; critics argue the concept generalises worries about some groups to all (this critique is aimed at Hassan-style theorists, per Gallagher on the Hassan page).
- **Relevance:** good source of **operators on the information environment** (channel control, language compression), which is a distinct class from interpersonal operators.

## 10. Hassan's BITE model

- **Citation:** Hassan, S. *Combating Cult Mind Control* (1988, Park Street Press), where BITE is first set out (validation: corrected; the draft placed it in *Freedom of Mind*, 2012, which restates it); *Releasing the Bonds* (2000); *Freedom of Mind* (2012); Hassan & Shah (2019), *Journal of Forensic and Legal Medicine*. Hassan's 2020 doctoral dissertation (Fielding Graduate University) proposed a BITE scale; that is author-developed, not independent validation.
- **URLs checked:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Hassan ; https://freedomofmind.com/cult-mind-control/bite-model/ (the second page was a 404 on my first attempt at a different path; the fetch that returned content was the `/cult-mind-control/bite-model/` path, which gave the summary below).
- **Their vocabulary:** control of Behaviour, Information, Thought, Emotion. Used "to measure degrees of exploitative control or undue influence". The organisation's page gave example indicators only for information (deception; teaching distrust of critics and former members) and emotion (installing phobias about questioning or leaving).
- **Operands (translated):** a member; the group's control over four domains.
- **Operators (translated):** four control-classes (CONTROL-BEHAVIOUR, -INFORMATION, -THOUGHT, -EMOTION). I did not retrieve the full indicator lists. **Formality:** a four-way taxonomy with many items under each.
- **Predictive/control claim:** a group scoring high is likely exerting undue influence. **Empirical standing: no validation found.** The Hassan page reports criticism (Gallagher, Shupe and Bromley, Langone) and does not describe any empirical test. The organisation page makes no validation claim and says not every group uses every element.
- **Limits:** advocacy origin; unvalidated; overlaps Lifton heavily.
- **Relevance:** useful as a cross-check of Lifton: the four domains are a decent **partition of what an influencer acts on** (what you do, what you know, what you think, what you feel).

## 11. Schein's coercive persuasion (and the link to Lewin)

- **Citation:** Schein, E. H., Schneier, I., & Barker, C. H. (1961). *Coercive Persuasion.* W. W. Norton. (Cited in the Cultic Studies Journal results; stages attributed there.)
- **URLs checked:** search results from library.internationalculticstudies.org, edbatista.com PDF title "Edgar Schein Kurt Lewin's Change Theory" (listed, not read); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coercive_persuasion (did not cover Schein; covers Singer and DIMPAC).
- **Their vocabulary:** unfreezing (weaken the equilibria supporting beliefs, esp. beliefs about self, by fear, guilt, social ostracism, sleep deprivation, restraint); changing (alter cognitive structure by controlled information, dissonance, peer and authority influence); refreezing (stabilise and integrate; social confirmation and reinforcement). One secondary source says Schein borrowed the framework from Lewin's change model.
- **Operands (translated):** a person's belief-structure (self-beliefs especially); a social environment that confirms or disconfirms.
- **Operators (translated):** UNFREEZE, CHANGE (reshape), REFREEZE, as a **three-phase composite**. Component moves: ISOLATE, DESTABILISE (guilt, fear), CONTROL INFORMATION, PEER-PRESSURE, CONFIRM. **Formality:** the cleanest ordered sequence in the cluster: a three-step pipeline, but each step is a bag of acts.
- **Predictive/control claim:** the sequence produces durable change if refreezing is socially supported. Standing: Schein's work drew on observational study of prisoners of the Chinese; the broader coercive-persuasion thesis was contested (see APA/DIMPAC and Frye notes above). McCauley & Moskalenko list "unfreezing" as a radicalisation mechanism too (see section 15).
- **Limits:** observed under extreme control (captivity); extension to voluntary groups is the contested step.
- **Relevance:** gives the algebra a **phase** concept: the same operator (confirm) does different things before and after the belief structure is made plastic. It argues the operand state needs a plasticity variable.

## 12. Cialdini's principles of influence

- **Citation:** Cialdini, R. B. (2001). *Influence: Science and Practice* (4th ed.). Allyn and Bacon. First published 1984 as *Influence: The Psychology of Persuasion* (per Wikipedia).
- **URL checked:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Influence:_Science_and_Practice
- **Licence note:** the list of principles and section prose follow the Wikipedia article 'Influence: Science and Practice' (CC BY-SA 4.0); primary source Cialdini.
- **Their vocabulary:** reciprocation; commitment and consistency (foot-in-the-door, low-ball); social proof; liking; authority; scarcity; unity (added in *Pre-Suasion*, 2016, and the 2021 expanded edition of *Influence*; the original list was six). Door-in-the-face is a named reciprocation tactic.
- **Operands (translated):** a target and an influencer; a request; the target's prior commitments; the reference group.
- **Operators (translated):** GIVE-FIRST (obligates), ASK-SMALL-THEN-LARGE (commitment), ASK-LARGE-THEN-SMALL (door-in-the-face), SHOW-OTHERS (social proof), BE-LIKED (similarity, praise, shared goals), INVOKE-AUTHORITY, RESTRICT (scarcity), INVOKE-SHARED-IDENTITY (unity). **Formality:** seven named levers; each is a rule of the form "if X then compliance probability rises".
- **Predictive/control claim:** each lever raises compliance. The Wikipedia page lists reviews (Carrell 1986; Boster 1988) but does not summarise them and itself flags the empirical-support claims as uncited. I did not retrieve replication data. **Replication status is unconfirmed**.
- **Limits:** effect sizes vary by principle; the page contains no moderators.
- **Relevance:** a **ready-made inventory of single-target one-step operators**, many of which have a counterpart as group-level operators (social proof is "show the group's state to a member").

## 13. Bernays and Ellul on propaganda

- **Citation:** Bernays, E. L. (1928). *Propaganda.* Horace Liveright. Ellul, J. (1962 French, *Propagandes*; English 1965; Vintage 1973). *Propaganda: The Formation of Men's Attitudes.* Trans. K. Kellen & J. Lerner.
- **URLs checked:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propaganda_(book) ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propaganda:_The_Formation_of_Men%27s_Attitudes
- **Licence note:** section prose and Ellul's four axes follow the Wikipedia articles 'Propaganda (book)' and 'Propaganda: The Formation of Men's Attitudes' (CC BY-SA 4.0).
- **Their vocabulary:** Bernays: "invisible government" (1928); "engineering consent" is from his later essay "The Engineering of Consent" (1947, *Annals of the American Academy of Political and Social Science*, 250, 113-120), not from *Propaganda*; persuasion through hidden motives rather than reason. Ellul: political vs sociological; agitation vs integration; vertical vs horizontal; rational vs irrational. Propaganda aims to provoke action, not change opinion; works by continuous surrounding pressure; needs an organisation; builds on existing stereotypes and myths; depends on mass society.
- **Operands (translated):** a mass or public; opinion-shapers (Bernays' unseen mechanism-manipulators); existing myths and stereotypes; a mass-media environment.
- **Operators (translated):** Bernays: APPEAL-TO-HIDDEN-MOTIVE; NARROW-CHOICE (emotional appeals reduce choices to binary, per Wikipedia summary). Ellul: AGITATE (mobilise to overturn), INTEGRATE (stabilise norms), PUSH-DOWN (vertical), SPREAD-FROM-WITHIN (horizontal, via internal debate), SATURATE (continuous pressure). **Formality:** typologies, not calculi. Ellul's four axes give a 2x2x2x2 taxonomy of propaganda *types*.
- **Predictive/control claim:** Bernays claims engineered consent is both possible and necessary. Ellul claims propaganda is effective and erodes personal judgement. Standing: both are essays. Wikipedia notes critical analysis of Bernays is "surprisingly scarce", and the Ellul page carries a style warning. No empirical tests were retrieved.
- **Limits:** mass-scale, one-to-many; says little about small-group dynamics; Ellul's causal claims are unfalsifiable.
- **Relevance:** the **one-to-many** and **horizontal diffusion** operator families, and the distinction agitate/integrate (change vs preserve a state) which is a useful operator-polarity.

## 14. Kelman's three processes (added)

- **Citation:** Kelman, H. C. (1958). "Compliance, identification, and internalization: Three processes of attitude change." *Journal of Conflict Resolution*, 2(1), 51-60.
- **URL checked:** https://hckelman.scholars.harvard.edu/publications/compliance-identification-and-internalization-three-processes-attitude-change (search result only; summary from the search tool).
- **Their vocabulary:** compliance (go along for approval or to avoid disapproval; private view unchanged); identification (adopt behaviour to keep a valued relationship); internalisation (accept because the content fits one's own values).
- **Operands (translated):** public behaviour vs private belief (two coordinates); the influencing source.
- **Operators (translated):** three depth-levels of one operator, INFLUENCE_c, INFLUENCE_i, INFLUENCE_int, distinguished by what the target's public and private states end up as.
- **Predictive/control claim:** the persistence of change depends on which process produced it; standing: a textbook staple (the summary says so), no replication data retrieved.
- **Relevance:** supplies the **public/private split** the algebra will need: operand state is a pair (what I do, what I hold).

## 15. Radicalisation: Moghaddam, McCauley & Moskalenko

- **Citation:** Moghaddam, F. M. (2005). "The staircase to terrorism: A psychological exploration." *American Psychologist*, 60(2), 161-169. DOI 10.1037/0003-066X.60.2.161 (validation: matches volume, issue and pages; consistent with APA's DOI scheme). McCauley, C. & Moskalenko, S. (2008). "Mechanisms of political radicalization: Pathways toward terrorism." *Terrorism and Political Violence*, 20(3), 415-433. McCauley, C. & Moskalenko, S. (2011). *Friction: How Radicalization Happens to Them and Us.* Oxford University Press (validation: year corrected from 2009; revised edition 2017 as *Friction: How Conflict Radicalizes Them and Us*). McCauley, C. & Moskalenko, S. (2017). "Understanding political radicalization: The two-pyramids model." *American Psychologist*, 72, 205-216. McCauley & Moskalenko (2014). "Toward a profile of lone wolf terrorists." *Terrorism and Political Violence*, 26(1), 69-85 (validation: journal and pages confirmed from the validator's knowledge of the literature).
- **URLs checked:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staircase_model ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radicalization ; https://www.start.umd.edu/publication/understanding-political-radicalization-two-pyramids-model (search result).
- **Licence note:** the staircase floor list (Moghaddam 2005) and section prose follow the Wikipedia articles 'Staircase model' and 'Radicalization' (CC BY-SA 4.0).
- **Their vocabulary:** Staircase: ground floor (perceived injustice), first floor (look for options), second (displaced aggression/blame), third (moral engagement with a group), fourth (us-versus-them, secrecy, cut off from family), fifth (inhibition overcome by categorisation and distancing). Twelve mechanisms (this is the *Friction* 2011 list; the 2008 paper's twelve differ in labels, e.g. "extremity shift in like-minded groups", "extreme cohesion under isolation and threat" and three kinds of competition): personal grievance, group grievance, slippery slope, love, risk and status, unfreezing (individual); polarisation, isolation, competition (group); jiu-jitsu politics, hatred, martyrdom (mass). Two pyramids: opinion (neutral up to personal moral obligation to act violently) and action (inert up to committing terrorist acts), with the premise that most people with radical views never act.
- **Operands (translated):** an individual at a position on a staircase or pyramid level; a group (with a within-group polarisation state); a state and its challenger; the mass public.
- **Operators (translated):** ASCEND (step up, with fewer exits each floor); DISPLACE BLAME; OFFER MORAL ENGAGEMENT; CUT OFF; CATEGORISE and DISTANCE (to overcome inhibition); POLARISE (group discussion); ISOLATE (validation: corrected; group isolation is extreme cohesion under isolation and threat, where an isolated, threatened group raises cohesion and conformity pressure, not "letting persuasive members set the agenda"); COMPETE (groups outbid rivals); PROVOKE (jiu-jitsu: provoke a crackdown that alienates moderates); DEHUMANISE (hatred); MARTYR (signal the absolute value of the cause). **Formality:** staircase is an ordered state ladder with transitions; mechanisms are a list; pyramids are a two-axis state space (opinion x action). The two-pyramid separation is the best structural contribution: opinion and action are different coordinates.
- **Predictive/control claim:** Moghaddam says the staircase is a metaphor organising existing knowledge and not a formal theory; the Staircase page reports a review by Lygre (2011, *Scandinavian Journal of Psychology*) that found most processes have some support but little evidence of movement between floors, and suggested the staircase may be the wrong metaphor. The two-pyramids model was proposed in response to criticism that a single-track "conveyor belt" model is wrong. I did not retrieve outcome-prediction data.
- **Limits:** terrorism-specific; retrospective; base-rate problem (few radicals act); metaphors of sequence rather than demonstrated stages.
- **Relevance:** the **state-space** idea (two independent coordinates, opinion and action) and a good list of **group-level operators with inter-group coupling** (competition, jiu-jitsu).

## 16. Classic experimental operators: Asch, Milgram, Sherif, Allport, Zimbardo

**Asch conformity.** Asch (1951; 1955 *Scientific American*; 1956 *Psychological Monographs*). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asch_conformity_experiments (wording follows the Wikipedia article 'Asch conformity experiments', CC BY-SA 4.0). Design: confederates unanimously give a wrong line judgement. Per the page, 35.7% of responses matched the wrong majority and 74% of participants gave at least one wrong answer; 26% never did. Variations: one true partner cut conformity to about 5%; group size effects saturated after three opponents; written responses reduced conformity. Replication/critique: Franzen & Mader (2023, *PLOS ONE*) replicated and extended; Turner et al. argue for referent informational influence, not normative; Griggs (2015) says textbooks omit the independence findings; Bond & Smith (1996, *Psychological Bulletin* 119, 111-137): 133 studies, 17 countries; conformity higher in collectivist cultures and declining in the US since the 1950s. **Operators:** UNANIMOUS-MAJORITY (apply pressure), DISSENT-SEED (a single ally breaks it), SIZE-SATURATION (pressure is non-linear in group size). **Operands:** subject, majority of size n, a private judgement. **Formality:** quantitative, the most formal input in the cluster: conformity rate as a function of n, unanimity, response mode.

**Milgram obedience.** Milgram, S. (1963). "Behavioral Study of Obedience." *Journal of Abnormal and Social Psychology*, 67(4), 371-378. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milgram_experiment (wording follows the Wikipedia article 'Milgram experiment', CC BY-SA 4.0). 65% reached 450 V in the baseline. Variations: proximity of learner (touch 30%, remote by phone 21% for instructions, same room 40%); two refusing peers left only 4 of 40 continuing, while a complying peer left 37 of 40. Replication/critique: Burger (2009) with a 150 V cutoff found similar rates; Perry (2012) says published accounts did not match the archives and that only about half believed the shocks were real; Haslam, Reicher & Birney: participants responded to science-based prods more than to orders ("engaged followership"). **Operators:** COMMAND (prod ladder), NEARNESS (proximity modulates), PEER-REFUSE / PEER-COMPLY (peer behaviour moves outcomes massively), LEGITIMISE (science vs order). **Formality:** parametric and strongly testable. The peer-refusal result is the largest effect in the cluster.

**Sherif, Robbers Cave.** Sherif, Harvey, White, Hood & Sherif (1961). *Intergroup Conflict and Cooperation: The Robbers Cave Experiment.* Univ. Book Exchange. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robbers_Cave_experiment (wording follows the Wikipedia article 'Robbers Cave experiment', CC BY-SA 4.0). Phases: in-group formation, friction (competition for prizes; 93% of friendships in-group), integration via superordinate goals (tasks needing cooperation); contact alone did not reduce hostility. Critique (page): earlier studies in the series saw boys unite against the experimenters (Cherry 1995); Billig says the experimenters were a third group; Diab's Beirut repeat (18 boys) saw fighting between the experimental groups not along religious lines. Perry, G. (2018). *The Lost Boys: Inside Muzafer Sherif's Robbers Cave Experiment.* Scribe. Perry documents the 1953 Middle Grove (New York) precursor, in which the boys did not split into hostile groups as planned, came to suspect manipulation and turned on the staff, and the study was abandoned; she also argues the 1954 Robbers Cave camp was more steered by staff than the published account says (validation: added from the validator's knowledge and the publisher's description; the boys-versus-experimenters detail matches Wikipedia's "earlier studies in the series" line). Robbers Cave itself ran in 1954. **Operators:** SORT (assign to groups), COMPETE (zero-sum prizes), SUPERORDINATE-GOAL (task needing both groups). **Formality:** three experimental interventions with a qualitative before/after. Direct precedent of a *group-state-changing operator with a known inverse*: competition raises hostility, superordinate goal lowers it.

**Allport contact hypothesis.** Allport, G. (1954). *The Nature of Prejudice.* Addison-Wesley. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contact_hypothesis (wording follows the Wikipedia article 'Contact hypothesis', CC BY-SA 4.0). Four conditions: equal status, shared goals, cooperation without competition, authority support. Pettigrew & Tropp (2006, *JPSP* 90(5), 751-783) meta-analysis of 515 studies (mean r about -.21) supports the hypothesis, stronger when conditions hold; Paluck, Green & Green (2019, *Behavioural Public Policy*): the conditions are helpful, not essential; Bertrand & Duflo (2017): selection effects may explain correlations; negative contact may weigh more than positive. **Operator:** CONTACT, with applicability preconditions. This is a rare example of an operator *with its preconditions written down* and moderate meta-analytic support.

**Stanford prison experiment (the caution).** Zimbardo 1971; Haney, Banks & Zimbardo (1973). *International Journal of Criminology and Penology*, 1, 69-97 (validation: pages corrected; Wikipedia gives 69-77, the article runs 69-97). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_prison_experiment (wording follows the Wikipedia article 'Stanford prison experiment', CC BY-SA 4.0). Claimed that roles, rules and anonymity drive behaviour. Critiques per the page: demand characteristics (Banuazizi & Movahedi 1975), selection bias (Carnahan & McFarland 2007), Le Texier (2019, *American Psychologist*, 74(7), 823-839) arguing guards were instructed; BBC Prison Study (Haslam & Reicher, 2002/2006) got different results, emphasising leadership. Haney, Haslam, Reicher & Zimbardo issued a joint "Consensus statement on the Stanford Prison Experiment and BBC Prison Study" in 2018 (validation: reworded; per Wikipedia it asserts both studies were valid, so it is not a concession by Zimbardo). Reicher & Haslam's BBC study is published as *British Journal of Social Psychology* 45 (2006), 1-40. **Verdict for use:** do not use the SPE as evidence that "role assignment" is an operator that produces cruelty; use it as an example of a *claim that fails as operator evidence* and of the leader-instruction confound.

## 17. Groupthink (Janis)

- **Citation:** Janis, I. L. (1972). *Victims of Groupthink.* Houghton Mifflin; (1982) *Groupthink: Psychological Studies of Policy Decisions and Fiascoes.* Houghton Mifflin.
- **URL checked:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Groupthink (first 100,000 characters only)
- **Licence note:** section prose follows the Wikipedia article 'Groupthink' (CC BY-SA 4.0); the eight symptoms are from Janis 1972/1982.
- **Their vocabulary:** symptoms in three groups: overestimation of the group (illusion of invulnerability, belief in the group's morality), closed-mindedness (rationalising warnings, stereotyping outsiders), pressure toward uniformity (self-censorship, illusion of unanimity, direct pressure on dissenters, "mindguards"). The eight (validation: added from Janis 1972/1982): (1) illusion of invulnerability; (2) collective rationalisation; (3) belief in the group's inherent morality; (4) stereotyped views of out-groups; (5) direct pressure on dissenters; (6) self-censorship; (7) illusion of unanimity; (8) self-appointed mindguards. Antecedents: high cohesion (Janis's most important), structural faults (insulation, no impartial leader, no methodological norms, homogeneity), situational stress. Prescriptions: critical evaluator, leader impartiality, outside experts, rotating devil's advocate, independent groups.
- **Operands (translated):** a decision-making group with attributes cohesion, insulation, leader style, stress.
- **Operators (translated):** SELF-CENSOR, PRESSURE-DISSENTER, MINDGUARD (filter information), RATIONALISE, and the *counter-operators* ASSIGN-CRITIC, ROTATE-DEVIL'S-ADVOCATE, CONSULT-OUTSIDERS. **Formality:** a conditional model: antecedents -> symptoms -> poor decisions, with named preventions. Rare in that it supplies the inverse operators.
- **Predictive/control claim:** antecedents predict groupthink. Standing: Park (1990): only partial support; no main effect of cohesiveness found in the studies reviewed; Leana (1985): no cohesion-by-leadership interaction; McCauley (1989): structural conditions predicted groupthink and situational ones, including cohesion, did not. Overall (per the page): homogeneity and insulation have support; cohesion and leadership mixed. Esser (1998, *Organizational Behavior and Human Decision Processes* 73, 116-141) reviewed 25 years of work and found the evidence limited and mixed, much of it case-study based.
- **Limits:** built from case studies of historical fiascos; post hoc.
- **Relevance:** supplies **structural variables** (insulation, homogeneity, impartial leadership) that predict failure, plus inverse operators, which fits an algebra with an invertibility question.

## What this cluster already covers

1. **A large, overlapping vocabulary of group-level operators.** About 50 numbered entries (many bundling several names), reducible to about 20 distinct ones (list below).
2. **Triads and coalitions as the structural primitive** (Bowen, Minuchin, Haley): a three-party structure with inside/outside positions and, for Haley, a rank condition.
3. **Reciprocal-pair dynamics with a sign** (Bateson, Watzlawick): symmetric vs complementary, with an escalation law and dampers.
4. **Typed relation edges and a drawing notation** (Minuchin maps, genograms), including boundary permeability on a three-level scale.
5. **Phase models for change** (Schein/Lewin; Moghaddam; Kelman's depth levels): ordered stages with exit probabilities.
6. **Controlled-experiment dose-response data on a few operators** (Asch group size and unanimity; Milgram proximity and peer behaviour; Sherif competition vs superordinate goal; Allport contact conditions).
7. **A few inverse operators** (Janis's preventions; superordinate goals; naven-type ritual damping; one ally breaking unanimity).
8. **Information-environment operators** (Lifton's milieu control and loaded language, Hassan's information control, Ellul's saturation).
9. **Dispositional response functions** (Satir stances).

## What it does not cover

Said bluntly:

- **No algebra.** Nothing here has composition rules, identity, inverse (except informal), or commutativity. "What happens if you triangulate then cut off?" has no defined answer anywhere in this cluster.
- **The notations are static pictures.** Minuchin's map and the genogram draw states. Verbs live in prose. No notation for *transitions*, and no notation for operators acting.
- **Operands are under-specified.** "Person" is a name or a differentiation level; none of it addresses what state a person carries (belief, commitment, plasticity, public vs private) in a way that composes. Kelman's public/private pair and Schein's plasticity are hints only.
- **Little quantification.** Only the experimental operators (Asch, Milgram, Sherif, contact) have dose-response data, and each has had procedure or mechanism contested. The clinical frameworks (Bowen, Minuchin, Satir, Haley) have no validation evidence in what I retrieved.
- **Most of it is one-way influence.** Influencer acts, target responds. Few of the sources model many-to-many simultaneous action, which is what "a group" actually is. Bateson and Watzlawick come closest.
- **Brainwashing-lineage evidence is weak.** Lifton, Hassan and Singer are descriptive checklists; Hassan's BITE has no validation found; US courts have rejected the testimony under Frye; the APA declined the DIMPAC report. Use these as vocabularies for operators, not as established mechanisms.
- **No treatment of power as an operand or resource** beyond Haley's hierarchy and Watzlawick's equal/different. Reciprocity, exchange and resources are missing from this cluster entirely.
- **Culture and context are mostly implicit.** Western nuclear-family assumptions (Bowen, Minuchin) and WEIRD experimental samples.
