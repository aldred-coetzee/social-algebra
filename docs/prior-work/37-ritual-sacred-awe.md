# Prior work 37: ritual, the sacred, and awe as group-drive binders

Date: 2026-10-29. Survey for the social-algebra strand. Cluster: the mechanisms groups use to synchronise and bind members' drives (synchrony, ordeal, costly commitment, sacred values, awe). Cross-references, not repeated: **18** (Durkheim and Páez et al. 2015 collective effervescence; Collins named in its gap list), **24** (identity fusion, Swann et al. 2012, Gomez et al. 2017, Varmann 2023 meta-analysis; devoted actors; sacredness as a blocker), **06 section 15** (Collins interaction ritual chains, with model figure), **22** (oxytocin failure, endorphin-adjacent neuroscience), **23** (collective-dynamics models that would formalise synchrony coupling), **31** (ethology of signals and costly signalling in animals).

Crossref confirms bibliography, not claims. Abstracts were read through Europe PMC and OpenAlex (`[abstract]`). Read at source (`[read]`): Ginges et al. 2007 (full text of the PNAS PDF, design, sample sizes, deal wordings) and Atwood, Schachner & Mehr 2022 (via a summarising fetch of the PMC page; effect sizes it does not report are marked as unconfirmed). **Summary.**
1. This cluster is almost entirely **empirical psychology and anthropology with no equations**. The only formal object found is the mathematical model in Whitehouse et al. 2017 (validation pass: its Models section was read; it is a group-structured evolutionary model of a collective-action game, with payoff and contest-success functions, in which conditioning effort on shared dysphoric experience evolves by selection). Everything else gives directional hypotheses with a mediator ("synchrony -> self-other merging -> cooperation"; "ordeal -> fusion -> sacrifice"; "awe -> small self -> prosociality"). For the algebra these are **operators with known sign and weak magnitude**.
2. The strongest and most algebra-ready result in the cluster is not about binding but about a **gate**: **sacred values invert the sign of the incentive operator** (Ginges et al. 2007 `[read]`). That is a true, stateable theorem-shaped fact: for non-absolutists d(opposition)/d(incentive) < 0; for moral-absolutists d(opposition)/d(incentive) > 0. Of everything in this file it is the nearest thing to a conditional law.
3. The best-known binding operator, **synchrony -> cooperation, is the weakest on evidence**. Rennung & Göritz (2016) report a medium pooled effect (attitudes g = 0.49, k = 48; behaviour g = 0.45, k = 35), but in **their own** subgroup analysis the **behavioural** effect in the nine experiments with a blinded experimenter could not be distinguished from zero (z = 0.56, p = .58); the **attitudinal** effect shrank with blinding but stayed above zero `[read, Rennung & Göritz full text]`. Atwood et al. 2022 cite and build on this result. Controlled replications have failed. Treat `sync` as a candidate operator whose effect on bonding/affiliation is real and whose effect on cooperative behaviour is contested.
4. **Costly ritual -> commitment** is supported by field data (Sosis; Xygalatas; Whitehouse) but almost all of it is correlational, small-N, or one-site. The causal arrow (cost produces fusion vs fused people accept cost) is not settled.
5. **Awe** has a clean mechanism claim (small self mediates prosociality), three or four labs' worth of experiments, and an unusually large share of studies from one group (Keltner's). Independent replication evidence was not found in this search.

## 1. Ritual synchrony and cooperation

### 1.1 Wiltermuth & Heath (2009)

- **Citation.** Wiltermuth, S. S. & Heath, C. (2009). Synchrony and cooperation. *Psychological Science* 20(1), 1-5. doi:10.1111/j.1467-9280.2008.02253.x (Crossref confirmed).
- **Their vocabulary.** Synchrony (acting in time with others: marching, singing, dancing), free-rider problem, strengthened social attachment, weakened self-group boundary.
- **Drives/operands.** Cooperative motive vs self-interest; the operand is a small group of strangers; the state variable is "social attachment" (never measured as a scalar; inferred from game choices).
- **Operators / equations.** None. Manipulation: walk in step vs walk normally (Exp. 1, 30 participants in groups of three `[search/secondary]`), then a group economic exercise; the paper reports three experiments. `[abstract]`
- **Theorems / predictions.** Synchrony -> more cooperation in a later group economic game, including when it costs the individual; positive affect is not required (the abstract says so). `[abstract]`
- **Empirical standing.** The founding study of the paradigm; sample sizes tiny. Replication record is poor where experimenters are blind: Schachner & Garvin (2010) ran two replications of Exp. 3 with condition-naive experimenters and found no increase in cooperation, conformity, trust, similarity or same-team feeling `[read, via Atwood et al. 2022; N not reported there]`. Kirschner & Ilari (2013) found no prosocial effect of joint drumming in preschoolers `[read, via Atwood et al.]`.
- **Translation.** Operator `sync(A, B)` on two or more people acting in time. Effect: reduces perceived self-other boundary (candidate state: merge m in [0,1]); consequence: raises weight on group payoff. Sign +, magnitude uncertain.

### 1.2 The meta-analyses and the expectancy critique

- **Rennung, M. & Göritz, A. S. (2016).** Prosocial consequences of interpersonal synchrony: a meta-analysis. *Zeitschrift für Psychologie* 224(3), 168-189. doi:10.1027/2151-2604/a000252 (Crossref confirmed, including volume and pages). `[read, full text PMC5137339]`: 60 published and unpublished experiments; overall random-effects g = 0.48 [0.39, 0.56]; prosocial attitudes g = 0.49 [0.40, 0.57] (k = 48); prosocial behaviour g = 0.45 [0.30, 0.60] (k = 35, I² = 59%). Moderators: experimenter blinding (attitudes and behaviour) and intentionality of the synchrony (behaviour); no other coded moderator mattered. For attitudes the effect was larger by g = 0.29 when experimenters knew the hypotheses, but stayed significant in every subgroup. For behaviour, in the nine experiments with a blinded experimenter "the hypothesis that g equals null could not be rejected, z = 0.56, p = .58", which the authors call "preliminary evidence that a methodological artifact may account for the reported behavioral effects". **The blind-experimenter null is Rennung & Göritz's own result**, not Atwood's re-analysis. The "about 75% of studies with condition-aware experimenters" figure is Atwood et al.'s estimate, which they attribute to Rennung & Göritz's coding.

## 2. Whitehouse: modes of religiosity, dysphoria, fusion

- **Citations.**
  - Whitehouse, H. (2000). *Arguments and Icons: Divergent Modes of Religiosity*. Oxford UP. doi:10.1093/oso/9780198234142.001.0001 (Crossref confirmed).
  - Whitehouse, H. & Lanman, J. A. (2014). The ties that bind us: ritual, fusion, and identification. *Current Anthropology* 55(6), 674-695. doi:10.1086/678698 (Crossref confirmed, pages included; abstract read in validation).
  - Whitehouse, H., Jong, J., Buhrmester, M. D., Gómez, Á., Bastian, B., Kavanagh, C. M., Newson, M., Matthews, M., Lanman, J. A., McKay, R. & Gavrilets, S. (2017). The evolution of extreme cooperation via shared dysphoric experiences. *Scientific Reports* 7, 44292. doi:10.1038/srep44292 (Crossref confirmed; abstract and Models section read in validation).
  - Whitehouse, H. (2018). Dying for the group: towards a general theory of extreme self-sacrifice. *Behavioral and Brain Sciences* 41, e192. doi:10.1017/s0140525x18000249 (Crossref confirmed; abstract read).
  - Atkinson, Q. D. & Whitehouse, H. (2011). The cultural morphospace of ritual form: examining modes of religiosity cross-culturally. *Evol. Hum. Behav.* 32(1), 50-62. doi:10.1016/j.evolhumbehav.2010.09.002 (Crossref confirmed).
  - Jong, J., Whitehouse, H., Kavanagh, C. & Lane, J. (2015). Shared negative experiences lead to identity fusion via personal reflection. *PLOS ONE* 10(12), e0145611, doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0145611 (Crossref confirmed).
- **Their vocabulary.** Doctrinal mode (high frequency, low arousal, semantic memory, routinised, large anonymous communities, hierarchy) vs imagistic mode (low frequency, high arousal, episodic "flashbulb" memory, small cohesive groups) `[standard account]`. Identity fusion (a visceral oneness; see 24), identification (group-level, depersonalised), dysphoria (ordeal, pain, fear), reflection (rumination on the shared event).
- **Drives/operands.** Operands: ritual event (frequency, arousal, cost), person (memory, fusion level), group size. Whitehouse & Lanman 2014 distinguish *fusion* (local, imagistic) from *identification* (doctrinal, scalable) as two bonding routes. `[abstract, read in validation]`: the paper offers "a general and testable theory" connecting ritual elements ("causal opacity and emotional arousal") to two forms of cohesion, group identification and identity fusion, grounded in an evolutionary account of which ritual modes suit which resource-acquisition strategies.
- **Operators / equations.**
  - Verbal: `high-arousal low-frequency ritual -> episodic memory -> reflection -> fusion`; `frequent routinised ritual -> semantic memory -> identification`. `[standard account]`
  - Mathematical: Whitehouse et al. 2017 state that they "develop a mathematical model showing how conditioning cooperation on previous shared experience can allow individually costly pro-group behavior to evolve" `[abstract]`. Validation pass, Models section read: G groups of constant size n, discrete non-overlapping generations; individual effort z_ij in a collective action with payoff from benefit b and cost c; group success P_j(Z_j) either as an "us vs. nature" saturating function (half-saturation Z_0) or an "us vs. them" contest share; a proportion π of groups has a euphoric and 1-π a dysphoric prior experience (E_j), which together with P_j sets group survival. It is a volunteer's-dilemma collective-action game evolving under selection across generations (so it **is** dynamical in the evolutionary sense, not a within-lifetime rate law). Its prediction: groups with shared dysphoric experience evolve to contribute more to later collective action. The paper reports predictions tested in veterans, fraternity/sorority members, football fans, martial artists and twins `[abstract]`: shared painful experience -> fusion -> willingness to fight and die.
  - Atkinson & Whitehouse 2011 is a factor analysis: ritual features cluster on two or more axes, "morphospace" `[standard account, not read]`.
- **Theorems / predictions.** Shared dysphoria -> fusion (stronger than shared non-dysphoric); fusion -> extreme self-sacrifice; fusion also from perceived shared biology (kin cues) `[abstract of Whitehouse 2018]`. The 2018 BBS target article generalises this to a single theory.
- **Empirical standing (blunt).** Samples are special populations, results are correlations plus a few experiments; "shared negative experience -> fusion" works through personal reflection in Jong et al. 2015 (so not through the experience alone). Selection (the fused join dangerous groups) is not separated from causation in the veteran/fraternity data. The modes-of-religiosity cross-cultural test (Atkinson & Whitehouse 2011) found support for the clustering but the claimed dichotomy is partly a continuum `[standard account]`. A pseudo-experiment on Trump's inauguration (Kapitány et al. 2018, PsyArXiv preprint, doi:10.31234/osf.io/53waf, Crossref confirmed) is the only attempt found at a secular-ritual fusion test; not read. For fusion's own meta-analysis see 24 (Varmann 2023, r = .50, not rechecked here).
- **Translation.** Operand: a person with fusion f and a group. Operators: `ordeal(group)` (raises f in all participants, magnitude depends on intensity and reflection), `routine(group)` (slowly raises identification i), `fuse(p,G)` is 24's operator. Fusion is a **persistent state change**, unlike synchrony which is a transient coupling; the algebra needs both a fast and a slow variable.

## 3. Fire-walking and extreme-ritual physiology (Xygalatas, Konvalinka, Fischer)

- **Citations.**

  - Xygalatas, D., Khan, S., Lang, M., Kundt, R., Kundtová-Klocová, E., Krátký, J. & Shaver, J. (2019). Effects of extreme ritual practices on psychophysiological well-being. *Current Anthropology* 60(5), 699-707, doi:10.1086/705665 (Crossref confirmed; author list corrected in validation; abstract not read).
  - Xygalatas, D., Maňo, P., Bahna, V., Kundtová Klocová, E., Kundt, R., Lang, M. & Shaver, J. H. (2021). Social inequality and signaling in a costly ritual. *Evol. Hum. Behav.* 42(6), 524-533, doi:10.1016/j.evolhumbehav.2021.05.006 (Crossref confirmed; abstract not read).
  - Fischer, R., Callander, R., Reddish, P. & Bulbulia, J. (2013). How do rituals affect cooperation? An experimental field study comparing nine ritual types. *Human Nature* 24, 115-125. doi:10.1007/s12110-013-9167-y (Crossref confirmed; abstract not read).
- **Their vocabulary.** Synchronised arousal, collective effervescence, recurrence quantification analysis (RQA), cross-recurrence (CRQA), related vs unrelated spectators, extreme ritual (Kavadi), ordeal, pain.
- **Drives/operands.** Operand: person's heart-rate time series (arousal). Group state: shared arousal trajectory. Konvalinka: 38 participants `[abstract]`, fire-walkers and spectators in a Spanish ritual.
- **Operators / equations.** No model equation; the **measurement** is the contribution: cross-recurrence quantification of two heart-rate series, so "synchrony" is operationalised as the proportion of recurrent points `[abstract and standard account]`. Not stated in the paper as a law.
- **Findings.** `[abstract]` Konvalinka: heart-rate dynamics of fire-walkers matched those of **related** spectators (kin/friends) but not unrelated spectators, though bodily behaviour differed greatly; the authors infer an **informational** (not mimicry) mechanism. Xygalatas 2013 `[abstract + search/secondary]`: in Thaipusam, the high-ordeal Kavadi bearers and their observers donated more to charity than low-ordeal prayers; secondary summaries (press reports, 2013) give Mauritius as the site, N = 86 men of whom 19 were Kavadi performers, and a link between reported pain and donation; one flashcard source contradicts the pain link, original not read, so treat the pain-donation effect size as **unconfirmed**.
- **Theorems / predictions.** Shared arousal tracks social tie, not proximity; ordeal intensity predicts later generosity (within-ritual comparison).
- **Empirical standing.** Konvalinka is a single-ritual, small-N, correlational field study. It is the cleanest published demonstration that **tie strength modulates physiological coupling**, which is exactly the quantity the algebra needs for an `affect-coupling(A,B)` weight. The Xygalatas donation result is a natural experiment (self-selection into ordeal is uncontrolled; the 2021 follow-up shows poorer participants taking more piercings, suggesting ordeal is also a status-seeking signal). No replication found in this search.
- **Translation.** Edge weight w(A,B) = coupling of arousal series, to be estimated from tie type (kin/friend/stranger). This is the empirical hook for 23's coupled-oscillator models and 20's dyadic coupling.

## 4. Costly signalling theory of religion

- **Citations.**

  - Soler, M. (2012). Costly signaling, ritual and cooperation: evidence from Candomblé. *Evol. Hum. Behav.* 33(4), 346-356. doi:10.1016/j.evolhumbehav.2011.11.004 (Crossref confirmed; abstract not read).
  - Power, E. A. (2017). Social support networks and religiosity in rural South India. *Nature Human Behaviour* 1, 0057, doi:10.1038/s41562-017-0057; Power (2018), Collective ritual and social support networks in rural South India, *Proc. R. Soc. B*, doi:10.1098/rspb.2018.0023 (both Crossref confirmed; abstracts not read).
- **Their vocabulary.** Costly signal, hard-to-fake, commitment, free-rider screening, credibility-enhancing display (CRED), taboo and ritual requirements, adaptive benefit of religion.
- **Drives/operands.** Operands: member (type: committed or free-rider), community (survival time). The quantity "cost" is a property of requirements (food, possessions, communication, marriage, sexuality, rituals).
- **Operators / equations.** The theory is an application of Zahavi/Spence signalling: a signal is honest when its cost is lower for committed than for non-committed types `[standard account]`. No equation is given in the Sosis & Bressler abstract; they run a survival analysis on 83 19th-century US communes `[abstract]` (30 religious, 53 secular `[search/secondary, not re-confirmed in validation]`). Sosis 2000 analysed 200 communes (88 religious, 112 secular `[search/secondary]`) and reports religious communes survived longer at every stage `[abstract/secondary]`.
- **Theorems / predictions.** Cost of requirements predicts longevity **in religious communes but not in secular ones** (an interaction `cost x religiosity`) `[search/secondary]`: the abstract says only that the authors tested "whether costly requirements and religiosity interact" and that results "support aspects of" the theory; the religious-only result is from secondary accounts (e.g. summaries of Haidt 2012, *The Righteous Mind*), not the paper. As reported, it is the paper's central claim and makes the cost term conditional on a sacred frame. Figures like "6% vs 39% survived 20 years" appeared only in a secondary quotation and are **unconfirmed**.
- **Empirical standing (blunt).** Archival, 19th-century US sample, observational; survivorship and ideology are confounded with founding conditions and leader quality. No independent replication found. Costly signalling for ritual has support from the kibbutz and Candomblé field work in the titles above (not read), and the CRED line (Henrich) gives a different mechanism (cost as proof of belief for learners, not screening of types), so the theories are **not** equivalent. The overall literature on "does religion cause cooperation" is contested; Norenzayan et al. 2016 (BBS 39, e1; doi:10.1017/s0140525x14001356, online 2014) and Purzycki et al. 2018 (*Religion, Brain & Behavior* 8(2), 101-132, doi:10.1080/2153599x.2016.1267027), both Crossref confirmed and unread, are the main syntheses.
- **Translation.** Operator `demand(group, cost c)` on a membership population: it filters members by commitment type and, for those who stay, raises the group's expected payoff per member (the claim). Needs a type variable per member and a conditional on sacred framing. Same shape as 21's psychological-game gate.

- **Their vocabulary.** Sacred value, protected value, taboo trade-off, moral-absolutist vs non-absolutist, instrumental vs non-instrumental reasoning, symbolic concession, devoted actor, deontic vs utilitarian processing, "pseudo-sacred" (Tetlock et al. 2017, *AER* P&P, doi:10.1257/aer.p20171110, Crossref confirmed, unread).
- **Drives/operands.** Operand: an agent with a set of values, each tagged sacred or non-sacred. Tetlock's taxonomy (standard account): routine trade-offs, tragic trade-offs (two sacred values clash), taboo trade-offs (sacred vs secular); only the taboo trade-off triggers moral outrage and "moral cleansing".
- **Operators / equations. `[read]` (Ginges et al.).** No equation; the design is a 3-condition experiment per sample, each deal presented as a hypothetical peace deal.
  - **Taboo**: a compromise over a sacred issue (e.g. recognise Israel; give up East Jerusalem; give up 99% of the West Bank; give up the right of return).
  - **Taboo+**: Taboo plus a material or instrumental benefit (e.g. 1 billion US dollars a year for 10 years; 1,000 dollars per Palestinian family per year; "peace and prosperity").
  - **Symbolic**: Taboo plus an adversary concession on one of the adversary's own sacred values, written to add no instrumental value (e.g. Israel apologises; Israel gives up its "sacred right" to the West Bank).
  - Outcome measures: emotional outrage (anger, disgust), support for violent opposition, in-group support.
- **Samples `[read]`.** 601 Israeli settlers (46% moral-absolutist on the Land of Israel); 719 Palestinian students (54% treated both the right of return and Jerusalem as sacred); 535 Palestinian refugees (over 80% absolutists on the right of return). Each participant randomly assigned one of the three deals, about a third each (the paper says one-third to Taboo and one-third to Taboo+; the remainder to Symbolic).
- **Theorems / derived predictions `[read]`.**
  - (i) For non-absolutists, adding a material incentive reduced opposition (the rational response). For moral-absolutists it **increased** outrage and support for violence; Ginges et al. report that this held "in all experiments with Palestinian students and Jewish settlers with respect to support for violent responses (all P < 0.0065, one-tailed t-test) and emotional outrage (all P < 0.0025)", and the same pattern in refugees.
  - (ii) Symbolic concession lowered outrage and violence support among absolutists. Reported odds ratios: Palestinian students' predicted odds of intense anger/disgust fell by a multiplicative factor 0.518 (P = 0.014); settlers' intense support for violent response by 0.25 (P = 0.003); refugees' odds of "joy" at a suicide attack by 0.519 (P = 0.009). Symbolic concessions did not raise belief that a deal would be implemented.
  - Equivalently, for sacred s in {0,1}: sign( d(opposition) / d(material incentive) ) = -1 if s = 0, +1 if s = 1; and d(opposition) / d(symbolic concession) < 0 for s = 1. This is my formalisation of the paper's result, not an equation in it.
  - Sheikh et al. 2013 `[abstract]`: devoted actors (sacred value holders) were less amenable to social influence, perceived conflict events as temporally closer (past and future), and were blind to individual escape opportunities.
  - Berns 2012 `[abstract]`: refusal to sell a value for real money activated left TPJ and ventrolateral PFC (semantic rule retrieval), taken as deontic processing, not cost-benefit.
- **Empirical standing (blunt).** Hypothetical deals, one-tailed tests, conflict-region samples, mostly one research group (Atran/Ginges and collaborators); selected effects are large and consistent across three samples within the paper. Independent replication outside this group, and field evidence that the backfire changes actual behaviour, was **not found** in this search. The follow-up Iran work (Dehghani 2009, 2010) and Sheikh 2012/2013 are by the same group. The existence of non-fungible values is well supported conceptually (Tetlock; Baron & Spranca); the claim that *incentives backfire* is the extraordinary part and rests on this paper and its follow-ups. The Science 2012 piece `[abstract]` is a review ("immunity to material incentives").
- **Translation.** Operand: a value v with a flag or weight in the agent's utility. Operator `offer(x)` on a good x: normally increases acceptance; on a sacred good, it is a **sign-flipping** operator (insult). The algebra needs (a) a type predicate sacred(v), (b) the incentive operator defined conditionally, (c) a **second operator**, `symbolic(v')`, acting in the sacred register, which is the only one that works. Sacred values therefore define **two orthogonal currencies** (material, sacred) with conversion forbidden in one direction. This is a direct challenge to any algebra that assumes one utility scale; it is the sharpest structural result in this file. Link to 24 (sacredness as blocker) and 21 (psychological games).

  - Sturm, V. E. et al. (2022). Big smile, small self: awe walks promote prosocial positive emotions in older adults. *Emotion* 22(5), 1044-1058. doi:10.1037/emo0000876 (Crossref confirmed; abstract read).

  - Fiske, A. P., Seibt, B. & Schubert, T. (2019). The sudden devotion emotion: kama muta and the cultural practices whose function is to evoke it. *Emotion Review* 11(1), 74-86. doi:10.1177/1754073917723167 (Crossref gave the 2017 online year; abstract not read).
- **Their vocabulary.** Awe (perceived vastness plus need for accommodation), small self / diminished self, collective emotion, self-transcendent emotion, elevation (moral awe at others' virtue), admiration, kama muta (being "moved" by sudden intensification of communal sharing), dark awe (threat-based).
- **Drives/operands.** Operand: self-size s (pictorial and verbal "perceived self-size" scales, Bai 2017 `[abstract]`), prosocial inclination, collective engagement.
- **Operators / equations.** None. Mediation structure: `awe -> small self -> prosociality / collective engagement` `[abstract, Piff and Bai]`. Piff 2015: 5 studies, N = 2,078 (dispositional awe predicted generosity in a representative sample; inductions raised ethical decision-making, generosity, prosocial values; standing in a eucalyptus grove increased helping and lowered entitlement) `[abstract]`. Bai 2017: 6 studies, N = 2,137; awe, not other positive emotions, diminished the self; the effect held in collectivist and individualist samples but varied in magnitude and content `[abstract]`. Sturm 2022: 60 older adults, 8 weekly awe walks vs control, more awe and smaller selves in photographs; **anxiety, depression and life satisfaction did not change** `[abstract]`.
- **Theorems / predictions.** Awe dissolves the self-weight on individual interests and raises weight on the collective (the self-size reduces conflict between self and group drives). Gordon et al. 2017 add that awe can be threat-based and then need not be prosocial `[abstract not read; title only]`.
- **Empirical standing (blunt).** All the core studies come from Keltner's network. Self-report mediators, brief inductions, U.S. online and student samples, demand characteristics plausible (participants know they are watching awe videos or standing in a grove). I **did not find** a preregistered independent replication of Piff 2015 in this search; the search surfaced only a DePaul student poster with preliminary manipulation checks and a 2025 three-level meta-analysis of awe and prosocial behaviour in *Acta Psychologica Sinica* 57(4), 631-651 (doi:10.3724/SP.J.1041.2025.0631, not Crossref-checked, result **not read**). Treat the awe -> prosocial effect as plausible, small-to-moderate and not yet independently confirmed, and the "small self" mediator as the weakest link (self-reported, measured after induction). Elevation and gratitude have a similar self-report evidence base `[standard account]`.
- **Translation.** Operand: self-size s. Operator `awe(stimulus)`: s decreases, group weight w_G increases. In the algebra this is the **same functional role as `fuse`** but triggered by a perceptual stimulus rather than an ordeal, and acting on a person without needing other people present. A single "self-versus-group weight" state with three inputs (synchrony, ordeal, awe) is a candidate reduction; 18 already lists "mode switch" with these triggers (line 149), and the present file supplies the triggers' empirical grounds and their weaknesses.

## 7. Music and dance as synchronisers: Tarr, Launay, Dunbar

  - Tarr, B., Launay, J., Cohen, E. & Dunbar, R. (2015). Synchrony and exertion during dance independently raise pain threshold and encourage social bonding. *Biology Letters* 11, 20150767. doi:10.1098/rsbl.2015.0767 (Crossref confirmed; abstract read).
  - Tarr, B., Launay, J. & Dunbar, R. I. M. (2016). Silent disco: dancing in synchrony leads to elevated pain thresholds and social closeness. *Evol. Hum. Behav.* 37(5), 343-349. doi:10.1016/j.evolhumbehav.2016.02.004 (Crossref confirmed; abstract read).
  - Launay, J., Tarr, B. & Dunbar, R. I. M. (2016). Synchrony as an adaptive mechanism for large-scale human social bonding. *Ethology* 122, 779-789. doi:10.1111/eth.12528 (Crossref confirmed).
  - Tarr, B., Launay, J., Benson, C. & Dunbar, R. I. M. (2017). Naltrexone blocks endorphins released when dancing in synchrony. *Adaptive Human Behavior and Physiology* 3, 241-254. doi:10.1007/s40750-017-0067-y (Crossref confirmed; abstract not read; the Crossref title carries quote marks).
  - Hagen, E. H. & Bryant, G. A. (2003). Music and dance as a coalition signaling system. *Human Nature* 14(1), 21-51. doi:10.1007/s12110-003-1015-z (Crossref confirmed).
- **Their vocabulary.** Self-other merging, pain threshold as endorphin proxy, social bonding (IOS-type closeness), exertion, synchrony, coalition signalling.
- **Drives/operands.** Operand: participant with pain threshold (proxy for opioid state) and a bonding score. The group-drive claim: the endorphin system, the same one tied to primate grooming, is recruited by synchronised exertion to scale bonding beyond dyads.
- **Operators / equations.** None. 2x2 design (synchrony x exertion) in the 2015 paper: both factors **independently** raised pain threshold and in-group bonding `[abstract]`. Silent disco 2016: elevated pain thresholds under full synchrony only, partial and asynchrony conditions no analgesic effect; bonding higher under synchrony; **no increase in economic-game cooperation** `[abstract]`.
- **Theorems / predictions.** Synchrony + exertion -> opioid release -> bonding without requiring cooperation to increase. The naltrexone study (title only read) tests the opioid mechanism by blockade.
- **Empirical standing.** Small lab studies from one Oxford group with a pain-threshold proxy, which is itself confounded by exertion, arousal and mood; the dissociation between bonding and cooperation in the silent disco is the honest result and contradicts a simple "synchrony -> cooperation" chain. Wider intranasal-oxytocin failures in 22 are a warning about hormone-mechanism claims. A blockade study is better design in principle; result unread. Independent replication not found.
- **Translation.** Two distinct operator effects should be kept separate: `sync` -> bonding (supported by self-report) and `sync` -> cooperative behaviour (contested). Add an `exert` operator; combination is additive on the proxy in the one 2x2 design.

## What exists vs what does not

**Exists.**
- A well-populated set of **directional operators** with names, triggers and plausible mediators: `sync`, `ordeal`, `demand(cost)`, `awe`, `symbolic-concession`, each acting on a self-versus-group weight or on a coupling.
- One **conditional law** with direct quantitative support in a randomised design: the sacred-value sign inversion of the incentive operator (Ginges et al. 2007 `[read]`), with odds-ratio effects (0.25 to 0.52) on outrage and support for violence in three populations.
- A **measurement** method for coupling between people's arousal (cross-recurrence of heart rate) and an association with tie type (Konvalinka 2011).
- A **mathematical** evolutionary model of conditional cooperation after shared experience (Whitehouse et al. 2017; Models section read in validation).
- Honest critical work on the synchrony literature (Rennung & Göritz 2016's own blind-experimenter result for behaviour; Atwood et al. 2022's expectancy experiment).

**Does not exist (found).**
- Any **dynamical equation** for ritual binding: no rate law for fusion, no decay constant for effervescence, no dose-response curve for synchrony or ordeal. The algebra would have to posit them.
- A **measured emotional-energy variable** (Collins), or a persistence law across encounters.
- Independent **preregistered replications** of the awe -> small self -> prosocial chain, of the incentive-backfire result, or of extreme-ritual generosity. (Partial exception on the dysphoria side: Qi et al. 2020, an independent team, replicated "shared pain -> cooperation" from Bastian et al. 2014, though not the fusion step and not preregistered as far as the abstract shows.)
- A **comparison of operators on a common outcome**: synchrony, ordeal, awe and costly demands have never been tested in one design with one fusion/engagement outcome.
- A **quantitative scale-up** from dyadic synchrony (lab) to crowd or population states.
- Any account of **ritual failure**, decay or negative sides (excess fusion, ritual fatigue) beyond Gordon's dark awe and the extremism literature.
