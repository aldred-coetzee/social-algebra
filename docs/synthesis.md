# Synthesis of the prior-work survey

Date: 2026-10-29. Input: `docs/prior-work/01` to `14`, each written once and validated once; where a body and its validation log disagreed, the log was taken. Citations are file and section, e.g. [04 §10]. Provenance labels in the files ([validator], [secondary], Unconfirmed) are carried through; nothing marked Unconfirmed is stated here as fact.

**Purpose.** To put the fourteen traditions side by side on the one question that matters for this strand: who has operands of the right sorts (persons, roles, groups), who has operators that are *acts* rather than ties or rules, who has a stated composition law, and where each stops. From that, the recurring gaps are written as requirements the algebra must meet, and the formal structures that could house it are listed with what each gives and costs. No recommendation is made; Aldred chooses.

**Provenance.** Every structural claim in the fourteen files now carries [read] or [from abstract/standard account]. Almost everything is the latter: the formal descriptions below are the standard textbook forms, checked against abstracts and metadata, not against the primary texts. The exceptions, read in full, are Emerson 1962 [03 §8], RBAC96 [14 §5a], Akerlof-Kranton [10 §10], Lehnert, Versu and Comme il Faut [12 §A5, §B1-B2], and Tarasenko's statement of Lefebvre's reflexive games [03 §3] (Lefebvre's own text unread). In the table and §(b), load-bearing claims are marked [read] or [sa] (standard account); anything unmarked is [sa]. Three entries added after the second pass, [01 §White-IC], [06 §role theory] and [14 §relation algebra], have not themselves been second-passed and are leaned on only for vocabulary.

## (b) The closest traditions, and exactly what each lacks

1. **AGR with RBAC/ARBAC administration [14 §5, §7]** (RBAC96 [read]; AGR and ARBAC [sa], AGR full text never obtained). The only place with persons, roles and groups as three distinct sorts, role local to group, overlap allowed, and guarded operations (join, leave, assign, can_assign) with pre- and post-conditions over a relational state. Lacks: any act of one agent *on* another beyond message passing; any equation between operations; groups that act or are acted on (they are contexts); internal state of agents (affect, obligation, belief); "roles play roles" (Boella-van der Torre, outside AGR); dynamics other than membership change.
2. **Comme il Faut and Versu [12 §B1-B2]** (both [read]). Executable act inventories with typed social state: operator = (preconditions, intent, response rule, effects) on (traits, statuses, relationships, scalar networks, history); in Versu, acts are role-indexed affordances inside practice instances with their own lifetime. Lacks: a group sort (effects reach only the two parties; third-party effects only via trigger rules); any law of composition, identity or inverse; derivation (every inventory is hand-written per world); validation against real groups; roles beyond "role in this practice".
3. **Singh's commitments and DEL action models [12 §C3; 04 §10; 14 What it does not cover]** (Singh's six operations [secondary]; DEL composition [sa]). Directed social acts with a defined life cycle on a persistent relation (create, discharge, cancel, release, delegate, assign; detach as a postulate), a context group G as party to every commitment; and, in DEL, group epistemic acts that genuinely compose (A;B is an action model, with nondeterministic choice). Lacks: more than one relation type (commitment; knowledge); roles as a sort; affect, status, resources; in DEL, agents as bare indices and groups as subsets; in Singh, no equational theory beyond the postulates.
4. **Network role algebras with kinship algebras [01 §2-4; 02 §1-5]** (all [sa]; no primary text read in either file). The one genuine algebra with social operands: compound relations under Boolean product form a (partially ordered) semigroup, with homomorphism theorems; kinship classes carry a finite group action in which clans are the operands. Lacks: operators that are acts (they are ties, or rules fixed by the society); any state on operands; any change of the structure (static fit; vacancy chains are Markov and non-algebraic); groups as anything but blocks or classes; semigroup size exploding with tie types.

Near misses, each strong on one axis only: Lefebvre's reflexive games (a group graph as a Boolean polynomial with alliance = ∧, conflict = ∨, [03 §3]) has no acts and no roles; ACT ([03 §2]) is the best act-transforms-state model but dyadic, fitted, and law-free; Jackson-Wolinsky ([10 §9]) has the cleanest relationship operators (add needs consent, sever does not) and nothing else.

## (d) Candidate formal homes

Each paragraph: what it gives for free, which of R1-R11 it meets, what it costs, and which surveyed work already lives there. No ranking.

**1. A semigroup or monoid of relations, with state.** Gives composition of ties for free, the Boolean-matrix machinery, homomorphism and congruence-lattice theory for comparing and reducing structures, a partial order by inclusion, inverse semigroups for partial maps, and group actions for the rule-fixed case. Meets R3 for relations, R5 in the weak sense that relations are the elements, R11 (fitting methods exist). Costs: the elements are relations, not acts, so R2 is unmet unless each act is encoded as a transformation of the pair (network, state), which turns the structure into a transformation semigroup on states and loses the "friend of a boss of" reading; no sorts (R1); groups only as blocks or classes (R6); the semigroup grows combinatorially with tie types [01 §2]; cross-group comparison can destroy the generators [01 §6]. Tarski relation algebras [14 §relation algebra, not second-passed] add converse, complement and the equational theory the sociological line never used, the role semigroup being a composition subreduct of the full relation algebra on the actor set; the costs are representability questions and that complement is unnatural for social ties ("not a friend" is not a role). Converse is the design point: "A advises B" has one, and role semigroups ignore it. Lives there: 01, 02 §1-5, 05 (stochastic matrices), 03 §4 (Z/2), 14 §2 (multilayer tensor as the representation).

**2. A many-sorted algebra, or an institution-style signature, over AGR/RBAC-like sorts.** Gives the sorts person, role, group with role-local-to-group scoping, operations typed by sort, constraints as invariants (separation of duty, cardinality), guarded commands with pre- and post-conditions in the ANSI style, and, via Goguen-Burstall institutions [04 §16], a disciplined way to lay a deontic, an epistemic and a counts-as logic over the same signature and move between them. Meets R1, R7 (ARBAC's can_assign is exactly a role-guarded operator), R6 in part (group as a sort), R2 as a signature with the semantics left open. Costs: the equations must be supplied, and none exist (R3 stays open); universal algebra wants total operations while social acts are partial and guarded, so either partial algebras or a transition system over models of the signature; dynamics then live outside the algebra (R10); group identity through membership change needs an explicit answer (Ritchie's structure-plus-occupants, [14 §6d]). Lives there: 14 §5, §6, §7; 04 §12 MOISE+/OperA; 04 §1 ADICO; 12 §B1 Versu's exclusion logic as a concrete state language.

**3. A category of acts: state transformers, or a Kleisli category, with DEL action models as precedent.** Gives acts as morphisms on a state object, sequential composition and identity for free, laws as commuting diagrams (what "thank after give" equals is a stated equation or a stated non-equation), and a monad to choose the kind of dynamics: powerset for nondeterminism, distributions for probability (R10 in part), writer for history. DEL shows that acts on a *group* informational state compose and that the composite is computable [04 §10]; ACT's event map [03 §2], the interdependence transformations [06 §9], CiF's pipeline [12 §B2], strategies as operators on histories [09 §A3] and Lefebvre's substitution of images [03 §3] are all endomaps already. Meets R2, R3 (associativity and identity free; inverses and commutation proved case by case), R4, R8 (parameters as morphism data). Costs: nothing beyond associativity is free, so every commutation law is a theorem to prove; sorts (R1) sit inside the state object, not in the category, unless a second structure is added (a monoidal product for groups side by side, a functor from a category of sorts); performer and target are data on the morphism, not part of the type, so R7 must be enforced by partiality or by a guard monad; nesting (R6) is not native.

**4. An operad or wiring-diagram composition, with decorated cospans as the ambient category.** Gives group formation as composition: a group is the composite of its members with its own external interface, nesting and multi-level structure come free from operad composition (R6, R9), the hypergraph-category operations copy/merge/discard match join, split and leave, the black-box functor is "what a group presents outside", and an algebra over the operad is a candidate home for a Price-style level decomposition [09 §B2]. Meets R6, R9, R1 in part (port types as roles). Costs: wiring is static and typed, so any act that changes a relationship rewires the diagram and needs a rewriting or dynamic extension (R5, R10); there is no act vocabulary at all (R2); nobody has applied it to a human organisation, the nearest being network-model operads for engineered teams [14 §3c]; the reader's entry cost is high. Lives there: 14 §3; compositional game theory is named in 14's missing-work list only, so it is a lead, not a surveyed result.

**5. A process calculus, including bigraphs.** Gives agents as processes with names and channels, the bigraph's place graph as nesting (groups) and link graph as relationships, reaction rules as acts that change both at once, structural congruence as an equational theory, and executable semantics with behavioural equivalence. Meets R2 (reactions have a performer by construction only if encoded), R5, R6, R10 (reaction semantics), and, through the Actor model [14 §8a], a minimal floor of send/create/become. Costs: no role sort (controls approximate it), no norms, no internal state beyond the process term (R4, R7 by encoding), the equational laws concern structural rearrangement, not social meaning (R3 only syntactically); no published bigraphical model of a human group was found, and the absence was not confirmed [04 §13, Unconfirmed]; stochastic variants exist in the calculus literature but were not surveyed. Lives there: 04 §13; 14 §8a.

**6. A guarded, possibly stochastic, transition system on a sorted relational state.** Supported by the survey rather than requested: SAOM/REM [10 §23-24], CiF [12 §B2], ANSI RBAC [14 §5b] and the pairwise stochastic operators of [05 §7-8] all have this shape. Gives rates and scheduling (R10), guards (R7), typed state (R4), estimation from panel or event data (R11), and the one thing the formal homes above lack: a proven route from notation to fitted model. Meets most of R1-R11 pragmatically. Costs: it is a model class, not an algebra. There is no composition law of moves, no identity or inverse, no invariants beyond stationarity, and the meaning of a move is whatever its covariates say; the state space is combinatorial. Lives there: 10 §22-25, 12 §B1-B2, 14 §5b, 09 §C4.

Two layers the survey suggests belong under any of the above, not instead of them: a **valence layer** (Z/2 balance [03 §4] or Lefebvre's Boolean polynomial [03 §3]) as a constraint on relation states, and a **level map** (Hinde [09 §E1]; Price [09 §B2]) as the correctness test for aggregation.

## Not covered, and why

Named by the coordinator as absent from the fourteen files. One line each on why their absence is tolerable at the operator-and-operand stage; these are the synthesis's judgements, not survey findings, and any of them can be reopened.

- **Bourdieu** (field, habitus, capital): a topological metaphor with no operators; the one operator-shaped idea, conversion between capitals, is a transfer like Parsons's media [11 §6] and Coleman's control [05 §21].
- **Latour** (actor-network theory): refuses formalisation on principle and makes non-humans actants; translation and enrolment are named moves without effects, like the catalogues in [11] and [13].
- **Giddens** (structuration): duality of structure is a verbal claim about levels, which [09 §E1] and R9 already state in a usable form.
- **Garfinkel** (ethnomethodology): anti-formal by design; its one formal descendant, conversation analysis, is covered [06 §13].
- **Hannan-Freeman** (organisational ecology): birth and death rates of organisations in a population, aggregate and density-dependent; the shape is [05] and [09 §F1], with no within-group operator.
- **Putnam, Lin** (social capital): a stock measured on ties, not an operator; the structural content is Burt's.
- **Leskovec** (large-scale network measurement, cascades): measurement and prediction at scale; the formal content overlaps influence maximisation [05 §15] and adds no sorts or laws.
- **Burt** (structural holes): brokerage as a position measure; the act of brokering is covered via Boissevain [10 §18] and Padgett [08 §11], and [11 §1] records that Burt's reading was wrongly attributed to Simmel.

## Open questions for Aldred

Decisions only he can make; the survey does not settle them.

1. **Scope of operands.** Persons, roles, groups only, or also classes (elites, peasants, [08]) and institutions? Is Luhmann's alternative, events as operands and persons as environment [11 §10], in or out?
2. **What a role is.** A position in a pattern of ties [01], a normative bundle of rights and duties [04 §6; 11 §20; 14 §6a], a status with a role-set and role conflict [06 §role theory, not second-passed], an identity that switches between netdoms [01 §White-IC, not second-passed], or all of these with maps between them? May roles play roles [14 §6c]?
3. **Atomic configuration.** Dyad, or triad as Bowen and Simmel argue [13 §1; 11 §1]?
4. **Quantities and affect.** Are scalar states (EPA [03 §2], opinion [05], emotional energy [06 §15], a dependence value [03 §8]) in scope, or only discrete and relational state? The choice fixes which of the six homes is even possible.
5. **Dynamics.** Deterministic, nondeterministic, or probabilistic with rates [10 §23-24]? Discrete turns or continuous time? Is update order a modelling choice or part of the algebra [09 §C4; 06 §16]?
6. **The target's reading.** Is the other's model of me (Mead [11 §9], Lefebvre's nested images [03 §3], I-POMDP [12 §C6]) part of operand state, or an operator parameter?
7. **Which acts.** The clean, small set (speech acts and commitments [12 §C3-C4; 04 §5]) or the full catalogue (about 40 in [11], about 20 primitives in [13], plus [06] and [08])? Related: are rules-about-rules (changing P in [10 §10]; nested ADICO [04 §2]) first-class?
8. **Observability.** Hidden roles and private state [12 §C7; 13 §14]: does the algebra model what an observer can see, or the full state?
9. **Fit first or write first.** Derive operators from data as the network-statistics and KAES lines do [10 §23; 02 §9], or write the algebra and test it by reachability claims [07 "working implication"]?
10. **Whether to choose a home now.** `CLAUDE.md` says to decide the form of the first model from the notes, not up front; the six homes above are not mutually exclusive at the operator-table stage. Which requirements in (c) are load-bearing enough to rule homes out already?
