## Claim 1. A group's drive state is a distribution, and the merge law is a sandwich

**1.1 Definition (group drive state).** For the crowd law (v0.1 §4.4; Granovetter's map, L1831 [read]) the state of a group of n members is not a number but the empirical distribution of its members' thresholds, F(r) = #{i : θ_i ≤ r}/n, or equivalently the **excess function** E_F(r) = F(r) − r. Its equilibrium is τ(F) = lfp(F), the share that eventually acts (v0.1 Theorem 6.3). **Merge** of (n_1, F_1) and (n_2, F_2) is the mixture F_m = λF_1 + (1 − λ)F_2, λ = n_1/(n_1 + n_2): thresholds are kept as fractions of the crowd, so a merge dilutes. **Split** by any rule on members gives the conditional distributions. Merge is linear in E_F; the equilibrium is E_F's first non-positive point.

*Why this and not a mean.* v0.1 §2.3 already showed (Granovetter's pair, L1225 [read]) that the equilibrium is not determined by mean and variance. Proposition 1.6 below sharpens this to: no summary of the parts' equilibria and sizes determines the merged equilibrium at all.

**Fact 0.** τ(F) = min P(F), where P(F) = {r ∈ [0,1] : F(r) ≤ r} is the pre-fixed set (1 ∈ P(F) always). *Proof.* The orbit from 0 converges to a fixed point r* (v0.1 6.3(i)); if r ∈ P(F) then by induction every orbit point is ≤ r, so r* ≤ r. ∎ [derived, shown]

**1.2 Theorem (merge sandwich).** For any two crowds and any λ ∈ (0,1),

  min(τ_1, τ_2) = τ(min(F_1, F_2)) ≤ τ(F_m) ≤ τ(max(F_1, F_2)) = min(P(F_1) ∩ P(F_2)),

with the lower end attained **iff τ_1 = τ_2** (and then τ(F_m) = τ_1 = τ_2); when τ_1 < τ_2 the inequality τ_1 < τ(F_m) is strict for every λ ∈ (0,1), with τ(F_m) → τ_1 as λ → 1. The upper end is attained by the example in 1.4.

*Proof.* If λE_1(r) + (1−λ)E_2(r) ≤ 0 then E_1(r) ≤ 0 or E_2(r) ≤ 0, so P(F_m) ⊆ P(F_1) ∪ P(F_2); if both are ≤ 0 so is the combination, so P(F_1) ∩ P(F_2) ⊆ P(F_m). Take minima with Fact 0. The ends: P(min(F_1,F_2)) = P(F_1) ∪ P(F_2) and P(max(F_1,F_2)) = P(F_1) ∩ P(F_2). Strictness (next-pattern's audit): τ_1 is a fixed point, so E_m(τ_1) = (1−λ)E_2(τ_1), and τ_1 ∈ P(F_m) iff τ_1 ∈ P(F_2); if τ_1 < τ_2 = min P(F_2) then τ_1 ∉ P(F_m), and with τ(F_m) ≥ min(τ_1, τ_2) = τ_1 this gives τ(F_m) > τ_1. If τ_1 = τ_2 = t then t ∈ P(F_1) ∩ P(F_2) ⊆ P(F_m), so τ(F_m) = t. ∎ [derived, shown; audited by next-pattern 2026-11-01, strictness supplied by the audit; checked on 20,000 unequal and 30,664 equal pairs in exact arithmetic]

*Reading.* **A merge is strictly more active than its less active part unless the parts are equally active, and then it equals them**; it can rise above both, but never beyond the smallest share at which *both* parts would stop on their own.

**1.3 Theorem (monotonicity and convexity).** (a) F ≤ G pointwise ⇒ τ(F) ≤ τ(G); hence F_1 ≤ F_2 ⇒ τ_1 ≤ τ(F_m) ≤ τ_2. (b) For every c, {F : τ(F) ≥ c} is convex, because τ(F) ≥ c iff F(r) > r for all r < c, a family of strict linear inequalities; so any mixture of any number of crowds has τ at least the least of the parts', two full cascades merge to a full cascade, and λ ↦ τ(F_λ) is quasi-concave (every superlevel set in λ is an interval; not necessarily a single strict peak). (c) τ(F) = 0 iff F(0) = 0: two dormant crowds merge dormant; a dormant crowd merged with any live one is live. *Proofs* as stated. [derived, shown]

**1.4 Theorem (ignition).** τ(F_m) > max(τ_1, τ_2) is possible. Smallest example: a crowd of one with threshold 1/6 (dormant, τ_1 = 0) merged with a crowd of two with thresholds {0, 2/3} (τ_2 = 1/2) gives F_m(0) = 1/3, F_m(1/3) = 2/3, F_m(2/3) = 1: τ(F_m) = 1. **Necessary** condition, with τ_1 ≤ τ_2 = M > 0 (the "host" is crowd 2, the "intruder" crowd 1): **F_1(M) > M and F_1(M^−) ≥ M** — latent pressure at the host's stall point *and* at its left limit. *Proof.* If F_1(M) ≤ M then E_m(M) = λE_1(M) ≤ 0 and M ∈ P(F_m). For the left limit: F_2 cannot jump at M (a jump would put E_2 < 0 just below M, contradicting M = min P(F_2)), so F_2 ≡ M on some [r_k, M] and there E_m(r) = λE_1(r) + (1−λ)(M − r) → λE_1(M^−) as r → M^−; if E_1(M^−) < 0 a pre-fixed point sits just below M for every λ. ∎ *In words:* the intruder's members with thresholds strictly below the host's stall share must already make up at least that share of the intruder, and those at or below it must exceed it. The set of weights that ignite is an interval (0, λ*) (`sim/results/mixture_equilibria.md` M7(ii)). **Sufficient** condition: let B = {r ∈ [0, M] : E_1(r) ≤ 0} (where the intruder has no pressure) and ε = inf_B E_2 (inf ∅ = +∞); if ε > 0 then every λ < ε/(1+ε) ignites. *Proof.* On B, E_m ≥ −λ + (1−λ)ε > 0 (E_1 ≥ −1 always); off B within [0, M], E_1 > 0 and E_2 ≥ 0, so E_m > 0; hence no pre-fixed point in [0, M] and τ(F_m) > M. ∎ In the smallest example B = [0, 1/6) and ε = 1/3, so every λ < 1/4 ignites, and λ = 1/3 does by the interval. (A first version stated sufficiency with the margin inf_{r<M} E_2(r), which is zero for every crowd, so it was vacuous; corrected after next-pattern's audit, which also supplied the left-limit condition.) [derived, shown; audited 2026-11-01; corrected condition checked on 1,889 pairs, the necessity on 5,752 igniting pairs]

*Reading.* Ignition needs neither a dormant part nor a large one; two crowds that each recruit nobody beyond their instigators can ignite together (1,428 such pairs found [computed]). It is the formal content of "a group is more than its members" for this law.

**1.5 Proposition (what fails).** τ(F_m) ≤ max(τ_1, τ_2) is false (1.4); τ(F_m) ≥ λτ_1 + (1−λ)τ_2 and ≤ are both false (31.8% and 26.6% of 255,568 random pairs where they can differ, [computed]); the average bound from below holds when neither part recruits beyond its instigators (M10, [derived, shown]).

**1.6 Proposition (non-reducibility).** τ(F_m) is not a function of (τ_1, τ_2, n_1, n_2): two pairs with identical (τ_1, τ_2, n_1, n_2) have merged equilibria 1 and 1/3 (`sim/results/mixture_equilibria.md` §5). *Consequence:* a group-level drive state for this law must carry the excess function E_F (equivalently the threshold distribution), not any summary of it. This is the definitional claim of this file.

**1.7 Corollaries for split.** min(τ_A, τ_B) ≤ τ(whole); no split makes both parts strictly more active than the whole; cutting by threshold gives τ_B ≤ τ ≤ τ_A. [derived, shown] By contrast the escalation certificate is hereditary under split (models/01 2.1) and never improves under merge (ρ(Γ_m) ≥ max ρ_i): the two kinds of group state compose in opposite directions, which is why one law cannot serve both.

**1.8 Numerics [computed].** Exact rational arithmetic on a 10⁷ grid, 829,756 random merge pairs (n from 10 to 10,000; uniform, beta, bimodal, clipped-normal, Granovetter families) plus exhaustive small cases and an independent pure-Python re-implementation: zero violations of 1.2–1.7; ignition in 14.1% of random pairs where it is possible; in 27.8% of fair-coin splits of cascading wholes some part is strictly more active than the whole; fair-coin splits kill about half of cascades. Script `sim/explore/mixture_equilibria.py`; results `sim/results/mixture_equilibria.md`.

**1.9 Conjecture.** For two crowds drawn i.i.d. from one population whose first crossing of the diagonal is transversal, P(ignition) → 0 as sizes grow, at about n^{−1/2}. [conjecture; linearisation heuristic in the results file]

**1.10 Neighbourhood (so the claim is not derivative by omission).** τ(F) = min{r : F(r) ≤ r} is Granovetter 1978's own equilibrium (the first crossing of the diagonal; L1831), and Fact 0 is its Knaster–Tarski reading. 1.3(a), the least equilibrium monotone in the map, is an instance of monotone comparative statics of least equilibria (Topkis; Milgrom & Roberts 1990 Econometrica, doi 10.2307/2938316, for games with strategic complementarities) and should be named as such, not claimed. Granovetter's two-crowd example is the standard demonstration that the mean does not decide, reused by Macy & Evtushenko 2020 (Sociological Science 7, doi 10.15195/v7.a26), who also study instigator fractions; neither merges crowds. Granovetter & Soong 1983 (J. Math. Sociol. 9, doi 10.1080/0022250x.1983.9989941) and 1988 treat two populations whose thresholds are defined on the *other* group's participation, not a mixture: the nearest named construction, to be read before any novelty is claimed in print. Ignition has a sociological name, **critical mass** (Oliver, Marwell & Teixeira 1985, AJS 91, doi 10.1086/228313; Marwell & Oliver 1993), where heterogeneity and pooled early contributors let a group reach what none of its parts would; their model is a production function, not a threshold mixture, so 1.4 is the threshold-model formalisation of critical mass, and the left-limit condition is the part no one has stated. The convex-combination argument itself is elementary, which is a strength. Four searches by next-pattern found no prior statement of the sandwich, its strictness, the convexity of {F : τ(F) ≥ c}, or the ignition criterion. *Ours, on that evidence:* the definitional claim (state = excess function; non-reducibility) and the sandwich with strictness; *ours as a formalisation inside a different model:* the ignition criterion. DOIs resolved at Crossref 2026-11-01.

**1.11 Validity domain and test.** The law is v0.1's crowd law with its two-sided gate switched off (monotone F); era-free as a theorem; its empirical content is whatever F is. *Refutation test:* a merger of two mobilised groups whose combined action exceeds the upper end of the sandwich (the least share at which both would have stopped alone) refutes the model's merge law as a description of that merger. *Data candidates:* merged protest coalitions with per-group participation (089's event data cannot do it; a per-member panel can); online community mergers with per-member activity.
