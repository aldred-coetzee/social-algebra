# A modelled population to test the equations on

_2026-10-29_

Aldred: *"The idea with the model was that we had kind of like a modelled
human set that we could test our equations on… like a population group,
something that models the social group we want to test against."*

What this is: a simulated population (synthetic population, artificial
society, digital twin of a group) on which candidate equations are run,
perturbed and checked.

**The constraint that makes it useful: no circularity.** If the simulated
people run on our own equations, testing those equations on them proves
nothing. The population's behaviour must come from somewhere independent of
the equations under test:

1. **Fitted to real data**: each simulated person's parameters come from
   measured humans (experience sampling, couples, economic games).
2. **Built on a different theory**: e.g. PSI- or Zurich-model agents, or
   LLM agents grounded in interviews; our equations must recover their
   behaviour without being told how they work.
3. **Known ground truth for identifiability**: can our equations be told
   apart from rivals given realistic amounts of noisy data?

Order: research → draft equations → test on an independently built
population → test on real data. The testbed is designed from evidence:
directions on synthetic populations, the validity record of artificial
societies, LLM-agent populations and their failure modes, digital twins of
groups, and calibration datasets go on the research map.
