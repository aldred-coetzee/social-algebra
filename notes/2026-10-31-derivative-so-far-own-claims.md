# "This all seems completely derivative"

_2026-10-31_

Aldred: *"This all seems completely derivative. I'm hoping that once we've
considered the past, we can also integrate the current and become less
derivative."*

He is right. The survey was derivative by design; v0.1 is a patchwork of
other people's equations; `models/01`'s composition theorems are small
corollaries of linear algebra. Nothing in the repo is yet ours.

Where original work is expected to come from (the session's bet, in
order), each to be stated as OUR claim with a test on CURRENT data, kept in
`models/02-our-claims.md` strictly apart from the inherited equations:

1. **Group drive state as a distribution**, not a mean: merge = mixture,
   split = conditioning; "a group is more than its members" as a theorem
   about which outcomes are non-additive under mixture (tipping is;
   certificates are not). Open question started today: bounds on a merged
   crowd's equilibrium from its parts' equilibria.
2. **The medium as an operator** on the gain matrix (a feed's ranking
   rule rewrites who hears whom); the certificate as a design constraint
   on recommender systems; Bail 2018's sign flip modelled rather than
   observed.
3. **Non-human members**: from the exact newcomer law, a broadcast-only
   agent cannot create an escalation loop; a reactive agent (an LLM in a
   group chat) can, with safe loop gain scaling with the group's slack.
4. **Levers that survive being known**: consent-based anchoring levers
   stay effective under reflexivity; gate-manipulating levers decay as
   they become known — a formal reason positive steering beats
   manipulation, if it holds.
5. **Our own sign law**, written after batch 4's reads, tested on
   explicit-sign data from this century (Epinions, Slashdot, Wikipedia
   elections, Bitcoin trust, Reddit cross-community links, GitHub forks),
   with the Newcomb fraternity as a sanity check only.

Caution recorded with it: the novelty will be definitional and testable,
not deep mathematics; theorems stay modest.
