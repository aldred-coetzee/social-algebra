# Go on all; accelerated change and the datedness of theories

_2026-10-31_

Aldred, after the max-effort plan: *"Go on all, but also consider
accelerated change and datedness of some modern theories, so perhaps
consider a more fluid approach."*

**Go on all** = the five steps in order, reporting after each but not
stopping for a yes: (1) the n = 3 Newcomb test; (2) the sign-derivation
batch (where positive and negative ties come from — the hinge for the
drives hypothesis); (3) v0.2 with the certificate composition theorems,
the steering levers and, if the reads support one, a sign law; (4) the
team-scale dataset hunt; (5) only then the modelled population.

**Fluidity**, as taken here:

- Social reality has changed faster than the evidence base. The sources
  v0.1 leans on are a 1950s fraternity, 1990s couples, a 1970s karate
  club. Interaction is now largely mediated, algorithmically sorted, and
  about to include non-human members. Findings also decay on their own
  (the decline effect; cohort shifts) and because publication changes
  behaviour (reflexivity, file 093).
- So the equation set is built **modular and versioned**: every adopted
  law carries a validity domain (era of the data, medium, population,
  size range); theorems about *form* (the certificate, the cascade) are
  era-invariant given their hypotheses, while *magnitudes* (gates,
  couplings, the sign-generators) are era-bound and must be re-estimated;
  the ledger gains `era` and `medium` fields; v0.2 carries a validity
  column; the testbed can swap a law without rewriting the rest.
- A **datedness and drift batch** runs alongside the sign batch: temporal
  drift of findings, what digital mediation changes, non-human members,
  and an audit of every load-bearing empirical line in v0.1 for era,
  medium and whether it was re-tested after 2015.
- Contemporary explicit-relation data (project forks, community schisms)
  are preferred for the team-scale test for the same reason.
