# Not refuted: measured drives or a known world (2026-11-01)

After the fitted case failed its criterion, Aldred: *"I still think there's
something in trying to find a set of equations that can be used to model
human behavior. Are we saying that what we've done so far categorically
proves that this is not possible?"* Then: *"Ok go but be mindful of tokens."*

**Answer recorded.** No. Every test so far ran on public traces in which no
drive was measured (commits, vote signs, ratings, message counts). A null
there says drives are not recoverable from those proxies; it does not say
they do not exist or do not compose. The honest lesson of the battery is
that observational traces alone will not identify drives, so the programme
needs either measured drives or a world in which they are known.

**Two directions that could work, in order.**

1. **The modelled population as an identifiability experiment** (Aldred's
   day-one idea, never built properly). A synthetic world with explicit
   per-person drives generating behaviour by a mechanism that is *not* v0's
   equations (no-circularity rule, `notes/2026-10-29-modelled-population-testbed.md`).
   Then: can the drives be recovered from the observables alone, and does
   a drive-based model predict held-out group outcomes better than the
   person-effects rival that won the battery? If recovery fails when the
   drives are known to exist, that explains the battery and says what must
   be measured. If it succeeds, it is the inference method for real data.
   Three group sizes, as Aldred asked on 2026-10-30.
2. **A public experiment with measured drives.** Behavioural economics
   fits per-person parameters (risk and loss aversion, impatience,
   inequity aversion, social value orientation) and predicts group play
   from them; the group-size dependence of public-goods games is well
   studied. The test of *composition*, which is the part that is ours:
   does a composition rule of ours predict the group result better than
   the field's own aggregation. Dataset and DOIs to be found and checked
   at Crossref before anything is cited; nothing is named here from memory.

**A reframing kept open.** The battery's "person effect, not a drive"
(stable per-person propensities carrying most sign variance) can be read
as the first drive actually measured: a latent per-person disposition
estimated from many interactions. The identifiability experiment should
report whether such propensities are what its inference recovers.

**Token discipline.** One Sonnet agent per experiment with a written spec
and criteria fixed before any run; this session checks the numbers itself;
an Opus review only if a result is surprising. About 1 to 2M tokens for
(1).
