## 0. Answers in brief

1. **Is lfp(F_m) always between the parts' lfps? No: the lower half is a theorem, the upper half is false.** tau(F_m) >= min(tau_1, tau_2) held in all 829,756 pairs and has a one-line proof (M1). tau(F_m) > max(tau_1, tau_2) ("ignition") occurred in 26,515 of 187,427 random pairs where it is possible (14.1%; pairs with a full crowd or two dormant crowds cannot ignite) and in 17,224 of 469,756 exhaustive small pairs. The smallest case has three members: {1/6} merged with {0, 2/3} (section 5). **A live crowd is never dampened below the min** (M1), though it can be dampened far below its own value and below the lambda-average.
2. **Who ignites.** Dormancy is not required. Ignition occurs in every category pair where the max is below 1 and the pair is not two dormant crowds (table B), including two crowds that each have instigators and recruit nobody (I x I: 1,428). The exact condition: ignition above max requires **latent pressure**, F_low(tau_high) > tau_high (M7; 0 exceptions in 829,756 pairs), and the set of intruder weights that ignite is an interval (0, lambda*) (M7).
3. **Averages (Q2): neither bound holds.** In the 255,568 random pairs where the two can differ, tau_m < lambda-average in 31.8% (so "tau_m >= average" is violated that often) and tau_m > lambda-average in 26.6% (so "tau_m <= average" is violated that often). Dilution and discrete gaps push below (Granovetter-type crowds almost always: table A) but never when neither part recruits, where pooled instigators give tau_m >= average (M10); ignition and instigator pooling push above.
4. **Statements that hold in every draw (Q3), all with short proofs (section 3):** (a) both tau = 1 gives tau_m = 1, as the special case c = 1 of "{F : tau(F) >= c} is convex" (M4); (b) F_1 <= F_2 pointwise gives tau_1 <= tau_2, and then tau_1 <= tau_m <= tau_2 (M3); (c) the sandwich is exact, tau(min(F_1,F_2)) = min(tau_1,tau_2) <= tau_m <= tau(max(F_1,F_2)) = the least common pre-fixed point (M2); (d) as stated, "two crowds with lfp = 0 merge to lfp > 0" is **impossible** (lfp = 0 means F(0) = 0, and F_m(0) is the average of the F_i(0)); the true version is exact and short: two crowds that recruit nobody merge into one that does iff the merged instigator share reaches the smallest positive threshold (M6); (e) as stated, "lfp = 1 whole, two conditional parts with lfp = 0" is **impossible** for the same reason; the true version (whole cascades, both parts strictly below it) occurs in 49.6% of fair-coin splits of cascading wholes, mostly when the whole has one instigator, never when the parts are cut by threshold (M8).
5. **Certificate contrast (Q4).** rho(Gamma_B) <= rho(Gamma) for every block in 76,584 random splits (violations: 0), so the certificate is hereditary under split. The equilibrium is not: it only satisfies tau(whole) >= min(tau_parts), and in 27.8% of fair-coin splits of cascading wholes some part is strictly more active than the whole.
6. **Structure.** The merge is *linear* in the excess function E_F(r) = F(r) - r, and the equilibrium is its first non-positive point. So no function of the parts' equilibria and sizes can give the merged equilibrium (proposition N: two pairs with identical (tau_1, tau_2, n_1, n_2) have merged equilibria 1 and 1/3). A group-level drive state must carry the excess function, not a summary of it.
7. **Open (best conjecture):** for crowds drawn from one population whose first crossing is transversal, P(ignition) -> 0 as the sizes grow, at about the n^(-1/2) rate a linearisation suggests (section 4.8). Not proved.

## 1. Setup

A crowd of n members has thresholds theta_i in [0,1]; F(r) = #{i : theta_i <= r}/n; dynamics r(0) = F(0), r(t+1) = F(r(t)); equilibrium tau(F) = lfp(F) (v0.1 Thm 6.3). A threshold of 0 is an **instigator**; a threshold of 1 is a member who joins only when everyone else has (a "never"). Write P(F) = {r in [0,1] : F(r) <= r} (the **pre-fixed set**; 1 is always in it) and E_F(r) = F(r) - r (the **excess**).

- **Merge** of (n_1, F_1) and (n_2, F_2): F_m = lam F_1 + (1 - lam) F_2 with lam = n_1/(n_1 + n_2), i.e. the union of the two threshold lists with thresholds kept as *fractions of the crowd* (so a merge dilutes: a member who needed 10% of 100 people now needs 10% of 200). **Split** by a rule on members: the two conditional CDFs, same thresholds, new denominators. F is the mixture of its parts, so *a split is a merge read backwards*.
- **Exactness.** Thresholds are integers over a grid of D = 10^7 (theta = a/D), so "theta <= k/N" is the integer test a*N <= k*D, equilibria are `Fraction`s, and no floating-point comparison decides anything reported here. The equilibrium is computed as the smallest pre-fixed point (vectorised) and cross-checked against the literal orbit r(t+1) = F(r(t)) and against exact orbits of the min, max and mixture maps in 14,400 sampled draws with N <= 600: **0 disagreements**. Separately, an independent pure-python reimplementation (no numpy, integer arithmetic over a grid of 1/100) agrees with the main path on every field of the pair analysis (equilibria, upper bound, latent pressure, first-step test, categories) in 40,000 random small pairs: **0 disagreements**.
- **Draws.** Each random pair has two independent crowds with n_i log-uniform in [10, 10,000] (so N = n_1 + n_2 runs from 20 to 20,000); the Granovetter family uses n in {10, ..., 10,000} on the integer grid. Families: `uniform` (U[a,b], a in [0,.7]); `beta` (shapes log-uniform in [.3,8]); `bimodal` (two betas, modes in [.03,.35] and [.45,.95]); `normal` (Granovetter's clipped normal, mean .1 to .5, sd .02 to .4); `atoms` (1 to 4 point masses); `gran` (Granovetter's uniform crowd {0,1,...,n-1}/n, his perturbed crowd with the member at 1 moved to 2, and 1 to 3 random one- or two-step moves of that crowd); `full` (built so that tau = 1: the k-th member's threshold is at most k/n); `mixed` (families chosen at random per crowd); `sameG` (both crowds from the same distribution). Except `normal`, `gran` and `full`, each member is an instigator with probability z (0 with probability .25, else log-uniform in [.003,.3]) and a "never" with probability w (0 or U[0,.4]). **These rates describe my sampling design, not the world** (a quarter of crowds are dormant by construction); the tables show what exists and how it is structured, not how common it is.
- **Categories** of a crowd: D dormant (no instigators, tau = 0); I inert (instigators exist, tau = F(0): they recruit nobody); P partial cascade (F(0) < tau < 1); F full (tau = 1).

## 3. Proofs (short; the first one carries the rest)

**Fact 0.** tau(F) = min P(F). *Proof.* Run the orbit one step earlier, from 0: rho_0 = 0, rho_1 = F(0) (the problem's r(0)), rho_{t+1} = F(rho_t). Its limit r* satisfies F(r*) = r* (v0.1 Thm 6.3(i)), so r* is in P(F). If r is in P(F), then rho_0 = 0 <= r, and rho_t <= r implies rho_{t+1} = F(rho_t) <= F(r) <= r, so r* <= r. (This is the Knaster-Tarski reading of Thm 6.3.) Because F_lam is affine in lam, **E_{F_lam} = lam E_1 + (1 - lam) E_2**, and r is in P(F_lam) iff that combination is <= 0.

**M1 and M2.** If lam E_1(r) + (1-lam) E_2(r) <= 0 then E_1(r) <= 0 or E_2(r) <= 0, so P(F_lam) is contained in P_1 union P_2; if both are <= 0 so is the combination, so P_1 intersect P_2 is contained in P(F_lam). Take minima: min(tau_1, tau_2) <= tau_m <= min(P_1 intersect P_2). Since P(min(F_1,F_2)) = P_1 union P_2 and P(max(F_1,F_2)) = P_1 intersect P_2, the two bounds are exactly tau(min(F_1,F_2)) and tau(max(F_1,F_2)): **the sandwich in (c) is an identity, and neither end can be improved from the parts' equilibria alone** (the lower end can be approached, to within about one grid step, when F_2 hugs the diagonal from above up to tau_2 and F_1 drops far below it after tau_1). In terms of (tau_1, tau_2) alone nothing better than min <= tau_m <= 1 is valid: for (tau_1, tau_2) = (0, 1/2) the merged value can be anything from 1/3 to 1 (proposition N and section 5).

**M3.** F <= G pointwise gives P(G) contained in P(F), hence tau(F) <= tau(G). If F_1 <= F_2 then F_1 <= F_lam <= F_2.

**M4.** tau(F) >= c iff F(r) > r for every r < c: a family of strict *linear* inequalities, so {F : tau(F) >= c} is convex. Hence for any mixture of any number of crowds tau >= the least of the parts; c = 1 gives (a); and {lam : tau(F_lam) >= c} is an interval for every c, i.e. lam -> tau(F_lam) is unimodal.

**M5.** tau(F) = 0 iff r(0) = F(0) = 0. F_lam(0) = lam F_1(0) + (1-lam) F_2(0) is zero iff both are (for 0 < lam < 1). So (d) as literally stated cannot happen, and a dormant crowd merged with any live crowd is live.

**M6.** Let m = F(0) and theta+ the smallest positive threshold. The orbit starts at m, so tau = m iff F(m) = m iff no member has theta in (0, m] iff theta+ > m. For the merge, m = lam m_1 + (1-lam) m_2 and theta+ = min(theta+_1, theta+_2). *Reading:* two crowds that each recruit nobody (theta+_i > m_i) merge into one that does exactly when the smaller least threshold, say theta+_1, satisfies theta+_1 <= lam m_1 + (1-lam) m_2; this needs m_2 > theta+_1 > m_1, i.e. the other crowd's instigators, diluted by the weights, reach the first rung of the ladder. Whether the cascade then runs on is not decided by the first step.

**M7.** Let tau_1 < tau_2 and let lam be the weight of F_1. (i) If F_1(tau_2) <= tau_2 then tau_2 is in P_1 intersect P_2, so tau_m <= tau_2 for **every** lam (M2): ignition needs F_low(tau_high) > tau_high. (ii) tau_m > tau_2 iff E_lam > 0 on [0, tau_2]. For 0 < lam' < lam, E_lam' = (lam'/lam) E_lam + (1 - lam'/lam) E_2 with E_2 >= 0 on [0, tau_2], so E_lam' > 0 too: **the ignition set is downward closed in the intruder's weight**, an interval (0, lam*). (iii) If moreover eps := inf over r < tau_2 of E_2(r) is > 0 (a margin; generic for continuous thresholds) and E_1(tau_2) > 0, then E_lam >= -lam + (1-lam) eps > 0 below tau_2 and E_lam(tau_2) = lam E_1(tau_2) > 0 at tau_2, so every lam < eps/(1+eps) ignites: a small enough group with latent pressure at a stalled crowd's stall point restarts it. Without a margin (iii) fails, e.g. F_2 = 1/2 on [0, 2/3) touches the diagonal from above at its own equilibrium 1/2: F4 and section 5.

**M8 (split).** F = lam_A F_A + lam_B F_B, so M1 to M7 apply with the parts in the roles of crowds. (i) min(tau_A, tau_B) <= tau(F): **no split makes both parts strictly more active than the whole.** (ii) Both parts strictly *less* active is exactly ignition read backwards, so a split can kill a cascade. (iii) tau(F) = 0 iff both parts are dormant, so the literal (e) is impossible. (iv) If A = {theta <= c} and B = {theta > c} then F_B <= F <= F_A, so tau_B <= tau <= tau_A (M3): cutting by threshold can never kill. (v) With a fair coin and z instigators, P(some part has none) = 2^(1-z), and a part with none is dormant: this is the dominant way random splits kill (table E).

**M10.** tau_m >= F_m(0) = lam F_1(0) + (1-lam) F_2(0). If tau_i = F_i(0) for both parts (neither recruits beyond its instigators) the right side is lam tau_1 + (1-lam) tau_2. So pooling instigators is at least additive; "tau_m >= average" can fail only when some part is a partial or full cascade (table B: the D x D, D x I and I x I rows have no pair below the average).

**N (non-compositionality).** Pair 1: {1/6} with {0, 2/3}. Pair 2: {1} with {0, 2/3}. Both have (n_1, n_2) = (1, 2), tau_1 = 0, tau_2 = 1/2; the merged equilibria are given in section 5 (1 and 1/3). In general tau_m depends on E_1, E_2 only through their values on [0, tau_m], which is why a *distribution* is needed as the group state.

### 4.7 Table F (Q4): the certificate, by contrast

|  | certificate rho(Gamma) | equilibrium tau(F) |
|---|---:|---:|
| whole vs parts under split | rho(Gamma_B) <= rho(Gamma): 76,584 of 76,584 blocks (0 violations) | parts both above whole: never; some part above: 27.8%; both below (kill): 49.6% |

| inheritance | hereditary: certified whole implies certified parts | not hereditary: a cascading whole can have only dormant or inert parts |

One line: the certificate is a *worst-case over interactions* quantity, so the whole dominates every part; the equilibrium is a *mixture* quantity, so the whole dominates only the least active part. That is the asymmetry between the two kinds of group state: the set "certified" (rho < 1) is closed under split and not under merge; the set "full cascade" (tau = 1, or tau >= c) is closed under merge (M4) and not under split.

## 5. Counterexamples and examples with their thresholds

Smallest cases found by exhaustive enumeration (all unordered pairs of crowds with 1 to 3 members and thresholds in {0, 1/12, ..., 1}, and with 1 to 5 members and thresholds in {0, 1/6, ..., 1}; the two sets overlap, so "exhaustive pairs" counts evaluations); "smallest" is by merged size.

**F1: tau_m > max (ignition), N = 3.**
- crowd 1 = {1/6}, crowd 2 = {0, 2/3}: tau_1 = 0, tau_2 = 1/2, merged {0, 1/6, 2/3}: **tau_m = 1**; lambda-average 1/3. Latent pressure: F_1(1/2) = 1 > 1/2.

## 6. What I would carry into `models/02`, and what I would not

**Carry (proved here, short, to be audited):**

1. *Merge sandwich.* P_1 intersect P_2 is contained in P(F_lam), which is contained in P_1 union P_2; so min(tau_1,tau_2) <= tau(F_lam) <= tau(max(F_1,F_2)) for every lam, and both ends are identities for the maps min(F_1,F_2) and max(F_1,F_2). Consequences: {tau >= c} is convex (tau is quasi-concave on CDFs, unimodal in lam); both-full merges are full; a merge is never below both parts; no split makes both parts strictly more active than the whole.
2. *Ignition.* Above the higher part needs F_low(tau_high) > tau_high; the weights that ignite form an interval (0, lam*); with a margin, latent pressure suffices for small weights; the first step is exact (merged instigator share >= least positive threshold).
3. *Non-compositionality, and what composes.* tau does not compose (N); the excess E_F does, linearly under mixture, and tau is its first non-positive point. That is the precise sense in which a group's drive state is a distribution and not a mean or an equilibrium.
4. *Mean-type statements are false* (F1 to F3). Do not write "the merged equilibrium is between / above / below the average" anywhere.
5. *Asymmetry with the certificate.* Certificate: hereditary under split (models/01 Thm 2.1), merge >= max of parts. Equilibrium: merge >= min of parts, split not hereditary. This is the formal content of "a group is more than its members" for the cascade side.

**Conjecture (open, section 4.8).** C1: for crowds that are iid samples of a population G with a transversal first crossing (G(r) > r below tau_G, G'(tau_G) < 1, G(0) > 0), P(tau_m > max(tau_1, tau_2)) -> 0 as n_1, n_2 -> infinity with n_1/n_2 fixed, at rate about n^(-1/2).

**Not done, and where the next numerics should go.** (1) The two-sided gate of v0.1 (F = F_theta - F~, non-monotone): there is no least fixed point in general and none of this applies. (2) Partial mixing: the merge here has every member respond to the pooled share; a homophily weight h (respond to (1-h) own crowd + h pooled) interpolates between two separate crowds and the full merge, and is the natural Granovetter-style social-structure extension. (3) Merges of more than two crowds: M1 to M4 hold verbatim (convexity), M6 and M7 need restating. (4) Thresholds as counts rather than fractions (a merge that does not dilute) would change every result about dilution; v0.1 uses fractions, so this page does.
